Understanding PCA

PCA finds a new coordinate system for your data where the axes (called principal components) are ranked by how much variance they capture. The first axis points in the direction the data varies the most; each subsequent axis is perpendicular and captures as much remaining variance as possible.

💡 Core Insight

The principal axes are the eigenvectors of the covariance matrix, ordered by their eigenvalues. The largest eigenvalue equals the maximum projected variance; projecting and reconstructing back reveals the minimum reconstruction error you can achieve with that many components.

The explained-variance ratio for component \(k\) is \(\lambda_k / \sum_j \lambda_j\), telling you what fraction of total variance that component captures. Plotting these in order gives the scree plot — the "elbow" is where adding more components yields diminishing returns.

🔑 Dual Interpretation

Maximising projected variance and minimising reconstruction error are the same optimisation: both are solved by the top eigenvectors of \(\Sigma = \frac{1}{n}X^\top X\) (for centred data).

2
The Math — Two Equivalent Objectives

Setup. Let \(X \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}\) be the centred data matrix (\(\bar{x}=0\)). The sample covariance matrix is:

$$\Sigma = \frac{1}{n} X^\top X \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$$

Objective 1 — Variance Maximisation. Find unit vector \(w_1\) maximising variance of projections \(Xw_1\):

$$\max_{\|w\|=1}\; \operatorname{Var}(Xw) = \max_{\|w\|=1}\; w^\top \Sigma w$$

By the Rayleigh quotient theorem, the solution is the eigenvector of \(\Sigma\) with the largest eigenvalue \(\lambda_1\). The maximum variance equals \(\lambda_1\).

Objective 2 — Reconstruction Error Minimisation. For a rank-\(k\) projection \(\hat{X} = XW_kW_k^\top\) where \(W_k = [w_1,\ldots,w_k]\):

$$\min_{W_k \in \text{Stiefel}} \|X - XW_kW_k^\top\|_F^2$$

By the Eckart-Young theorem, the minimiser is the matrix of top-\(k\) eigenvectors of \(\Sigma\). Both objectives share the exact same solution.

📐 Key Equations

Eigendecomposition: \(\Sigma = V \Lambda V^\top\), columns of \(V\) are principal axes, \(\Lambda = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2 \ge \cdots \ge \lambda_d)\)

Explained-variance ratio for component \(k\): \(\quad r_k = \dfrac{\lambda_k}{\sum_{j=1}^d \lambda_j}\)

Cumulative explained variance with \(k\) components: \(\quad R_k = \sum_{j=1}^k r_j\)

Reconstruction MSE with \(k\) components: \(\quad \text{MSE}_k = \dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{j=k+1}^d \lambda_j\) (sum of discarded eigenvalues)

⚠️ Common Misconceptions

1. PCA assumes linear structure. It finds linear projections; non-linear structure (e.g., Swiss roll) requires kernel PCA or manifold methods.
2. Centring is mandatory, scaling is a choice. Always centre. Scale (standardise) when features have different units; skip scaling for genuinely comparable features.
3. Eigenvalues ≠ variance percentages. They are proportional to variance, but the ratio \(\lambda_k / \sum \lambda_j\) gives the fraction.

Numerical Trace — 2-D Example

Five centred points in \(\mathbb{R}^2\): \(X = \{(2,1),(3,2),(1,0),(-2,-1),(-4,-2)\}\).

$$\Sigma = \frac{1}{5}\begin{bmatrix}34&17\\17&9\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}6.8&3.4\\3.4&1.8\end{bmatrix}$$

Eigenvalues: \(\lambda_1 \approx 8.56,\; \lambda_2 \approx 0.04\). Eigenvector for \(\lambda_1\): \(w_1 \approx (0.894, 0.447)^\top\) (the diagonal direction — data lies almost on a line).

Explained-variance ratios: \(r_1 = 8.56/8.60 \approx 99.5\%,\; r_2 \approx 0.5\%\). Reconstruction MSE keeping 1 PC: \(\approx 0.04\) (nearly zero — data is nearly 1-D).

✅ When to Use PCA

Use PCA for: preprocessing before a linear model, removing multicollinearity, compressing features, denoising. Choose kernel PCA or autoencoders when the data lies on a non-linear manifold.
