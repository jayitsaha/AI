Support, Confidence, Lift & Conviction

Association Rule Metrics — the four numbers that tell you whether a rule is frequent, reliable, surprising, and directional.

Intermediate Classical ML Association Rules 4 Metrics
1
Understanding the Concept

What is this? When you mine a dataset of shopping baskets, transactions, or co-occurrences, you want to know: which items appear together, and does finding item X actually tell you something useful about item Y? Four metrics answer this:

  • Support — How often does this combination appear at all? (frequency)
  • Confidence — Given X, how often is Y also present? (conditional probability)
  • Lift — Is the X→Y link stronger than chance, or is Y popular regardless of X? (surprise factor)
  • Conviction — How badly does the rule break without Y? (directionality & dependence strength)
💡 Real-World Analogy

Imagine a coffee shop. Support = "20% of orders include both coffee and croissant." Confidence = "Of orders that include coffee, 60% also include a croissant." Lift = "Croissants sell in 30% of all orders — so coffee makes croissant 2× more likely, not just by coincidence." Conviction = "How often would coffee appear WITHOUT a croissant if they were independent vs. now?"

⚡ If you remember one thing

Support tells you how common; confidence tells you how reliable; lift tells you how surprising vs. random; conviction tells you how directional and strong. You need all four — support alone finds frequent but trivial rules, confidence alone misses base-rate effects, lift alone ignores directionality, conviction alone is hard to interpret without the others.

2
Formal Definitions & Math

Let \(\mathcal{T}\) be a set of \(N\) transactions. Each transaction \(t_i \subseteq \mathcal{I}\) is a subset of the item universe \(\mathcal{I}\). A rule has the form \(X \Rightarrow Y\) where \(X, Y \subseteq \mathcal{I}\) and \(X \cap Y = \emptyset\).

Support

The fraction of transactions containing both \(X\) and \(Y\):

$$\text{support}(X \Rightarrow Y) = \frac{|\{t \in \mathcal{T} : X \cup Y \subseteq t\}|}{N} = P(X \cup Y)$$

Support is symmetric: \(\text{supp}(X \Rightarrow Y) = \text{supp}(Y \Rightarrow X)\). It filters out rare itemsets early (Apriori anti-monotone property).

Confidence

The conditional probability \(P(Y \mid X)\) — given a transaction contains \(X\), how often does it also contain \(Y\)?

$$\text{confidence}(X \Rightarrow Y) = \frac{\text{support}(X \cup Y)}{\text{support}(X)} = P(Y \mid X) = \frac{P(X \cup Y)}{P(X)}$$

Confidence is not symmetric: \(\text{conf}(X \Rightarrow Y) \neq \text{conf}(Y \Rightarrow X)\) in general.

Lift

How much more often \(X\) and \(Y\) co-occur than expected under independence:

$$\text{lift}(X \Rightarrow Y) = \frac{\text{confidence}(X \Rightarrow Y)}{\text{support}(Y)} = \frac{P(X \cup Y)}{P(X)\,P(Y)}$$
  • \(\text{lift} > 1\) — positive association (X and Y co-occur more than chance)
  • \(\text{lift} = 1\) — statistical independence
  • \(\text{lift} < 1\) — negative association (X suppresses Y)

Lift is symmetric: \(\text{lift}(X \Rightarrow Y) = \text{lift}(Y \Rightarrow X)\).

Conviction

Measures the degree to which \(X\) occurs without \(Y\) more than expected if independent. Unlike lift, conviction is directional:

$$\text{conviction}(X \Rightarrow Y) = \frac{1 - \text{support}(Y)}{1 - \text{confidence}(X \Rightarrow Y)} = \frac{P(X)\,P(\lnot Y)}{P(X \cap \lnot Y)}$$
  • \(\text{conviction} = 1\) — independence (rule is no better than random)
  • \(\text{conviction} \to \infty\) — perfect rule (X never appears without Y)
  • \(\text{conviction} < 1\) — negative association
⚠️ Common Misconception

High confidence alone is misleading. If \(P(Y) = 0.95\) (Y is in 95% of baskets), any rule \(X \Rightarrow Y\) will have confidence ≈ 95% regardless of X. Lift corrects for this: lift = 95%/95% = 1.0 — the rule adds no information.

🎯 Decision Guide
  • Filter by support first (min-support threshold) — removes rare noise
  • Filter by confidence next — removes unreliable rules
  • Rank by lift — surfaces genuinely interesting associations over base-rate effects
  • Use conviction for direction — a high conviction rule with direction X→Y might not hold for Y→X
5
Key Takeaways
  • Support is the frequency gate — ensures rules have statistical basis.
  • Confidence = \(P(Y|X)\) — the precision of the rule as a prediction.
  • Lift > 1 = positive association, = 1 independence, < 1 suppression. Always check this before trusting confidence.
  • Conviction → ∞ signals a deterministic rule; conviction = 1 means no better than random baseline.
  • These metrics appear in Apriori, FP-Growth, and ECLAT algorithms, and are foundational to recommendation systems, market basket analysis, and medical co-occurrence mining.
