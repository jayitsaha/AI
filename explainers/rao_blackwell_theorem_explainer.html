Rao-Blackwell Theorem

Rao-Blackwellization: turning any unbiased estimator into a uniformly better one via conditioning on a sufficient statistic
Advanced Statistical Inference Probability & Statistics Variance Reduction
1
Understanding the Concept
▾

You have a rough estimate of some unknown quantity \(\theta\). The Rao-Blackwell theorem tells you: you can always improve that estimate — or at worst keep it the same — by averaging it over a smarter summary of your data. The improvement is in variance: the new estimate is just as unbiased but less noisy.

💡 The Key Insight

Conditioning any unbiased estimator \(\delta(X)\) on a sufficient statistic \(T(X)\) produces a new estimator \(\delta^*(X) = \mathbb{E}[\delta(X)\mid T(X)]\) that has equal or smaller variance. This process is called Rao-Blackwellization.

The Buffet Analogy

Imagine trying to guess a restaurant's average portion size by watching only one random diner. That's your naive estimator — unbiased but highly variable. Now imagine you're told the total amount of food served that evening (a sufficient summary). Averaging your guess conditioned on that total makes it far more stable, without any bias creeping in.

Why "Sufficient"?

A statistic \(T(X)\) is sufficient for \(\theta\) if it captures everything in the data about \(\theta\) — once you know \(T\), the remaining randomness in \(X\) contains no more information about \(\theta\). Conditioning on \(T\) averages out the "irrelevant noise" in \(\delta\).

Key Theorem Statement

If \(\delta(X)\) is an unbiased estimator of \(g(\theta)\) and \(T(X)\) is sufficient for \(\theta\), then \(\delta^* = \mathbb{E}[\delta(X)\mid T]\) satisfies:

① \(\mathbb{E}[\delta^*] = g(\theta)\) (still unbiased)   ② \(\operatorname{Var}(\delta^*) \leq \operatorname{Var}(\delta)\) (never worse)

How It Works Internally
▾

Step-by-Step: The Rao-Blackwellization Algorithm

Setup: You have data \(X = (X_1, \ldots, X_n)\) from a distribution with unknown \(\theta\). You have an unbiased estimator \(\delta(X)\) and a sufficient statistic \(T(X)\).

Worked Example — Uniform\((0,\theta)\)

Suppose \(X_1, \ldots, X_5 \stackrel{iid}{\sim} \text{Uniform}(0, \theta)\). We want to estimate \(\theta\).

The naive estimator: \(\delta_0 = 2X_1\). It's unbiased since \(\mathbb{E}[2X_1] = \theta\), but only uses one observation. Variance: \(\text{Var}(\delta_0) = \theta^2/3\).

The sufficient statistic: \(T = X_{(n)} = \max(X_1,\ldots,X_5)\). It is complete sufficient for \(\theta\).

Computing the Conditional Expectation

Given \(T = t\), exactly one of the \(n\) observations equals \(t\) (the maximum), and the remaining \(n-1\) are iid \(\text{Uniform}(0, t)\). Therefore:

$$\mathbb{E}[X_1 \mid X_{(n)} = t] = \frac{1}{n} \cdot t + \frac{n-1}{n} \cdot \frac{t}{2} = \frac{t(n+1)}{2n}$$

So the Rao-Blackwellized estimator is:

$$\delta^*(T) = \mathbb{E}[2X_1 \mid T] = 2 \cdot \frac{T(n+1)}{2n} = \frac{(n+1)}{n} \cdot T = \frac{(n+1)}{n} X_{(n)}$$

⚠️ Common Misconception

Rao-Blackwell does NOT require that \(T\) be a complete sufficient statistic. The theorem guarantees improvement (or equality) with any sufficient \(T\). Completeness is needed for the stronger Lehmann-Scheffé theorem (UMVUE).

Why It Works — The Proof
▾

Proof of Unbiasedness

By the tower property (law of iterated expectations):

$$\mathbb{E}_\theta[\delta^*(T)] = \mathbb{E}_\theta[\mathbb{E}[\delta(X) \mid T]] = \mathbb{E}_\theta[\delta(X)] = g(\theta)$$

So \(\delta^*\) is unbiased. The tower law is the key — it says the outer expectation of a conditional expectation collapses to the unconditional expectation.

Proof of Variance Reduction

Apply the variance decomposition formula (law of total variance):

$$\operatorname{Var}(\delta) = \underbrace{\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb{E}[\delta \mid T])}_{\operatorname{Var}(\delta^*)} + \underbrace{\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{Var}(\delta \mid T)]}_{\geq\, 0}$$

Since \(\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{Var}(\delta \mid T)] \geq 0\) always, we get:

$$\operatorname{Var}(\delta^*) = \operatorname{Var}(\delta) - \mathbb{E}[\operatorname{Var}(\delta \mid T)] \;\leq\; \operatorname{Var}(\delta)$$

Equality holds if and only if \(\operatorname{Var}(\delta \mid T) = 0\) a.s. — i.e., \(\delta\) is already a function of \(T\). In that case, Rao-Blackwellization returns \(\delta\) unchanged.

Formal Statement (Rao 1945, Blackwell 1947)

Let \(X\) have density/pmf \(f(x;\theta)\), \(\theta \in \Theta\). Let \(\delta(X)\) be an unbiased estimator of \(g(\theta)\) with \(\mathbb{E}_\theta[\delta^2] < \infty\), and let \(T(X)\) be sufficient for \(\theta\). Define \(\delta^*(t) = \mathbb{E}[\delta(X) \mid T(X)=t]\). Then:

(a) \(\delta^*(T)\) is a statistic (does not depend on \(\theta\) — critical! follows from sufficiency).

(b) \(\mathbb{E}_\theta[\delta^*(T)] = g(\theta)\) for all \(\theta\).

(c) \(\operatorname{Var}_\theta(\delta^*) \leq \operatorname{Var}_\theta(\delta)\) for all \(\theta\).

Why \(\delta^*(T)\) Is a Valid Statistic

This is the subtle point: \(\mathbb{E}[\delta(X) \mid T=t]\) must not depend on the unknown \(\theta\). It doesn't — because \(T\) is sufficient, the conditional distribution of \(X\) given \(T=t\) is the same for all \(\theta\). This is the definition of sufficiency: \(f(x\mid T=t; \theta)\) is free of \(\theta\).

The Geometric Intuition

In \(L^2\) space (square-integrable random variables), \(\delta^* = \mathbb{E}[\delta \mid T]\) is the orthogonal projection of \(\delta\) onto the subspace of \(T\)-measurable functions. Projection never increases the distance to any fixed point — including \(g(\theta)\). The squared distance is the MSE, so MSE (= variance when unbiased) cannot increase.

Connection to UMVUE (Lehmann-Scheffé)

Combine Rao-Blackwell with completeness: if \(T\) is complete sufficient, then by Lehmann-Scheffé, the Rao-Blackwellized estimator \(\delta^*(T)\) is the Uniformly Minimum Variance Unbiased Estimator (UMVUE) — the unique best unbiased estimator for all \(\theta\) simultaneously. In our example, \(\delta^* = \frac{n+1}{n}X_{(n)}\) is the UMVUE for \(\theta\) in Uniform\((0,\theta)\).

Applications: Particle Filters & RL
▾

Rao-Blackwellized Particle Filters

In sequential Monte Carlo (particle filters), the hidden state \(z_t\) can often be split: \(z_t = (z_t^{nl}, z_t^{l})\) where \(z_t^{l}\) is the linear-Gaussian part. Instead of sampling all components, condition on the nonlinear part and compute the linear part analytically via a Kalman filter. This is the Rao-Blackwellized Particle Filter (RBPF).

$$\delta^*(z_t^{nl}) = \mathbb{E}[\delta \mid z_t^{nl}] \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{Var}(\text{RBPF}) \leq \text{Var}(\text{standard PF})$$

Result: you can achieve the same accuracy with far fewer particles — typically \(10\times\)–\(100\times\) fewer. Widely used in simultaneous localisation and mapping (SLAM).

Variance Reduction in Reinforcement Learning

In policy gradient methods (REINFORCE, PPO), the gradient estimator is:

$$\nabla_\theta J = \mathbb{E}\bigl[G_t \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a_t | s_t)\bigr]$$

where \(G_t\) is the return. A baseline \(b(s_t)\) can be subtracted without bias (because \(\mathbb{E}[\nabla \log \pi \cdot b(s)] = 0\)). Choosing \(b = V^\pi(s_t)\) is precisely Rao-Blackwellization: you condition on the state \(s_t\) (sufficient for the value), replacing the noisy return \(G_t\) with the advantage \(A_t = G_t - V^\pi(s_t)\).

Rao-Blackwell in Modern ML
  • Variational inference: Rao-Blackwellizing the ELBO gradient by conditioning on discrete latent variables reduces gradient variance, enabling better training of VAEs with discrete latents.
  • Monte Carlo integration: Any MCMC estimator can be improved by conditioning on the sufficient statistics of known sub-models (collapsed Gibbs sampling).
  • Causal inference: Conditioning on confounders that are sufficient for the treatment assignment yields lower-variance propensity-score estimators.

Key Takeaways
▾
  • Rao-Blackwell theorem: conditioning an unbiased estimator on a sufficient statistic always reduces (or preserves) variance.
  • Proof engine: the law of total variance — \(\text{Var}(\delta) = \text{Var}(\delta^*) + \mathbb{E}[\text{Var}(\delta\mid T)]\), and the second term is \(\geq 0\).
  • Sufficiency is the key: \(\mathbb{E}[\delta\mid T]\) must not depend on \(\theta\); this is guaranteed iff \(T\) is sufficient.
  • UMVUE connection: add completeness → Lehmann-Scheffé → unique best unbiased estimator.
  • Beyond estimation: the same idea powers RBPF in robotics, advantage functions in RL, and collapsed inference in Bayesian models.
