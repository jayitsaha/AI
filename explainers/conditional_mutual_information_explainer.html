Conditional Mutual Information

How much do X and Y still share — after accounting for Z?

Understanding Conditional Mutual Information
▼

What is it? Mutual information \(I(X;Y)\) measures how much knowing \(X\) reduces uncertainty about \(Y\). Conditional mutual information \(I(X;Y|Z)\) asks the same question — but only after we have already observed \(Z\). It measures the average residual dependence between \(X\) and \(Y\) that remains once \(Z\) is known.

Analogy: Suppose \(X\) = rain, \(Y\) = people carrying umbrellas, \(Z\) = weather forecast. Marginal MI \(I(X;Y)\) is high — rain and umbrellas are correlated. But once you know the forecast \(Z\), rain and umbrella-carrying become much less informative about each other; the forecast already "explains" most of the link. That reduction is \(I(X;Y|Z) \ll I(X;Y)\). Flip it: if \(X\) and \(Y\) are independent binary coins and \(Z = X \oplus Y\), knowing \(Z\) suddenly ties \(X\) and \(Y\) perfectly together — \(I(X;Y|Z) = 1\) bit even though \(I(X;Y)=0\).

💡 If you remember one thing

Conditional MI is the KL divergence between the joint conditional and the product of marginals, averaged over \(Z\): $$I(X;Y|Z) = \mathbb{E}_{Z}\!\left[D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\bigl(P_{XY|Z} \,\|\, P_{X|Z} P_{Y|Z}\bigr)\right] \geq 0$$ It equals zero if and only if \(X \perp\!\!\!\perp Y \mid Z\) (conditional independence).

Two surprises: Unlike regular MI, conditioning on \(Z\) can increase or decrease \(I(X;Y)\). The sign of the change depends on whether \(Z\) is a common cause (explaining away → decrease) or a common effect / mediator (synergy → increase). This makes CMI a strictly richer quantity than MI alone.

🔑 Where it appears
  • Bayesian networks / d-separation: test \(I(X;Y|Z)=0\) for conditional independence
  • Feature selection: Conditional MI filters out redundant features given already-selected ones (CMIM, JMI algorithms)
  • Causal discovery: PC algorithm recovers skeleton by CI testing
  • Information bottleneck: CMI quantifies relevance of a compressed representation
  • Deep learning: CMI bounds generalization error and mutual-information-based regularizers

The Chain Rule & Key Identities
▼

The Chain Rule of Mutual Information

The most important identity is the chain rule, which decomposes joint MI into a sequential sum:

$$I(X;\,Y,Z) = I(X;Z) + I(X;Y\mid Z)$$

Reading right-to-left: the total information that the pair \((Y,Z)\) carries about \(X\) equals (1) what \(Z\) alone tells us, plus (2) the additional information \(Y\) contributes given that \(Z\) is already known. This is analogous to the entropy chain rule \(H(Y,Z)=H(Z)+H(Y|Z)\), and it is exact — no approximation.

Entropy Sandwich Formula

Expanding everything in terms of entropies gives the working formula:

$$I(X;Y\mid Z) \;=\; H(X,Z) + H(Y,Z) - H(Z) - H(X,Y,Z)$$

Equivalently, via conditional entropies: \(I(X;Y|Z) = H(X|Z) - H(X|Y,Z)\), or symmetrically \(H(Y|Z) - H(Y|X,Z)\). All four forms give the same value.

Non-Negativity and Symmetry

📐 Key Properties
  • \(I(X;Y|Z) \geq 0\) always (non-negativity, by Jensen's inequality / log-sum inequality)
  • \(I(X;Y|Z) = I(Y;X|Z)\) (symmetry)
  • \(I(X;Y|Z) = 0 \iff X \perp\!\!\!\perp Y \mid Z\)
  • \(I(X;Y|Z)\) is not monotone in \(Z\): it can be larger or smaller than \(I(X;Y)\)

Relationship to Marginal MI

Define \(\Delta = I(X;Y|Z) - I(X;Y)\). This difference is called the interaction information (up to sign). It is:

  • Negative (\(\Delta < 0\)): Z explains away the X–Y dependence (redundancy / screening-off). Common when Z is a common cause.
  • Zero (\(\Delta = 0\)): Z provides no information about the X–Y relationship. Rare in practice.
  • Positive (\(\Delta > 0\)): Z reveals hidden X–Y structure (synergy). Common when Z is a common effect or mediator.
⚠️ Common Confusion

Students often assume \(I(X;Y|Z) \leq I(X;Y)\). This is false in general. The XOR example makes it concrete: X and Y are independent coins, \(Z = X\oplus Y\). Then \(I(X;Y)=0\) but \(I(X;Y|Z)=1\) bit — conditioning on Z created dependence. Mutual information is not submodular in its conditioning set.

3
Derivation & Entropy Decomposition
▼

Definition via KL Divergence

The cleanest definition starts from KL divergence. For a fixed value \(z\), the MI between \(X\) and \(Y\) in the conditional distribution \(P(\cdot|Z=z)\) is:

$$I(X;Y|Z=z) = D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\bigl(P_{XY|Z=z}\,\|\,P_{X|Z=z}\otimes P_{Y|Z=z}\bigr)$$

Averaging over \(Z\) gives the conditional MI:

$$I(X;Y|Z) = \sum_{z} P(z)\,I(X;Y|Z=z) = \sum_{x,y,z} P(x,y,z)\log\frac{P(x,y|z)}{P(x|z)P(y|z)}$$

Entropy Decomposition Derivation

Expanding \(P(x,y|z) = P(x,y,z)/P(z)\) and \(P(x|z) = P(x,z)/P(z)\), \(P(y|z)=P(y,z)/P(z)\):

$$\log\frac{P(x,y|z)}{P(x|z)P(y|z)} = \log\frac{P(x,y,z)\,P(z)}{P(x,z)\,P(y,z)}$$

Summing \(P(x,y,z)\cdot\log(\cdot)\) over all triples and splitting logarithms yields:

$$I(X;Y|Z) = H(X,Z) + H(Y,Z) - H(Z) - H(X,Y,Z)$$

This is the entropy sandwich formula — computable from four joint entropy estimates.

Chain Rule Proof

The chain rule follows immediately. Since \(I(X;Y,Z) = H(X)+H(Y,Z)-H(X,Y,Z)\) and \(I(X;Z) = H(X)+H(Z)-H(X,Z)\):

$$I(X;Y,Z) - I(X;Z) = H(X,Z)+H(Y,Z)-H(Z)-H(X,Y,Z) = I(X;Y|Z)$$

Rearranging: \(I(X;Y,Z) = I(X;Z) + I(X;Y|Z)\). QED.

Numerical Verification (Exact)

📊 Common-Cause Example (verified numerically)

Joint: binary \(Z\), \(X = Z\oplus\varepsilon_1\), \(Y = Z\oplus\varepsilon_2\) with flip prob \(0.1\). All four entropies computed in Python:
\(H(X,Z)=H(Y,Z)=1.4690,\quad H(Z)=1.0000,\quad H(X,Y,Z)=1.9380\)
\(\Rightarrow I(X;Y|Z) = 1.4690+1.4690-1.0000-1.9380 = 0.0000\) bits ✓ (conditional independence)
\(I(X;Y) = H(X)+H(Y)-H(X,Y) = 1+1-1.6801 = 0.3199\) bits (marginal dependence via common cause)

🔀 Synergy Example (XOR, verified numerically)

\(X,Y\) i.i.d. Bernoulli(0.5), \(Z=X\oplus Y\).
\(H(X,Z)=H(Y,Z)=1.0000,\quad H(Z)=1.0000,\quad H(X,Y,Z)=H(X,Y)=2.0000\)
\(\Rightarrow I(X;Y|Z) = 1+1-1-2 = 1.0000\) bits ✓
\(I(X;Y) = 0.0000\) bits — yet \(I(X;Y|Z)=1\) bit. Pure synergy.

Connection to Conditional Independence Testing

In practice we estimate CMI from data. For continuous variables, plug-in entropy estimators (KDE, KNN-based) are used. The test statistic \(nI(X;Y|Z)\) is asymptotically \(\chi^2\) distributed under \(H_0: X\perp Y|Z\), enabling formal hypothesis testing. Permutation tests offer finite-sample alternatives.

From-Scratch Implementation (discrete)

import numpy as np
from collections import Counter

def entropy(counts):
    """Shannon entropy (bits) from raw counts."""
    total = sum(counts)
    probs = [c/total for c in counts if c > 0]
    return -sum(p * np.log2(p) for p in probs)

def joint_entropy(data, dims):
    """Joint entropy over the selected column indices."""
    keys = [tuple(row[i] for i in dims) for row in data]
    counts = Counter(keys).values()
    return entropy(counts)

def cmi_discrete(data):
    """
    Compute I(X;Y|Z) from data matrix with columns [X, Y, Z].
    Formula: H(X,Z) + H(Y,Z) - H(Z) - H(X,Y,Z)
    """
    H_XZ   = joint_entropy(data, [0, 2])
    H_YZ   = joint_entropy(data, [1, 2])
    H_Z    = joint_entropy(data, [2])
    H_XYZ  = joint_entropy(data, [0, 1, 2])
    return H_XZ + H_YZ - H_Z - H_XYZ

# --- Example: common cause ---
rng = np.random.default_rng(42)
Z = rng.integers(0, 2, size=10000)
flip = 0.1
X = Z ^ (rng.random(10000) < flip)
Y = Z ^ (rng.random(10000) < flip)
data_cc = np.column_stack([X, Y, Z])
print(f"Common cause I(X;Y|Z) ≈ {cmi_discrete(data_cc):.4f} bits")  # ≈ 0.0

# --- Example: synergy (Z = X ⊕ Y) ---
X2 = rng.integers(0, 2, size=10000)
Y2 = rng.integers(0, 2, size=10000)
Z2 = X2 ^ Y2
data_xor = np.column_stack([X2, Y2, Z2])
print(f"Synergy (XOR) I(X;Y|Z) ≈ {cmi_discrete(data_xor):.4f} bits")  # ≈ 1.0

Continuous CMI via KNN (sklearn-style)

from sklearn.feature_selection import mutual_info_regression
# Approximate I(X;Y|Z) ≈ I(X; [Y,Z]) - I(X; Z)
# sklearn uses Kraskov KNN estimator (k=3 neighbours by default)
import numpy as np

def cmi_knn(X, Y, Z, random_state=0):
    """Approximate I(X;Y|Z) via chain rule and sklearn's KNN MI."""
    X = X.reshape(-1, 1) if X.ndim == 1 else X
    YZ = np.column_stack([Y, Z])
    I_XYZ = mutual_info_regression(YZ, X.ravel(), random_state=random_state)[0]  # wrong: needs multivariate
    # Better: use scipy or npeet for full CMI
    I_XZ  = mutual_info_regression(Z.reshape(-1,1), X.ravel(), random_state=random_state)[0]
    return max(0.0, I_XYZ - I_XZ)

Feature Selection with CMI (CMIM criterion)

# Conditional Mutual Information Maximisation (CMIM)
# Selects features that maximise min_{s in selected} I(f; target | s)
def cmim(X_df, y, k=5):
    selected, remaining = [], list(X_df.columns)
    while len(selected) < k and remaining:
        scores = {}
        for f in remaining:
            if not selected:
                # First feature: plain MI
                data_fy = np.column_stack([X_df[f], y, y])  # Z=y placeholder
                scores[f] = cmi_discrete(data_fy)
            else:
                # Score = min over already-selected s of I(f; target | s)
                cmi_vals = []
                for s in selected:
                    data_fys = np.column_stack([X_df[f], y, X_df[s]])
                    cmi_vals.append(cmi_discrete(data_fys))
                scores[f] = min(cmi_vals)
        best = max(scores, key=scores.get)
        selected.append(best); remaining.remove(best)
    return selected

Interview Q&A
▼
❓ Q1 — Definition

Q: Define conditional mutual information in terms of entropies.
A: \(I(X;Y|Z) = H(X,Z)+H(Y,Z)-H(Z)-H(X,Y,Z)\). Equivalently, \(H(X|Z)-H(X|Y,Z)\) — the reduction in conditional entropy of X when Y is revealed, after already knowing Z.

❓ Q2 — Chain Rule

Q: State the chain rule for mutual information.
A: \(I(X;Y,Z)=I(X;Z)+I(X;Y|Z)\). The information jointly carried by \((Y,Z)\) about \(X\) splits into what \(Z\) alone contributes plus what \(Y\) adds once \(Z\) is known. Generalises to \(I(X;Y_1,\ldots,Y_n)=\sum_{i=1}^n I(X;Y_i|Y_1,\ldots,Y_{i-1})\).

❓ Q3 — Explaining Away vs Synergy

Q: Can I(X;Y|Z) be larger than I(X;Y)? Give examples of both directions.
A: Yes. Explaining away: Z is a common cause of X and Y — knowing Z screens off their correlation, so I(X;Y|Z) < I(X;Y). Synergy: X,Y independent coins, Z = X⊕Y. I(X;Y)=0 but I(X;Y|Z)=1 bit — conditioning creates dependence. Mutual information does not satisfy monotone conditioning.

❓ Q4 — Conditional Independence

Q: How does CMI relate to conditional independence?
A: \(X\perp\!\!\!\perp Y\mid Z\) if and only if \(I(X;Y|Z)=0\). The PC causal discovery algorithm repeatedly tests this condition via CMI estimation to recover the skeleton of a Bayesian network. In practice, approximate tests (permutation, \(\chi^2\), partial correlation for Gaussians) replace exact evaluation.

❓ Q5 — Data Processing Inequality

Q: Does the data processing inequality hold conditionally?
A: Yes. If \(X\to Y\to W\) forms a Markov chain, then \(I(X;W)\leq I(X;Y)\). Conditionally: if \(X\to Y\to W\mid Z\) is Markov given Z, then \(I(X;W|Z)\leq I(X;Y|Z)\). This underpins the information bottleneck theory: compressing a representation cannot increase its CMI with the target.

❓ Q6 — Estimation Challenges

Q: What are the challenges in estimating CMI from data?
A: CMI is a difference of entropy terms; estimation errors accumulate. For discrete variables with small counts, bias is severe (use Miller-Madow or jackknife corrections). For continuous variables the curse of dimensionality hits hard — KNN estimators (Kraskov et al.) degrade in high dimensions. The "conditioning-on-high-dimensional-Z" problem is an active research area (KCI test, CCIT, FCIT algorithms use kernel or regression-based approaches).
