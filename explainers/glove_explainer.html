GloVe: Global Vectors for Word Representation

Fitting word vectors to a global co-occurrence matrix via weighted least squares
Intermediate NLP Word Embeddings O(nnz(X)) per epoch
1
Understanding GloVe
▼

What is it? GloVe (Global Vectors, Pennington, Socher & Manning, 2014) is an unsupervised algorithm that turns a corpus into dense word vectors by counting how often every pair of words co-occurs across the entire corpus, then fitting vectors so their dot product reproduces the logarithm of that count. It sits between two older families: matrix-factorization methods like LSA (global counts, but weak on analogy structure) and local context-window predictors like word2vec's skip-gram (great local structure, but throws away corpus-wide statistics one window at a time).

Analogy: Skip-gram is like learning a language by eavesdropping on one conversation at a time and updating your beliefs after every sentence. GloVe is like first compiling a giant spreadsheet of "how often does word A appear near word B, anywhere in the whole library," and only then fitting a model to that spreadsheet. The spreadsheet is the co-occurrence matrix; the fitting procedure is weighted least squares.

💡 If you remember one thing

GloVe trains vectors so that \(w_i \cdot \tilde{w}_j + b_i + \tilde{b}_j \approx \log X_{ij}\), where \(X_{ij}\) is how often word \(j\) occurs in word \(i\)'s context. Rare pairs and unseen pairs are down-weighted by a function \(f(X_{ij})\) so noise and stop-word pairs don't dominate the loss.

How GloVe Works Internally
▼

Step 1 — Build the Global Co-occurrence Matrix

Scan the corpus once with a symmetric sliding context window of size \(L\). For every pair of words \((i, j)\) that fall within \(L\) tokens of each other at distance \(d\), add \(1/d\) to both \(X_{ij}\) and \(X_{ji}\). The \(1/d\) weighting means adjacent words contribute more than words at the edge of the window — a word right next to you tells you more about its meaning than one three tokens away.

Step 2 — Why Not Just Factorize \(\log X\) Directly?

Early count-based methods (LSA, HAL) applied SVD directly to raw or log-transformed counts. This fails for three reasons: (1) \(\log 0\) is undefined, but most word pairs never co-occur — the matrix is extremely sparse; (2) rare co-occurrences are statistically noisy and shouldn't be trusted as much as frequent ones; (3) extremely frequent pairs (stop-word pairs like "the"–"of") shouldn't dominate the objective just because they're frequent — beyond a point, more counts add no new information.

Step 3 — The Weighting Function \(f(x)\)

GloVe's fix is a weighted least-squares objective where each squared error is scaled by \(f(X_{ij})\):

$$f(x) = \begin{cases} (x / x_{\max})^{\alpha} & \text{if } x < x_{\max} \\ 1 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

This function is 0 at \(x=0\) (unseen pairs are simply skipped — no gradient), grows smoothly and sub-linearly for small counts, and saturates at 1 once \(x \ge x_{\max}\) so very frequent pairs get no extra leverage. The paper uses \(\alpha = 3/4\) and \(x_{\max} = 100\); the same \(3/4\) exponent that tempers frequent words in word2vec's negative-sampling distribution.

🔑 Design Decision: Why This Exact Shape?

Pennington et al. tried several weighting shapes empirically; a power law with \(\alpha \approx 0.75\) gave the best downstream analogy accuracy — steep enough to suppress noisy rare pairs, flat enough near \(x_{\max}\) to avoid stop-word domination. It is a design choice validated by experiment, not derived from first principles.

Why It Works — The Math
▼

Starting Point: Ratios of Co-occurrence Probabilities Encode Meaning

Let \(P_{ik} = X_{ik} / X_i\) be the probability that word \(k\) appears in the context of word \(i\), where \(X_i = \sum_k X_{ik}\). The GloVe paper's key empirical observation is that the ratio \(P_{ik}/P_{jk}\) is far more informative than either probability alone. For \(i=\)"ice", \(j=\)"steam": if \(k=\)"solid", the ratio is large (solid relates to ice, not steam); if \(k=\)"gas", the ratio is small; if \(k=\)"water" or "fashion" (related to both, or neither), the ratio is \(\approx 1\).

From Ratios to a Log-Bilinear Model

Requiring a function \(F(w_i, w_j, \tilde{w}_k) = P_{ik}/P_{jk}\) that depends only on vector differences and is invariant to how "context" vs "center" words are labeled leads — after imposing homomorphism between \((\mathbb{R}, +)\) and \((\mathbb{R}_{>0}, \times)\), i.e. using \(\exp\) — to the constraint:

$$w_i^\top \tilde{w}_k + b_i + \tilde{b}_k = \log X_{ik}$$

The bias terms \(b_i, \tilde{b}_k\) absorb the marginal frequencies \(\log X_i\) and \(\log X_k\) that a pure dot product cannot represent, restoring the symmetry that \(P_{ik}/P_{jk}\) required.

The Weighted Least-Squares Objective

Turning the equality above into a fitting problem over all observed pairs gives the full GloVe loss:

$$J = \sum_{i,j=1}^{V} f(X_{ij}) \left( w_i^\top \tilde{w}_j + b_i + \tilde{b}_j - \log X_{ij} \right)^2$$

The gradient with respect to \(w_i\) (context vectors and biases are symmetric) is:

$$\frac{\partial J}{\partial w_i} = 2\sum_j f(X_{ij})\left(w_i^\top \tilde{w}_j + b_i + \tilde{b}_j - \log X_{ij}\right)\tilde{w}_j$$

GloVe trains with AdaGrad (per-parameter adaptive learning rates) because gradient magnitudes vary hugely across the long-tailed frequency distribution of \(X_{ij}\). The final word vector is usually \(w_i + \tilde{w}_i\) — summing center and context vectors is empirically a small but consistent accuracy boost, and is cheap because both share the same objective at convergence.

⚠️ Common Misconception

"GloVe is just SVD on the co-occurrence matrix." It is not: SVD minimizes unweighted squared reconstruction error on raw (or log) counts, treating every entry — including the millions of zero and near-zero entries — equally. GloVe's \(f(x)\) weighting, bias terms, and skip-zero-pairs behavior make it a fundamentally different, nonconvex weighted regression, closer in spirit to word2vec's implicit factorization of a shifted PMI matrix than to classical LSA.

Key Takeaways
▼

When to use GloVe:

  • You have a large, static corpus and want fast, reproducible, corpus-wide word vectors
  • Downstream task benefits from strong linear analogy structure (king − man + woman ≈ queen)
  • You want training that scales with the number of nonzero co-occurrence entries, not corpus length — one pass builds \(X\), then training is fast

When NOT to use:

  • You need context-sensitive representations (the same word meaning different things in different sentences) — use a contextual transformer encoder instead
  • Vocabulary is huge and streaming/online updates are required — the co-occurrence matrix must be rebuilt
  • Corpus is small — sparse, noisy co-occurrence statistics give unstable vectors regardless of weighting

💡 Positioning

GloVe explicitly unifies the two dominant 2013–2014 paradigms: global matrix factorization (counts) and local context prediction (word2vec) — by factorizing global counts with a loss shaped to mimic what predictive models implicitly optimize.

import numpy as np
# 1. Corpus & vocabulary
sentences = [["I","like","deep","learning"],
["I","like","NLP"],
["I","enjoy","flying"]]
vocab = ["I","like","enjoy","deep","learning","NLP","flying"]
idx = {w: i for i, w in enumerate(vocab)}
# 2. Global co-occurrence matrix, window=2, weight = 1/distance
X = np.zeros((7, 7))
for sent in sentences:
ids = [idx[w] for w in sent]
for p in range(len(ids)):
for q in range(p+1, min(p+2, len(ids)-1)+1):
d = q - p
X[ids[p], ids[q]] += 1.0 / d
X[ids[q], ids[p]] += 1.0 / d
# 3. Weighting function f(x)
x_max, alpha = 2.0, 0.75
def f(x):
return (x / x_max) ** alpha if x < x_max else 1.0
# 4. GloVe objective: weighted least squares
# J = sum_ij f(X_ij) * (w_i . wt_j + b_i + bt_j - log X_ij)^2
# 5. Train with (Ada)Grad descent
