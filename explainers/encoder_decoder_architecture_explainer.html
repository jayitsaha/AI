Understanding the Encoder-Decoder Transformer
▼

What is it? The original Transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017) is a neural network built entirely out of attention — no recurrence, no convolution. It has two stacks: an encoder that reads the whole source sentence at once and builds a rich representation of it, and a decoder that generates the output sentence one token at a time, looking back at both what it has already written and the encoder's representation.

Analogy: Think of a human interpreter translating a speech. The encoder is like reading and fully understanding the entire source sentence first — building a mental map of how every word relates to every other word. The decoder is like speaking the translation aloud, one word at a time: at each moment you glance back at your own words so far (self-attention, but you're not allowed to peek at words you haven't said yet — hence "masked") and you glance at your mental map of the source sentence (cross-attention) to decide the next word.

💡 If you remember one thing

The decoder has one extra attention block the encoder doesn't: cross-attention, where queries come from the decoder but keys/values come from the encoder's output. That's the single wire connecting the two stacks — everything else (self-attention, FFN, Add&Norm, positional encoding) is shared machinery.

How It Works Internally
▼

Encoder stack (× N = 6 in the paper)

Source tokens are embedded to \(\mathbb{R}^{d_{model}}\) (\(d_{model}=512\) in the paper) and summed with a fixed sinusoidal positional encoding since attention itself has no notion of order. Each encoder layer then applies two sublayers, each wrapped in a residual connection and LayerNorm: (1) multi-head self-attention, where every source position attends to every other source position, and (2) a position-wise feed-forward network (two linear layers with a ReLU/GELU in between, expanding to \(d_{ff}=2048\) and back). The final encoder layer's output is the "memory" — a sequence of contextualized vectors, one per source token.

Decoder stack (× N = 6, autoregressive)

Target tokens (shifted right by one, so position \(i\) predicts token \(i+1\)) are embedded + positionally encoded the same way. Each decoder layer has three sublayers: (1) masked multi-head self-attention — identical to encoder self-attention except future positions are masked with \(-\infty\) before softmax, so token \(i\) can only see tokens \(\le i\); (2) multi-head cross-attention — queries \(Q\) come from the decoder's own hidden state, but keys \(K\) and values \(V\) come from the encoder's memory. This is the sole channel through which decoder positions gather information about the source sentence; (3) the same position-wise feed-forward network. A final linear layer projects to vocabulary logits, followed by softmax.

Design decision: why masking instead of a separate causal architecture?

Masking lets the decoder be trained with full teacher-forcing parallelism (one matrix multiply scores every position against every position) while still guaranteeing the autoregressive property at inference. The alternative — feeding one token at a time even during training — would be far slower and is unnecessary since the mask enforces the same causal constraint.

🔑 Key Property

Every sublayer computes \(\text{LayerNorm}(x + \text{Sublayer}(x))\). The residual stream lets gradients and positional/token identity flow unimpeded through all \(2N\) (encoder) + \(3N\) (decoder) sublayers — attention and the FFN only ever add a learned correction to the running representation.

Why It Works — The Math
▼

Scaled dot-product attention

The core primitive, used in all three attention blocks (encoder self-attn, decoder masked self-attn, decoder cross-attn):

$$\text{Attention}(Q,K,V) = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}} + M\right)V$$

where \(Q \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times d_k}\), \(K,V \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times d_k}\), and \(M\) is an additive mask (\(-\infty\) on disallowed positions, \(0\) elsewhere — empty for encoder self-attention and cross-attention, upper-triangular for decoder self-attention). The \(\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_k}}\) scale keeps dot products from growing large with \(d_k\), which would push softmax into a saturated, low-gradient regime.

Multi-head attention

$$\text{MultiHead}(Q,K,V) = \text{Concat}(h_1,\dots,h_H)W^O, \quad h_i = \text{Attention}(QW_i^Q,\, KW_i^K,\, VW_i^V)$$

Splitting \(d_{model}\) into \(H\) heads of size \(d_k = d_{model}/H\) lets different heads specialize (e.g. one head tracks syntactic dependencies, another tracks coreference), at the same total compute as one full-size head.

Positional encoding

$$PE_{(pos,2i)} = \sin\!\left(\frac{pos}{10000^{2i/d_{model}}}\right), \quad PE_{(pos,2i+1)} = \cos\!\left(\frac{pos}{10000^{2i/d_{model}}}\right)$$

Sinusoids of geometrically increasing wavelength let the model learn to attend by relative position, since \(PE_{pos+k}\) is a linear function of \(PE_{pos}\) for any fixed offset \(k\).

⚠️ Common Misconception

"The decoder mask makes attention causal like an RNN." Not quite — the decoder still computes attention over all positions in parallel during training; the mask just zeroes out (via \(-\infty\)) the softmax weight on future positions. It's a masked parallel computation, not a sequential one — that's exactly why the Transformer trains so much faster than an RNN.

Key Takeaways
▼

The encoder-decoder Transformer separates understanding (encoder: bidirectional self-attention over the whole input, computed once) from generation (decoder: causal self-attention + cross-attention, computed autoregressively). Cross-attention is the only bridge between the two stacks — decoder queries read encoder keys/values at every layer, every step.

Use it when: the task is a genuine sequence-transduction problem with a distinct input and output sequence — machine translation, summarization, speech-to-text. Prefer decoder-only (GPT-style) for open-ended generation/chat where there's no clean "source" to encode separately, and encoder-only (BERT-style) for representation learning / classification where you don't need to generate text at all.
