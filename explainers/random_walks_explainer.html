Understanding Random Walks
▼

What is it? A random walk is the path traced by an object that takes a sequence of steps, where each step direction is chosen randomly. The simplest version: flip a coin at every step — heads means +1, tails means −1. Your position after \(n\) steps is just the running total of all those flips.

Analogy: Imagine a drunk person walking out of a bar. Each second they take one step, but they can't control their direction — 50% chance left, 50% right. After 100 seconds they're somewhere, but where? Not far from where they started on average — but the spread of possible positions grows steadily as \(\sqrt{n}\). This diffusive spread is the hallmark of a random walk.

If you remember one thing

After \(n\) steps of ±1, the expected position is zero (\(\mathbb{E}[S_n] = 0\)), but the expected distance from zero grows as \(\sqrt{n}\): specifically \(\mathbb{E}[|S_n|] \approx \sqrt{2n/\pi}\). This \(\sqrt{n}\) "diffusive scaling" appears everywhere — Brownian motion, SGD noise, the central limit theorem.

How Random Walks Work Internally
▼

Formal Definition

Let \(X_1, X_2, \ldots\) be i.i.d. random variables with \(\Pr(X_i = +1) = \Pr(X_i = -1) = \frac{1}{2}\). The simple symmetric random walk is the partial-sum process:

$$S_0 = 0, \qquad S_n = X_1 + X_2 + \cdots + X_n = \sum_{k=1}^n X_k$$

Key Statistical Properties

Since each step is independent with mean 0 and variance 1:

$$\mathbb{E}[S_n] = 0 \qquad \operatorname{Var}(S_n) = n \qquad \mathbb{E}[S_n^2] = n$$

The spread is linear in \(n\), so the standard deviation grows as \(\sqrt{n}\). By the CLT, the rescaled walk converges to a Gaussian:

$$\frac{S_n}{\sqrt{n}} \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0,1) \quad \text{as } n \to \infty$$

The √n Diffusive Envelope

Most paths stay within a band of width ±2√n around zero most of the time. More precisely, by the law of the iterated logarithm:

$$\limsup_{n \to \infty} \frac{S_n}{\sqrt{2n \log \log n}} = 1 \quad \text{almost surely}$$
Key Property: Recurrence vs. Transience

Pólya's theorem (1921): The simple random walk is recurrent in 1D and 2D — with probability 1 it returns to the origin infinitely often. In 3D and higher it is transient — it escapes to infinity almost surely. The 3D return probability is approximately 0.3405.

Why It Works — The Math
▼

Deriving E[|S_n|] ∝ √n

By the CLT, \(S_n / \sqrt{n} \approx Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)\) for large \(n\). For a standard normal \(Z\):

$$\mathbb{E}[|Z|] = \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}$$

Because \(|S_n| = \sqrt{n} \cdot |S_n/\sqrt{n}| \approx \sqrt{n} \cdot |Z|\), we get:

$$\mathbb{E}[|S_n|] \approx \sqrt{\frac{2n}{\pi}} \approx 0.7979\,\sqrt{n}$$

Numerically verified: at \(n = 1000\), simulation gives \(\mathbb{E}[|S_n|] \approx 24.94\) vs \(\sqrt{2000/\pi} \approx 25.23\).

Reflection Principle & Ballot Problem

A fundamental combinatorial tool: every path from 0 to position \(k > 0\) that crosses zero can be reflected at the first zero-crossing to give a bijection with paths ending at \(-k\). This reflection principle powers the ballot problem:

$$\Pr(S_1 > 0,\, S_2 > 0,\, \ldots,\, S_{2n} > 0) = \Pr(S_{2n} = 0) = \binom{2n}{n} 2^{-2n} \sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi n}}$$

Connection to Diffusion

As the step size \(\delta x \to 0\) and step time \(\delta t \to 0$ with $\delta x^2 / \delta t = 2D$ (constant), the random walk converges to Brownian motion \(B_t\), the solution to the diffusion equation:

$$\frac{\partial p}{\partial t} = D \frac{\partial^2 p}{\partial x^2}, \qquad p(x,t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi D t}} \exp\!\left(-\frac{x^2}{4Dt}\right)$$
Common Pitfall

The expected position is zero, but the typical position grows as √n. Confusing \(\mathbb{E}[S_n] = 0\) (no average drift) with \(\mathbb{E}[|S_n|] = 0\) (no spread) is wrong — the walk does spread, it just spreads symmetrically around zero.

Key Takeaways & Connections
▼

Where random walks appear:

  • Brownian motion / diffusion — continuous limit of the discrete random walk
  • SGD noise — stochastic gradient descent follows a random walk in parameter space around the loss minimum
  • Stock prices — geometric Brownian motion, the basis of Black-Scholes
  • MCMC — Metropolis-Hastings uses a random walk to explore the posterior
  • PageRank — stationary distribution of a random walk on the web graph

Pólya recurrence summary:

  • 1D: Recurrent — return probability = 1
  • 2D: Recurrent — return probability = 1
  • 3D: Transient — return probability ≈ 0.3405
  • ≥4D: Transient — return probability decreases toward 0
The Big Picture

Random walks are the discrete skeleton of continuous stochastic processes. The universality of the \(\sqrt{n}\) scaling — driven by the CLT — means that almost any process with independent increments of finite variance will exhibit this diffusive behavior, regardless of the precise step distribution.

Code Trace
▼
import numpy as np
# Simple symmetric random walk: each step ±1 with prob 1/2
np.random.seed(42)
N = 1000 # number of steps
steps = np.random.choice([-1, 1], size=N) # i.i.d. ±1
S = np.concatenate([[0], np.cumsum(steps)]) # S_0=0, S_n=sum
# Key statistics
print(f"E[S_N] ≈ {S[-1]:.3f}") # single path, near 0
print(f"Var[S_N] ≈ {N}") # always = N
print(f"√N = {np.sqrt(N):.3f}") # std dev of final pos
# Monte Carlo verification of E[|S_N|]
trials = 10000
finals = np.sum(np.random.choice([-1,1],
size=(trials, N)), axis=1)
mean_abs = np.mean(np.abs(finals))
theory = np.sqrt(2*N/np.pi) # ≈ 25.23
print(f"E[|S_N|] ≈ {mean_abs:.3f}") # ≈ 24.94
print(f"√(2N/π) = {theory:.3f}") # ≈ 25.23
# Diffusion limit: rescale by 1/√N
t = np.linspace(0, 1, N+1)
B = S / np.sqrt(N) # → Brownian motion
envelope = np.sqrt(t) # ±√t band
