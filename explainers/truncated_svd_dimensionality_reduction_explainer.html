1
Understanding Truncated SVD
▼

What is it? Every matrix \(A\) can be decomposed into a sum of rank-1 "slices," each one contributing a different amount of energy (measured by its singular value). Truncated SVD discards the small-energy slices and keeps only the top \(k\)—producing the best possible rank-\(k\) approximation of \(A\). The result is a compressed representation that preserves the dominant structure.

Analogy: Imagine a high-resolution photo stored as millions of pixels. SVD finds the most important "patterns" in the image (like edges, gradients, colour blocks). Truncated SVD keeps only the top-\(k\) patterns, like JPEG compression: \(k=5\) looks blurry, \(k=50\) looks nearly perfect. You're not storing the whole photo — just its essence.

💡 If you remember one thing

The truncated SVD \(A_k = U_k \Sigma_k V_k^\top\) is the best rank-\(k\) approximation of \(A\) in both Frobenius and spectral norm — no other rank-\(k\) matrix is closer. Keeping the top \(k\) singular values retains fraction \(\sum_{i=1}^k \sigma_i^2 / \sum_{i=1}^r \sigma_i^2\) of the total variance ("energy").

Three practical uses covered here: (1) Low-rank matrix approximation — compress/denoise data. (2) Latent Semantic Analysis (LSA) — find hidden topics in text by applying truncated SVD to a term-document matrix. (3) PCA via SVD — center the data, run SVD on the data matrix (not the covariance matrix) to get principal components directly.

2
How It Works — From Full SVD to Rank-k
▼

Step 1 — Full (Economy) SVD

For \(A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}\) with \(m \geq n\), the economy (thin) SVD is:

$$A = U \Sigma V^\top, \quad U \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n},\ \Sigma = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_n),\ V \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$$

with \(\sigma_1 \geq \sigma_2 \geq \cdots \geq \sigma_n \geq 0\). \(U\) and \(V\) have orthonormal columns.

Step 2 — Keep only the Top-k Triplets

Truncate to the first \(k\) columns of \(U\), the \(k\times k\) top-left block of \(\Sigma\), and the first \(k\) rows of \(V^\top\):

$$A_k = U_k \Sigma_k V_k^\top = \sum_{i=1}^{k} \sigma_i \, \mathbf{u}_i \mathbf{v}_i^\top$$

This outer-product expansion shows that \(A_k\) is literally a sum of \(k\) rank-1 matrices weighted by their singular values. Higher \(\sigma_i \Rightarrow\) more important slice.

Step 3 — Retained Energy / Explained Variance

$$\text{Energy retained by } A_k = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^k \sigma_i^2}{\sum_{i=1}^r \sigma_i^2} = \frac{\|A_k\|_F^2}{\|A\|_F^2}$$

The squared singular values play exactly the role of eigenvalues of \(A^\top A\): each \(\sigma_i^2\) is the variance explained by the \(i\)-th principal direction. The scree plot shows this curve; a sharp "elbow" tells you a good \(k\).

🔑 Eckart–Young Theorem (the guarantee)

For any matrix \(B\) of rank at most \(k\): $$\|A - A_k\|_F \leq \|A - B\|_F \quad \text{and} \quad \|A - A_k\|_2 \leq \|A - B\|_2$$ \(A_k\) is optimal — no rank-\(k\) approximation does better.

LSA: Apply to a Term-Document Matrix

Build \(A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\) where \(A_{ij}\) = TF-IDF weight of term \(i\) in document \(j\). After truncated SVD with small \(k\): \(U_k\) gives a \(k\)-dimensional embedding of terms; \(V_k\) gives embeddings of documents; rows of \(U_k\Sigma_k\) are "topic coordinates" for each term. Documents and terms close in this space share latent topics.

PCA via SVD of Centered Data

Mean-center the data: \(\tilde{X} = X - \bar{X}\). Then \(\tilde{X} = U\Sigma V^\top\). The columns of \(V_k\) are the principal components (loading vectors); the projected scores are \(T_k = U_k \Sigma_k = \tilde{X} V_k\). Singular values satisfy \(\sigma_i^2/(n-1) = \lambda_i\) (the \(i\)-th eigenvalue of the sample covariance matrix).

⚠️ Common Confusion

PCA via SVD of the data matrix \(\tilde{X}\) is numerically superior to computing the covariance matrix \(\tilde{X}^\top\tilde{X}/(n-1)\) and then eigendecomposing — it avoids squaring condition numbers and is preferred in all production implementations (scikit-learn, numpy).

3
The Math — Derivations & Key Formulas
▼

Why squared singular values = variance

For the economy SVD \(\tilde{X} = U\Sigma V^\top\) of a centred \(n\times p\) data matrix:

$$\frac{1}{n-1}\tilde{X}^\top\tilde{X} = \frac{1}{n-1}V\Sigma^\top U^\top U \Sigma V^\top = V \frac{\Sigma^2}{n-1} V^\top$$

This is the eigendecomposition of the sample covariance matrix \(S\). The eigenvalues are \(\lambda_i = \sigma_i^2/(n-1)\) and the eigenvectors are the columns of \(V\). So

$$\text{Explained variance ratio of PC}_i = \frac{\sigma_i^2}{\sum_j \sigma_j^2} = \frac{\lambda_i}{\sum_j \lambda_j}$$

Projected scores

The score of the \(j\)-th observation on the \(i\)-th principal component is the \((j,i)\) element of:

$$T_k = \tilde{X} V_k = U_k \Sigma_k \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times k}$$

Reconstruction formula

$$\hat{X} = T_k V_k^\top = U_k \Sigma_k V_k^\top$$

The reconstruction error in Frobenius norm equals the sum of dropped eigenvalues:

$$\|A - A_k\|_F^2 = \sum_{i=k+1}^{r} \sigma_i^2$$

LSA cosine similarity

In the \(k\)-dimensional LSA space, term \(i\) has row vector \(\mathbf{t}_i = (U_k)_i \cdot \Sigma_k\) and document \(j\) has row \(\mathbf{d}_j = (V_k)_j \cdot \Sigma_k\). Semantic similarity is:

$$\text{sim}(\text{term}_i, \text{doc}_j) = \frac{\mathbf{t}_i \cdot \mathbf{d}_j}{\|\mathbf{t}_i\|\,\|\mathbf{d}_j\|}$$
📐 Key formula recap

Best rank-\(k\) approx: \(A_k = U_k \Sigma_k V_k^\top\)
Energy retained: \(\sum_{i \leq k}\sigma_i^2\,/\,\sum_i\sigma_i^2\)
Reconstruction error²: \(\sum_{i>k}\sigma_i^2\)
PCA scores: \(T_k = \tilde{X}V_k = U_k\Sigma_k\)
PCA eigenvalues: \(\lambda_i = \sigma_i^2/(n-1)\)

Key Takeaways & When to Use
▼

When to use Truncated SVD

Use TruncatedSVD (sklearn) when your data matrix is sparse (e.g., TF-IDF, recommendation matrices) — it does not centre the data. Use PCA (also SVD-based internally) when you have dense, already-loaded feature matrices that you want to mean-centre first.

Choosing k

Plot the scree curve (cumulative explained variance vs \(k\)) and look for the "elbow" — the point where adding another component gives diminishing returns. In NLP/LSA, \(k\) between 50–300 is typical. For image compression, the elbow often appears around \(k = 20\text{–}100\).

✅ Summary

Truncated SVD: \(A_k = U_k\Sigma_k V_k^\top\) — optimal rank-\(k\) approximation (Eckart–Young).
Energy retained: \(\sum_{i\leq k}\sigma_i^2\,/\,\|A\|_F^2\).
LSA: apply to TF-IDF matrix; \(U_k\) = term topics, \(V_k\) = doc topics.
PCA via SVD: centre data, SVD → columns of \(V\) are PCs, \(\sigma_i^2/(n-1)=\lambda_i\).
Cost: exact SVD \(O(\min(m,n)^2\max(m,n))\); randomized SVD \(O(mnk)\).
