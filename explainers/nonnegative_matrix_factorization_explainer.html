Nonnegative Matrix Factorization (NMF)

Parts-based decomposition: \(V \approx WH\) with \(W, H \geq 0\)
Intermediate Linear Algebra Dimensionality Reduction O(knm) per iteration
1
Understanding NMF
▼

What is it? Nonnegative Matrix Factorization (NMF) takes a nonneg matrix \(V\) (e.g., pixel intensities of face images, word counts in documents) and expresses it as a product of two smaller nonneg matrices: \(V \approx W H\). Here \(W\) holds basis patterns (the "parts") and \(H\) holds the mixing coefficients (how much of each part each data point uses).

Why nonneg? The nonnegativity constraint forces an additive parts-based representation: components combine constructively, never cancelling each other out. A face becomes a sum of eye, nose, and mouth parts — never "face minus eye." This makes NMF factors directly interpretable, unlike SVD or PCA which allow arbitrary sign changes.

Analogy: Imagine you have a collection of paintings of faces. SVD/PCA might decompose them into ghostly "eigenfaces" that look like averaged or subtracted faces — hard to interpret. NMF instead finds actual face parts — an eye template, a nose template, a mouth template — and says "this face is 70% of eye-1, 50% of nose-2, 80% of mouth-3." Every pixel contribution is positive, so the parts look like real face components.

💡 If you remember one thing

NMF enforces \(W \geq 0,\; H \geq 0\) on the factorization \(V \approx WH\). This nonnegativity constraint uniquely forces an additive, parts-based representation — making the factors semantically interpretable as components that only add, never subtract.

2
How NMF Works Internally
▼

The Objective

Given an \(n \times m\) nonneg data matrix \(V\) and a rank \(r \ll \min(n,m)\), NMF solves:

$$\underset{W \geq 0,\; H \geq 0}{\operatorname{minimize}} \;\; \|V - WH\|_F^2$$

where \(W \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times r}\) and \(H \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times m}\). This is a non-convex optimization problem — no global minimum is guaranteed. In practice, multiplicative update rules (Lee & Seung 2001) are used because they are simple, closed-form, and guaranteed to be monotonically non-increasing.

The Multiplicative Update Rules

The key insight by Lee & Seung: derive updates that guarantee \(\|V - WH\|_F^2\) is non-increasing while maintaining nonnegativity. The rules are:

$$H \leftarrow H \odot \frac{W^\top V}{W^\top W H + \epsilon} \qquad W \leftarrow W \odot \frac{V H^\top}{W H H^\top + \epsilon}$$

where \(\odot\) is elementwise multiplication and \(\epsilon \approx 10^{-10}\) prevents division by zero. Each update is elementwise: since \(V, W, H \geq 0\), the numerators \(W^\top V\) and \(V H^\top\) are nonneg, and the denominators \(W^\top W H\) and \(W H H^\top\) are nonneg — so nonnegativity is automatically maintained.

Design Decision: Why Multiplicative (not Gradient Descent)?

Additive gradient descent requires a projected step (clamp negatives to 0) and separate learning rate tuning. Multiplicative updates are self-scaling — the ratio \(\frac{W^\top V}{W^\top WH}\) naturally equals 1 at convergence (KKT condition \(W^\top V = W^\top WH\)), and the update magnitude is automatically bounded. However, multiplicative updates can be slow near the optimum; many modern libraries switch to ANLS (alternating nonneg least squares) for faster convergence.

🔑 Key Property: Auxiliary Function Bound

Lee & Seung proved correctness by constructing an auxiliary function \(\mathcal{G}(H, H')\) satisfying \(\mathcal{G}(H,H) = F(H)\) and \(\mathcal{G}(H,H') \geq F(H)\). Minimizing \(\mathcal{G}\) over \(H\) while holding \(H'\) fixed gives the multiplicative update. Since each step minimizes an upper bound, \(F(H)\) is monotonically non-increasing.

⚠️ Non-Convexity and Non-Uniqueness

The joint optimization over \(W\) and \(H\) is non-convex. NMF factors are not unique: if \(WH = V\) is a factorization, then for any nonneg diagonal matrix \(D\), \((WD)(D^{-1}H)\) is also valid. Different random initializations can converge to different local optima. Multiple runs with different seeds and best-reconstruction selection is standard practice.

3
The Math Behind NMF
▼

KKT Conditions

Since the objective is lower-bounded by 0 and the constraints are \(W \geq 0,\; H \geq 0\), the KKT conditions for stationarity are:

$$\frac{\partial \|V - WH\|_F^2}{\partial H_{ak}} = -2(W^\top V)_{ak} + 2(W^\top W H)_{ak} = 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad (W^\top W H)_{ak} = (W^\top V)_{ak}$$

Complementary slackness: \(\mu_{ak} H_{ak} = 0\) where \(\mu_{ak} \geq 0\) is the KKT multiplier for \(H_{ak} \geq 0\). This means either \(H_{ak} = 0\) (inactive component) or the gradient is zero (active component). The multiplicative rule's fixed point exactly satisfies this: at convergence, \(H \odot \frac{W^\top V}{W^\top WH} = H\) means \(W^\top V = W^\top WH\) for positive entries.

Derivation of the Multiplicative Update for H

Starting from the gradient of the squared Frobenius loss w.r.t. \(H\):

$$\nabla_H \|V - WH\|_F^2 = -2W^\top V + 2W^\top W H$$

A standard gradient step would be \(H \leftarrow H - \alpha \nabla_H\). Lee & Seung choose the elementwise step size \(\alpha_{ak} = \frac{H_{ak}}{(W^\top W H)_{ak}}\), giving:

$$H_{ak} \leftarrow H_{ak} - \frac{H_{ak}}{(W^\top W H)_{ak}} \left[ -\,(W^\top V)_{ak} + (W^\top W H)_{ak} \right] = H_{ak} \cdot \frac{(W^\top V)_{ak}}{(W^\top W H)_{ak}}$$

This is the multiplicative update. The same derivation applies symmetrically for \(W\).

Connection to KL Divergence

With a different loss — the generalized KL divergence \(D_{KL}(V \| WH) = \sum_{ij} \left[ V_{ij} \log \frac{V_{ij}}{(WH)_{ij}} - V_{ij} + (WH)_{ij} \right]\) — the multiplicative updates become:

$$H \leftarrow H \odot \frac{W^\top (V / WH)}{\mathbf{1}^\top W} \qquad W \leftarrow W \odot \frac{(V / WH)\, H^\top}{W \mathbf{1}^\top}$$

where \(\mathbf{1}\) is an all-ones vector and division is elementwise. This variant is used for topic modeling (document-word counts follow a Poisson-like distribution better suited to KL divergence than squared error).

📐 Complexity

Each multiplicative update requires \(O(knm)\) operations (two matrix products of sizes \(k \times n\) and \(n \times m\)). For \(T\) iterations: total \(O(Tknm)\). Memory: \(O(nm + k(n+m))\). Convergence is typically reached in 50–500 iterations. ANLS-based solvers (e.g., scikit-learn's 'cd' solver) often require fewer iterations.

From Scratch (NumPy)

import numpy as np
def nmf_multiplicative(V, r, n_iter=200, tol=1e-6, seed=42):
"""Nonneg Matrix Factorization via multiplicative updates (Lee & Seung 2001).
Args:
V: (n, m) nonneg data matrix
r: number of components (rank)
n_iter: max iterations
tol: stop if relative improvement < tol
Returns:
W (n, r), H (r, m) with W >= 0, H >= 0, W @ H ≈ V
"""
rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
n, m = V.shape
eps = 1e-10 # numerical floor to prevent 0/0
# Initialize with small positive values (NNDSVD is better but complex)
W = rng.uniform(0, 1, (n, r))
H = rng.uniform(0, 1, (r, m))
prev_err = np.linalg.norm(V - W @ H, 'fro') ** 2
for i in range(n_iter):
# Update H (fix W, optimize H)
H = H * (W.T @ V) / (W.T @ W @ H + eps)
# Update W (fix H, optimize W)
W = W * (V @ H.T) / (W @ H @ H.T + eps)
# Check convergence
err = np.linalg.norm(V - W @ H, 'fro') ** 2
if abs(prev_err - err) / (prev_err + eps) < tol:
print(f"Converged at iteration {i}, error={err:.4f}")
break
prev_err = err
return W, H
# Example: 4x4 topic-affinity matrix, rank 2
V = np.array([[5,4,1,2],[4,5,1,2],[1,1,4,5],[2,2,5,4]], dtype=float)
W, H = nmf_multiplicative(V, r=2)
print(f"Reconstruction error: {np.linalg.norm(V - W@H,'fro')**2:.4f}")

Production Usage (scikit-learn)

from sklearn.decomposition import NMF
import numpy as np
# --- Topic Modeling example: document-term matrix ---
from sklearn.feature_extraction.text import TfidfVectorizer
from sklearn.datasets import fetch_20newsgroups
docs = fetch_20newsgroups(subset='train', categories=['sci.space','rec.sport.hockey'])
tfidf = TfidfVectorizer(max_features=1000, stop_words='english')
V = tfidf.fit_transform(docs.data) # shape (n_docs, n_words), nonneg by design
model = NMF(
n_components=2, # number of topics (rank r)
init='nndsvda', # deterministic NNDSVD init (better than random)
solver='cd', # 'cd' (coord descent) often faster than 'mu' (mult update)
beta_loss='frobenius', # 'frobenius' = squared error; 'kullback-leibler' = KL loss
max_iter=500,
random_state=42,
)
W = model.fit_transform(V) # (n_docs, 2): document weights per topic
H = model.components_ # (2, n_words): topic-word distributions
# Print top words per topic
feature_names = tfidf.get_feature_names_out()
for topic_idx, topic in enumerate(H):
top_words = [feature_names[i] for i in topic.argsort()[-10:][::-1]]
print(f"Topic {topic_idx}: {', '.join(top_words)}")
# ⚠️ Gotcha: V must be strictly nonneg (TF-IDF is fine, raw counts are fine)
# ⚠️ Gotcha: init='nndsvda' is deterministic; 'random' gives different results each run
# ⚠️ Gotcha: n_components is a hyperparameter — use reconstruction error or
# coherence scores to select it, not just visual inspection
⚠️ Common Gotchas

1. Initialization matters: Random init can lead to poor local optima. Use NNDSVD (Nonneg Double SVD) via init='nndsvda' for deterministic, well-conditioned initialization.

2. Scaling: Columns of \(V\) with very different magnitudes dominate the loss. Normalize before factorizing, or use column-stochastic \(V\).

3. Rank selection: No perfect criterion. Common approaches: elbow in reconstruction error vs rank plot, or topic coherence for NLP use cases.

Key Takeaways & When to Use NMF
▼

When to Use NMF

Use NMF when: your data is intrinsically nonneg (images, counts, spectra, audio spectrograms), and you want interpretable parts-based factors. Prime use cases: topic modeling (documents × words), face decomposition (images × pixels), audio source separation (spectrograms), bioinformatics (gene × sample expression).

Use PCA/SVD instead when: your data has negative values, you need exact orthogonal components, or you need a unique globally optimal solution. PCA is faster, globally optimal, and uniquely determined — but factors are not guaranteed to be interpretable or additive.

Use LDA instead when: you are modeling document-topic distributions probabilistically, want a generative model with proper Bayesian semantics, or need per-document topic uncertainty estimates.

Comparison at a Glance

Property NMF PCA / SVD LDA
Data constraint V ≥ 0, W ≥ 0, H ≥ 0 Any real values Count data
Components Additive parts (interpretable) Orthogonal directions (less interpretable) Probabilistic topics
Uniqueness Not unique (scaling) Unique (sign flip only) Not unique (label swap)
Optimality Local minimum (non-convex) Global minimum (convex) Local minimum (EM)
Typical use Images, NLP topics, audio Feature compression, whitening Document topic modeling
🎯 Decision Rule

If your data is nonneg and you want factors you can actually look at and name → NMF. If you just need a compressed representation of arbitrary data → PCA. If you're doing text topic modeling with probabilistic inference → LDA.
