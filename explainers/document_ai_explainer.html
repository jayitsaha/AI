Understanding Document AI
▼

What is Document AI? Document Artificial Intelligence (Document AI) refers to a field of AI focused on enabling computers to understand, process, and extract information from documents. Unlike natural language processing (NLP) which primarily deals with raw text, Document AI must also contend with the visual layout, structure, and often embedded images within documents like invoices, forms, reports, and academic papers.

Traditional approaches relied on Optical Character Recognition (OCR) to convert images to text, followed by standard NLP models. However, this often loses crucial spatial information. The models explored here — LayoutLM, LayoutLMv3, and Donut — represent a paradigm shift towards truly multimodal understanding, integrating visual and textual cues directly into deep learning architectures.

Analogy: Imagine you're handed an invoice. You don't just read the words; you scan for the "Invoice No:" field, identify the "Total:" line, and visually separate the header from the line items. A human uses both the semantic content of the text and its spatial arrangement (e.g., "Invoice No" is usually at the top, the total is usually bold and at the bottom-right). Document AI models aim to replicate this multimodal comprehension.

💡 If you remember one thing

Effective Document AI requires understanding not just what the text says, but also where it is on the page. The spatial layout provides critical context for interpreting document structure and extracting key information.

2
How These Models Work Internally
▼

LayoutLM: Text + 2D Layout Embeddings

The original LayoutLM (2019) extended the BERT architecture to incorporate layout information. It works in two main steps:

  1. OCR Preprocessing: First, an OCR engine extracts text tokens and their corresponding bounding box coordinates \((x_0, y_0, x_1, y_1)\) from the document image.
  2. Multimodal Embedding: For each text token, three types of embeddings are combined:
    • Text Embedding: Standard BERT token embeddings.
    • 1D Position Embedding: Standard BERT sequence position embeddings.
    • 2D Position Embedding: Embeddings derived from the bounding box coordinates. Each coordinate (e.g., \(x_0\), \(y_0\), \(x_1\), \(y_1\)) is mapped to a distinct embedding.
  3. Transformer Encoder: These combined embeddings are fed into a Transformer encoder, allowing the model to learn relationships between text tokens based on both their semantic content and their spatial proximity/arrangement.

Pre-training tasks for LayoutLM include Masked Visual-Language Model (MVLM), where masked tokens are predicted considering their context, and Text-Image Matching (TIM), where the model predicts if a text segment corresponds to a specific image segment.

LayoutLMv3: Unified Text-Image Transformer

LayoutLMv3 (2022) significantly improves upon its predecessors by introducing a unified multimodal Transformer encoder. Instead of separate branches for text and vision, it processes both modalities through a single architecture:

  • Unified Input: The document image is tokenized into visual patches (similar to a Vision Transformer, ViT), and OCR'd text is tokenized into word embeddings. These are concatenated into a single input sequence.
  • No Explicit 2D Position Embeddings: Unlike LayoutLM, LayoutLMv3 relies on absolute position embeddings and relative position bias within the Transformer to capture spatial relationships.
  • Joint Pre-training: It's pre-trained with three tasks:
    • Masked Language Modeling (MLM): Predicts masked text tokens.
    • Masked Image Modeling (MIM): Reconstructs masked visual patches.
    • Word-Patch Alignment (WPA): Predicts whether a text token corresponds to a masked visual patch (or vice-versa), enforcing cross-modal alignment.

This unified approach allows for deeper interaction between text and visual features, leading to state-of-the-art performance on various Document AI benchmarks.

Donut (Document Understanding Transformer): OCR-Free End-to-End

Donut (2021) takes a different, radical approach by completely bypassing the OCR step. It is an OCR-free, end-to-end image-to-text Transformer:

  • Encoder: A Vision Transformer (ViT) directly processes the raw document image pixels.
  • Decoder: An autoregressive Transformer decoder generates structured output (e.g., JSON, Markdown, or free-form text) token by token, conditioned on the encoder's visual features and a task-specific prompt.

Donut is pre-trained on large synthetic document datasets, learning to directly "read" and extract information from images without explicit character recognition. This eliminates the cascading errors often seen when OCR output is noisy or inaccurate, simplifying the pipeline significantly.

🔑 Key Property

The evolution of Document AI models shows a clear trend: from combining text with explicit layout coordinates (LayoutLM), to unifying text and image features within a single encoder (LayoutLMv3), and finally to completely OCR-free, end-to-end image-to-text generation (Donut). Each step aims for richer multimodal understanding and simpler pipelines.

Why It Works — The Math

LayoutLM: Combining Text and Layout Embeddings

For each token \(t_i\) with its bounding box \((x_{0i}, y_{0i}, x_{1i}, y_{1i})\), LayoutLM constructs a multimodal embedding. The general idea is to sum the standard token embedding with learned embeddings for each coordinate:

$$E_{multimodal}(t_i) = E_{token}(t_i) + E_{pos}(i) + E_{x_0}(x_{0i}) + E_{y_0}(y_{0i}) + E_{x_1}(x_{1i}) + E_{y_1}(y_{1i})$$

Here, \(E_{pos}(i)\) is the 1D sequential position embedding, and \(E_{x_0}, \dots, E_{y_1}\) are learned embeddings for each coordinate, often bucketed or normalized to a fixed range. This sum allows the Transformer to attend to tokens based on both their semantic meaning and their precise location and extent on the page.

LayoutLMv3: Unified Multimodal Pre-training Objective

LayoutLMv3's strength comes from its unified architecture and joint pre-training tasks. The overall loss function is a weighted sum of three components:

$$\mathcal{L} = \lambda_{MLM} \mathcal{L}_{MLM} + \lambda_{MIM} \mathcal{L}_{MIM} + \lambda_{WPA} \mathcal{L}_{WPA}$$

Where:

  • \(\mathcal{L}_{MLM}\) is the Masked Language Modeling loss (predicting masked text tokens).
  • \(\mathcal{L}_{MIM}\) is the Masked Image Modeling loss (reconstructing masked visual patches from their context).
  • \(\mathcal{L}_{WPA}\) is the Word-Patch Alignment loss (predicting if a text token and a visual patch are aligned).
This multi-task learning forces the model to learn rich, aligned representations across both text and image modalities simultaneously, making it highly robust to various downstream tasks.

⚠️ Common Pitfall / Challenge

While OCR-free models like Donut offer simplicity and robustness to OCR errors, they require massive amounts of paired image-text data for pre-training, often relying on synthetic document generation. For highly specialized documents with unique fonts or complex layouts not seen during pre-training, an OCR-based approach might still be more adaptable, albeit with the inherent risk of OCR errors.

Key Takeaways
▼

When to choose each model:

  • LayoutLM (and LayoutLMv2): Excellent foundational models when you have reliable OCR output and want to explicitly leverage 2D spatial information alongside text. Good for tasks like document classification, information extraction (NER), and question answering on scanned documents.
  • LayoutLMv3: The current state-of-the-art for many document understanding tasks. Its unified multimodal encoder allows for deeper interaction between text and image features, making it more robust and powerful. Prefer this for most tasks where both text and image are available and you want high accuracy.
  • Donut: Ideal for end-to-end information extraction tasks where OCR errors are a significant concern, or when you want to simplify the pipeline by removing the explicit OCR step. It shines in scenarios requiring direct structured output (e.g., JSON) from document images, such as invoice parsing or form filling.

When NOT to use / Consider alternatives:

  • LayoutLM/v3: If your documents are purely text-based (e.g., plain text files, emails) without meaningful layout, a standard BERT-like model might be sufficient and simpler. If OCR quality is extremely poor and hard to fix, Donut might be a better choice.
  • Donut: If your downstream task requires fine-grained analysis of the raw OCR text (e.g., advanced NLP, semantic search on document content, or tasks that need to operate on the exact text string before any structured extraction), Donut's direct image-to-structured-text approach might be less suitable. Also, Donut's reliance on synthetic data for pre-training means it might struggle with highly unique or niche document layouts not covered during training.
💡 The Future is Multimodal and End-to-End

The trend in Document AI is moving towards models that inherently understand the interplay between text and visuals, reducing reliance on brittle intermediate steps like OCR. Unified encoder-decoder architectures that directly map document images to structured outputs are becoming increasingly powerful and efficient, streamlining complex document processing pipelines.
