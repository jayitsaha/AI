Market Basket Analysis & Recommendation Pre-Filtering

From raw transactions to actionable association rules — and how lift-filtered rules power cheap, scalable recommendation pre-filters.

Intermediate Classical ML Association Rules Complexity: O(2ⁿ) worst, Apriori prunes
1
Understanding the Concept
▾

Imagine you own a grocery store and you print out every receipt from the past year. Market Basket Analysis (MBA) asks: which items tend to land in the same basket together? The output is a set of association rules of the form "customers who buy bread also tend to buy butter." No labels, no training targets — just raw co-occurrence patterns mined from transaction logs.

💡 If you remember one thing

A rule \(\{A\} \!\Rightarrow\! \{B\}\) is interesting only when lift > 1, meaning \(A\) and \(B\) co-occur more than chance. Lift is the ratio of observed joint probability to what independence predicts: \(\text{lift} = P(A \cup B)\,/\,(P(A)\cdot P(B))\).

Real-World Analogy

Think of lift like a doctor checking whether two symptoms co-occur more than random. If 20% of patients have a fever and 30% have a cough, you'd expect 6% to have both by coincidence. If you actually observe 18% — that's a lift of 3×, a strong signal worth investigating. MBA does exactly this for shopping carts.

Recommendation Pre-Filtering

Modern recommender systems (collaborative filtering, matrix factorisation, neural rankers) are expensive to run over millions of items. MBA rules act as a cheap, interpretable pre-filter: given the user's cart, instantly retrieve the top-\(k\) candidate items with lift > threshold, then pass only those to the heavy ranker. No training, no GPUs required for the filter stage.

How It Works Internally
▾

Step 1 — Represent Transactions as a Binary Matrix

Each of the \(N\) transactions is a set of items from a vocabulary of size \(|\mathcal{I}|\). Internally, this is a Boolean matrix \(M \in \{0,1\}^{N \times |\mathcal{I}|}\). The support of an itemset \(X\) is just the column-AND row-sum normalised by \(N\):

$$\text{support}(X) = \frac{|\{t \in \mathcal{T} : X \subseteq t\}|}{N}$$

Step 2 — Apriori Pruning (Anti-Monotonicity)

Naive enumeration of all \(2^{|\mathcal{I}|}\) subsets is impossible for realistic \(|\mathcal{I}|\). The Apriori algorithm exploits a key property: if an itemset is infrequent, all its supersets are infrequent. This allows aggressive level-wise pruning — generate candidate \(k\)-itemsets only from frequent \((k-1)\)-itemsets, shrinking the search space exponentially.

Step 3 — Generate Rules and Score Them

From each frequent itemset \(X\), generate all non-empty splits \(A \cup B = X\), \(A \cap B = \emptyset\). For each candidate rule \(A \Rightarrow B\), compute three metrics:

$$\text{confidence}(A \Rightarrow B) = \frac{\text{support}(A \cup B)}{\text{support}(A)}$$
$$\text{lift}(A \Rightarrow B) = \frac{\text{confidence}(A \Rightarrow B)}{P(B)} = \frac{\text{support}(A \cup B)}{\text{support}(A)\cdot\text{support}(B)}$$
Key Concept — Why Lift, not just Confidence?

Confidence \(\{A\}\!\Rightarrow\!\{B\}\) = 0.8 sounds impressive, but if \(B\) appears in 90% of all baskets (e.g. "bag"), the rule is trivial — you'd buy \(B\) anyway. Lift normalises confidence by \(P(B)\), exposing whether \(A\) genuinely causes more \(B\) purchases than baseline.

Step 4 — Use as a Recommendation Pre-Filter

Build a lookup table keyed by antecedent itemsets. At request time: (1) identify which frequent antecedents match the user's current cart; (2) retrieve consequents sorted by lift; (3) deduplicate and pass top-\(k\) to downstream ranker. Latency is milliseconds (hash lookup) vs. seconds for full model inference over millions of items.

The Math — Numerical Trace
▾

Using our five toy transactions below (T1–T5):

TxIDItems
T1Bread, Butter, Milk
T2Bread, Butter
T3Bread, Eggs
T4Butter, Milk, Jam
T5Bread, Butter, Eggs

Support of individual items (min_support = 0.4, N = 5)

$$\text{sup(Bread)} = 4/5 = 0.80 \quad \text{sup(Butter)} = 4/5 = 0.80$$
$$\text{sup(Milk)} = 2/5 = 0.40 \quad \text{sup(Eggs)} = 2/5 = 0.40$$

Jam (1/5 = 0.20) is pruned — below min_support. Good demonstration of Apriori's pruning.

Frequent 2-itemsets

$$\text{sup}(\{Bread, Butter\}) = \frac{|\{T1,T2,T5\}|}{5} = \frac{3}{5} = 0.60$$

Rule: {Bread} → {Butter}

$$\text{confidence} = \frac{0.60}{0.80} = 0.75$$
$$\text{lift} = \frac{0.60}{0.80 \times 0.80} = \frac{0.60}{0.64} = 0.9375$$
⚠️ Surprise — Lift < 1 here!

Even though confidence is 75%, lift = 0.94 < 1 means Bread and Butter co-occur less than chance (because both are individually very popular at 80%). A lift-only filter would correctly reject this rule. This is the key insight: high confidence alone is insufficient.

Rule: {Bread} → {Eggs} — a genuinely interesting rule

$$\text{sup}(\{Bread, Eggs\}) = 2/5 = 0.40$$
$$\text{confidence} = \frac{0.40}{0.80} = 0.50$$
$$\text{lift} = \frac{0.40}{0.80 \times 0.40} = \frac{0.40}{0.32} = 1.25$$

Lift = 1.25 > 1 — Bread genuinely co-occurs with Eggs 25% more than chance. This rule would pass a lift threshold filter.

Conviction (an alternative to lift)

$$\text{conviction}(A \Rightarrow B) = \frac{1 - \text{support}(B)}{1 - \text{confidence}(A \Rightarrow B)}$$

Conviction = 1 means independence; high conviction means \(B\) is very unlikely without \(A\). For {Bread}→{Eggs}: \((1-0.40)/(1-0.50) = 0.60/0.50 = 1.20\).

Key Takeaways & When to Use
▾
✅ Use MBA When

• You need interpretable, human-readable rules
• Cold-start: no user history available
• Cheap pre-filter before expensive ML models
• Cross-sell placement in e-commerce / retail
• Audit trail required (regulators, merchandisers)

❌ Avoid MBA When

• Item vocabulary > 100k (combinatorial explosion)
• User-personalisation matters (rules are global)
• Temporal dynamics are key (rules are static)
• Sparse transactions (most items appear rarely)
• You need probabilistic uncertainty estimates

Connecting to Recommendation Systems

In a two-stage recommender: Stage 1 (retrieval) uses MBA rules for O(1) candidate generation. Stage 2 (ranking) runs a learned model (BPR, NCF, LightGCN) only over the retrieved candidates. This cuts ranking compute by 99%+ while maintaining recall because lift-filtered rules have high precision for the most obvious co-purchases.
