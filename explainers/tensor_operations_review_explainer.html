Tensor Operations: Contraction & Outer Product

How lower-order tensors combine into higher-order ones — and how summing over shared indices collapses them back
Foundational Linear Algebra Part I · Sec 0.1 Review Track
1
Understanding the Concept
▼

A tensor is a multi-dimensional array that generalizes scalars (0-D), vectors (1-D), and matrices (2-D) to any number of axes (modes). We characterize a tensor by its order (number of axes) and its shape — the size along each axis.

Two fundamental ways to combine tensors are the outer product and contraction. They are, in a precise sense, inverses of each other in terms of rank:

Core Insight

The outer product \(\mathbf{a} \otimes \mathbf{b}\) increases rank — it builds a rank-2 tensor from two rank-1 vectors, encoding every pairwise product. Contraction decreases rank by summing over one (or more) shared indices, converting, for example, a pair of rank-2 tensors into a rank-2 result (matrix multiplication) or a rank-2 tensor into a scalar (trace).

Real-world analogy

Think of the outer product like a multiplication table: given a column of row prices \([1, 2, 3]\) and a row of quantities \([4, 5]\), you fill every cell \((i,j)\) with \(\text{price}_i \times \text{quantity}_j\). Contraction is the reverse: collapse a table by summing along one axis — like computing total revenue per item by summing across customers.

Key Concept

If you remember one thing: outer product = all pairwise products (rank goes up); contraction = sum over a shared index (rank goes down). Matrix multiplication is contraction of one index pair; trace is self-contraction of both indices of a square matrix.

2
How It Works — Formal Definitions
▼

Outer Product (Tensor Product)

Given \(\mathbf{a} \in \mathbb{R}^m\) and \(\mathbf{b} \in \mathbb{R}^n\), the outer product is the \(m \times n\) matrix:

$$(\mathbf{a} \otimes \mathbf{b})_{ij} = a_i \, b_j \qquad \forall\, i \in [m],\, j \in [n]$$

The result has rank at most 1 (it is a rank-1 matrix). More generally, for tensors \(\mathcal{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{I_1 \times \cdots \times I_p}\) and \(\mathcal{B} \in \mathbb{R}^{J_1 \times \cdots \times J_q}\):

$$(\mathcal{A} \otimes \mathcal{B})_{i_1,\ldots,i_p,\, j_1,\ldots,j_q} = \mathcal{A}_{i_1,\ldots,i_p} \cdot \mathcal{B}_{j_1,\ldots,j_q}$$

The result is an order-\((p+q)\) tensor. For example \(\mathbf{a} \in \mathbb{R}^3,\, \mathbf{b} \in \mathbb{R}^2,\, \mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^2\) gives \(\mathbf{a} \otimes \mathbf{b} \otimes \mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 2 \times 2}\).

Tensor Contraction

Contraction sums over one or more pairs of matching indices. For two tensors \(\mathcal{A}\) of order \(p\) and \(\mathcal{B}\) of order \(q\), contracting over the \(k\)-th axis of \(\mathcal{A}\) (size \(K\)) and the \(\ell\)-th axis of \(\mathcal{B}\) (same size \(K\)):

$$\mathcal{C}_{i_1,\ldots,\hat{i}_k,\ldots,i_p,\, j_1,\ldots,\hat{j}_\ell,\ldots,j_q} = \sum_{s=1}^{K} \mathcal{A}_{i_1,\ldots,s,\ldots,i_p} \cdot \mathcal{B}_{j_1,\ldots,s,\ldots,j_q}$$

The hat (\(\hat{\,}\)) denotes the axis that was summed away. The result has order \(p + q - 2\).

Common Confusion

The outer product \(\mathbf{a}\otimes\mathbf{b}\) is not the inner product \(\mathbf{a}^\top\mathbf{b}\). The former produces a matrix (rank up); the latter produces a scalar (full contraction). Both start with two vectors — the difference is whether you sum or not.

The Math — Index Notation & Einstein Summation
▼

Einstein Summation Convention

In Einstein notation, a repeated index in a single term implies summation over that index. This elegantly unifies all tensor contractions:

$$C_{ij} = A_{ik}\,B_{kj} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad (AB)_{ij} = \sum_k A_{ik}\,B_{kj}$$

The repeated index \(k\) (appearing once up, once down, or simply twice) signals contraction. Free indices \(i,j\) remain in the result.

Outer Product in Einstein Notation

Since no index is repeated, there is no summation — every combination is kept:

$$T_{ij} = a_i \, b_j \qquad \text{(outer product, no summation)}$$
$$T_{ijk} = a_i \, b_j \, c_k \qquad \text{(3-way outer product)}$$

Contraction Rules (Rank Arithmetic)

Contracting \(s\) pairs of indices between tensors of orders \(p\) and \(q\):

$$\text{order}(\mathcal{C}) = p + q - 2s$$

Examples with \(p=q=2\) (matrices): \(s=0\) → order-4 tensor (no contraction); \(s=1\) → order-2 (matrix multiply); \(s=2\) → order-0 scalar (full double contraction / Frobenius inner product).

Numerical Trace Example

Let \(A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 & 9 \end{bmatrix}\). The trace is a self-contraction \(\operatorname{tr}(A) = A_{ii} = A_{11}+A_{22}+A_{33} = 1 + 5 + 9 = 15\).

Numerical MatMul Contraction

With \(A\) above and \(B = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \\ 2 & 3 \end{bmatrix}\), the \((0,0)\) entry of \(C = AB\) is:

$$C_{00} = \sum_{k=0}^{2} A_{0k}\,B_{k0} = 1\cdot1 + 2\cdot0 + 3\cdot2 = 7$$
Formula Summary

Outer product: \((\mathbf{a}\otimes\mathbf{b})_{ij} = a_i b_j\); rank goes from \((p)+(q)\) to \((p+q)\).

Single contraction: \(C_{i_1\ldots \hat{k} \ldots} = \sum_k A_{i_1\ldots k\ldots} B_{\ldots k \ldots}\); rank decreases by 2.

Mode-n product: \((\mathcal{T}\times_n M)_{i_1\ldots j\ldots i_N} = \sum_{i_n} \mathcal{T}_{i_1\ldots i_n\ldots i_N}\,M_{j,i_n}\).

Key Takeaways & When to Use
▼

Summary

Outer product (\(\otimes\)) creates a higher-order tensor encoding all pairwise (or multi-way) products — used in attention-score computation, rank-1 decomposition building blocks, and neural network weight initialization strategies.

Contraction (summing over shared indices) is the workhorse of linear algebra: matrix multiplication, trace, inner products, and Tucker/CP tensor decompositions all reduce to contractions. In deep learning, torch.einsum / numpy.einsum let you express arbitrary contractions concisely.

Decision Guide

Need a rank-1 approximation? Use outer products \(\sum_r \mathbf{u}_r \otimes \mathbf{v}_r\) (CP decomposition). Need to collapse a dimension? Contraction is the right tool. Partial contraction? Mode-\(n\) product for Tucker decomposition, attention pattern formation, or batched matmul.
