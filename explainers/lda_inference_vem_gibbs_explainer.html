Understanding LDA Inference
▼

What is this? Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) is a generative model that explains why a document collection looks the way it does: each document mixes a few hidden topics, each topic is a distribution over words. The inference problem is: given only the words we can observe, figure out the hidden topic assignments and topic-word distributions. Crucially, the exact answer requires summing over an exponentially large space — it's mathematically intractable.

Analogy: Imagine a library where books are secretly assembled by mixing chapters from K master "subject encyclopedias." You see only the final books. Inference means reverse-engineering: which master encyclopedia contributed each paragraph? Two strategies exist: (1) Variational EM — guess the answer analytically by solving a simpler approximation; (2) Gibbs sampling — randomly reassign paragraphs one at a time, trusting that after many rounds the distribution of guesses converges to the right answer.

💡 If you remember one thing

The posterior \(p(\theta, \phi, z \mid w)\) is intractable because normalizing over all topic assignments couples every document together. Variational EM replaces it with a factored approximation \(q\) and maximizes the ELBO; collapsed Gibbs integrates out \(\theta\) and \(\phi\) analytically and samples only the discrete assignments \(z\), trading bias for asymptotically exact samples.
