Shallow fusion vs. deep fusion — two ways to let a language model correct the acoustic model's mistakes

1
Understanding LM Integration in ASR
▼

What is it? An ASR system models \(P(Y \mid X)\) — the probability of a word sequence \(Y\) given audio \(X\). The acoustic model (AM) learns this mapping from paired audio-transcript data, which is expensive and limited. A language model (LM), by contrast, is trained on huge amounts of plain text and knows which word sequences are grammatical, idiomatic, and contextually likely — completely independent of what anything sounds like. LM integration is the family of techniques for combining the AM's "does this sound right?" with the LM's "does this read right?" so the decoder picks a transcript that satisfies both.

Analogy: Picture two judges scoring a performance. One judge is deaf but has memorized every script ever written (the LM) — she rates how natural a line of dialogue is. The other judge can only hear, not read (the AM) — he rates how well the sounds match. Neither judge alone is reliable, but averaging (or otherwise combining) their scores usually finds the right answer.

💡 If you remember one thing

Acoustically, "recognize speech" and "wreck a nice beach" are almost identical — the AM alone genuinely cannot tell them apart. The LM breaks the tie because "recognize speech" is overwhelmingly more probable English. Shallow fusion adds the LM's score to the AM's score after the fact; deep fusion blends the LM's internal hidden state into the AM's decoder before the final prediction is made.

2
How Shallow Fusion Works
▼

Score-Level Combination

Shallow fusion keeps the AM and LM as two completely independent, separately-trained, frozen networks. At every step of beam search, each candidate continuation is rescored by simply adding the LM's log-probability, scaled by a tunable weight \(\lambda\):

$$s(y_{1:t}) = s(y_{1:t-1}) + \log P_{AM}(y_t \mid X, y_{

The beam is re-sorted after every step using this combined score, and only the top-\(k\) partial hypotheses survive to the next step. \(\lambda\) is tuned once on a held-out development set and then fixed.

Why "Shallow"?

Nothing about the internal representations is shared — the LM is a black box that only ever hands over a scalar log-probability per token. This is what makes shallow fusion "shallow": the fusion happens at the outermost score layer, not inside either network.

🔑 Key Property

Shallow fusion requires zero retraining of the acoustic model. You can drop in any external LM — a bigger neural LM, a domain-adapted LM, even an n-gram LM — at inference time and immediately get a quality boost. This is why it dominates production ASR systems.

3
How Deep Fusion Works — The Math
▼

Fusing Hidden States, Not Just Scores

Deep fusion (Gulcehre et al., 2015) goes one level deeper. Instead of adding two scalar log-probabilities, it merges the LM decoder's hidden state \(h_t^{LM}\) directly into the AM decoder's hidden state \(h_t^{AM}\), before the softmax ever runs. A small learned gate controls how much of the LM's hidden state leaks through:

$$g_t = \sigma\!\left(v_g^\top h_t^{LM} + b_g\right) \qquad s_t = \mathrm{DNN}\big([\,h_t^{AM}\, ;\, g_t \, h_t^{LM}\,]\big) \qquad P(y_t\mid\cdot) = \mathrm{softmax}(W_o s_t + b_o)$$

The gate \(g_t\) is a learned function of the LM's own hidden state — it can let more or less of the LM's opinion through at each individual decoding step, giving far finer-grained control than one global \(\lambda\).

What Gets Trained

The LM is pretrained on text and then frozen. Only the gate parameters \((v_g, b_g)\) and the small fusion DNN are trained, using paired audio-transcript data, so the acoustic decoder learns exactly how and when to lean on the LM's hidden state.

⚠️ Common Pitfall

Because the gate and fusion DNN are fit on the target domain's paired data, deep fusion is costlier to deploy than shallow fusion, and swapping in a different LM means retraining the fusion layer. Shallow fusion, by contrast, lets you swap LMs for free. A further step, cold fusion (Sriram et al., 2017), trains the acoustic model jointly with a frozen LM from scratch, gating on LM logits rather than hidden states, for even tighter integration.

Key Takeaways
▼

When to use shallow fusion:

  • You want a fast, cheap quality boost with no acoustic-model retraining
  • You need to swap language models freely (domain adaptation, new vocabulary, bigger LM)
  • This is the default choice in almost all production ASR pipelines today

When to consider deep (or cold) fusion:

  • You can afford joint fine-tuning on paired audio-text data
  • You want per-step, learned control over how much the LM influences decoding, rather than one global \(\lambda\)
  • You are optimizing a fixed, closed-domain system rather than one that needs frequent LM swaps
💡 Hierarchy

Shallow fusion (score-level, frozen models, hand-tuned \(\lambda\)) ⊂ Deep fusion (hidden-state-level, learned gate, frozen LM) ⊂ Cold fusion (trained jointly from scratch with the LM fully in the loop). Each step trades deployment simplicity for tighter, more adaptive integration.

# Beam-search step with SHALLOW fusion (score-level rescoring)
def shallow_step(beam, am_logits, lm_logits, lam=0.5):
new_beam = []
for hyp in beam:
for token, am_lp in am_logits(hyp).items():
lm_lp = lm_logits(hyp)[token] # frozen LM, no shared state
score = hyp.score + am_lp + lam * lm_lp
new_beam.append(hyp.extend(token, score))
return top_k(new_beam, k=BEAM_WIDTH)
# Beam-search step with DEEP fusion (hidden-state gating)
def deep_step(beam, am_decoder, lm_decoder, gate_net, out_dnn):
new_beam = []
for hyp in beam:
h_am = am_decoder.step(hyp.am_state)
h_lm = lm_decoder.step(hyp.lm_state) # frozen LM, hidden state exposed
g = sigmoid(gate_net(h_lm)) # learned, per-step gate
fused = out_dnn(concat(h_am, g * h_lm))
logits = output_layer(fused)
for token, lp in log_softmax(logits).items():
new_beam.append(hyp.extend(token, hyp.score + lp))
return top_k(new_beam, k=BEAM_WIDTH)
