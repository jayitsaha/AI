1
Understanding NLI
▼

What is it? Natural Language Inference (NLI), also called Recognizing Textual Entailment (RTE), asks: given two sentences — a premise and a hypothesis — does the hypothesis logically follow? A model outputs one of three labels: Entailment (the hypothesis must be true given the premise), Contradiction (the hypothesis must be false), or Neutral (the premise neither confirms nor denies it).

Analogy: Picture a judge hearing a witness's testimony (the premise) and then evaluating a claim (the hypothesis). If the claim is a direct logical consequence of the testimony, it's "entailed." If it flatly contradicts the testimony, it's a "contradiction." If it's plausible but simply unverifiable from what was said, the judge must rule "neutral."

💡 If you remember one thing

NLI reduces "does the model understand language" to a clean, labelable 3-way classification task — which is why it became the flagship benchmark (SNLI, MultiNLI) and later a universal reformulation for zero-shot text classification.

2
How NLI Systems Work Internally
▼

Two Architecture Families

(1) Independent (Siamese) encoding. Encode the premise and hypothesis separately into fixed vectors \(u\) and \(v\) with a shared encoder (a BiLSTM in InferSent; a simple mean-of-word-embeddings in the classic baseline). Then combine them with the matching heuristic \(m = [u;\,v;\,|u-v|;\,u\odot v]\), introduced by Bowman et al. (2015) and popularized by InferSent (Conneau et al., 2017). A linear layer / small MLP maps \(m\) to 3 logits.

(2) Cross / joint encoding. Instead of compressing each sentence independently, let every hypothesis token attend to every premise token before pooling. Decomposable Attention (Parikh et al., 2016) and ESIM (Chen et al., 2017) build a soft-alignment matrix from token-level similarity, then compose the aligned representations. Modern systems (BERT, RoBERTa) skip explicit alignment entirely: they jointly encode [CLS] premise [SEP] hypothesis [SEP] with self-attention, letting attention heads learn alignment implicitly, and classify from the pooled [CLS] vector.

🔑 Design Decision: why subtract AND multiply?

\(|u-v|\) is symmetric and grows large whenever the vectors disagree in any direction — a good contradiction detector. \(u\odot v\) is large and positive exactly when both vectors agree in sign and magnitude on the same dimensions — a good entailment detector. Handing the classifier these pre-computed features (instead of just \([u;v]\)) makes even a linear classifier a surprisingly strong baseline.

3
Why It Works — The Math
▼

From Encodings to a Label

Encode both sentences, then form the matching vector:

$$u = \text{Enc}(\text{premise}), \quad v = \text{Enc}(\text{hypothesis})$$
$$m = [\,u \,;\, v \,;\, |u-v| \,;\, u \odot v\,] \in \mathbb{R}^{4d}$$

A linear classifier maps \(m\) to logits, and softmax turns logits into a probability over the 3 classes:

$$z = Wm + b \in \mathbb{R}^3, \qquad p_c = \frac{e^{z_c}}{\sum_{c'=1}^{3} e^{z_{c'}}}$$

Training minimizes cross-entropy against the gold label \(y\):

$$\mathcal{L} = -\log p_y, \qquad \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial z_c} = p_c - \mathbb{1}[c=y]$$

The alignment view uses cosine similarity between token vectors \(w_i\) (premise) and \(w_j\) (hypothesis) to build a soft-alignment matrix, which ESIM/decomposable-attention pool into a richer representation than a single averaged vector:

$$\cos(w_i, w_j) = \frac{w_i \cdot w_j}{\|w_i\|\,\|w_j\|}$$
⚠️ Common Pitfall — Annotation Artifacts

Gururangan et al. (2018) showed a classifier that looks only at the hypothesis (ignoring the premise entirely) reaches ~67% accuracy on SNLI, versus 33% chance. Annotators used systematic lexical cues — negation words → contradiction, vague hedges → neutral, hypernyms → entailment — while writing hypotheses. High SNLI accuracy alone does not prove real entailment reasoning, which motivated harder diagnostics like HANS and adversarial NLI.

Key Takeaways
▼

The datasets: SNLI (Bowman et al., 2015) — 570k pairs, premises are Flickr30k image captions, single genre. MultiNLI (Williams et al., 2018) — 433k pairs across 10 genres (fiction, telephone speech, government reports, ...) with separate "matched" and "mismatched" dev/test splits to explicitly probe cross-genre generalization.

When to use NLI:

  • Benchmarking sentence-pair reasoning and general NLU (GLUE / SuperGLUE include NLI tasks)
  • Pretraining universal sentence encoders (InferSent trains on SNLI, transfers to unrelated tasks)
  • Reformulating zero-shot classification as NLI: "This text is about sports" becomes a hypothesis to test for entailment (Yin et al., 2019)

When to be cautious: don't treat high SNLI/MultiNLI accuracy as proof of robust reasoning — lexical-overlap heuristics (HANS) and hypothesis-only shortcuts can inflate scores without real inference.

💡 Hierarchy

Bag-of-embeddings (fast, weak) ⊂ Siamese BiLSTM + matching vector (InferSent) ⊂ Cross-attention (ESIM, Decomposable Attention) ⊂ Joint Transformer encoding (BERT/RoBERTa on concatenated pairs) — each stage lets premise and hypothesis interact more richly before the final decision.

Code Trace
▼
import numpy as np
# Toy 4-D word embeddings: [subject, activity, object, place]
vec = {'man':[.90,.10,.00,.20], 'playing':[.10,.90,.20,.00],
'guitar':[.00,.70,.90,.10], 'park':[.20,.10,.00,.90], ...}
premise = ['man', 'playing', 'guitar', 'park']
hypothesis = ['person', 'playing', 'musical', 'instrument', 'outside']
def encode(tokens):
return np.mean([vec[t] for t in tokens], axis=0) # bag-of-embeddings
u, v = encode(premise), encode(hypothesis)
# Cross-sentence token alignment (cosine similarity matrix)
align = np.array([[cos(vec[p], vec[h]) for h in hypothesis] for p in premise])
# Bowman/InferSent matching vector
diff = np.abs(u - v)
prod = u * v
m = np.concatenate([u, v, diff, prod]) # shape (4d,)
# Linear classifier -> 3 logits -> softmax
z = W @ m + b # z = [z_entail, z_neutral, z_contra]
p = np.exp(z - z.max()) / np.exp(z - z.max()).sum() # softmax, numerically stable
labels = ['entailment', 'neutral', 'contradiction']
print(labels[p.argmax()], p)
