Gauss-Markov Theorem (BLUE)

Why OLS is the Best Linear Unbiased Estimator — and the variance comparison proof
Intermediate Classical ML Linear Regression Core Theorem
1
Understanding the Concept

Imagine you're trying to measure something — like the effect of study hours on exam scores — using noisy data. There are infinitely many ways to estimate the relationship (different lines you could draw). The Gauss-Markov theorem tells you: if your noise obeys a few reasonable rules, then Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) gives you the most accurate estimate possible, among all estimators that are both linear and unbiased.

Key Concept

BLUE = Best Linear Unbiased Estimator. "Best" means lowest variance. "Linear" means the estimate is a linear function of \(y\). "Unbiased" means it's correct on average.

Real-world analogy: Think of archery. "Unbiased" means your arrows cluster around the bullseye on average (no systematic drift). "Minimum variance" means your cluster is as tight as possible. OLS is the tightest unbiased archer in the linear class — no other linear, unbiased strategy can outshoot it.

💡 If You Remember One Thing

Under the five GM assumptions, OLS = \((X^\top X)^{-1} X^\top y\) achieves the smallest variance for any coefficient, beating every other linear unbiased estimator — proven by showing the variance difference is a positive-semi-definite matrix.

2
The Five Gauss-Markov Assumptions

The theorem holds only when all five conditions are satisfied. Each has a precise meaning:

AssumptionFormal statementPlain language
GM1 Linearity\(y = X\beta + \varepsilon\)The true model is linear in parameters.
GM2 Strict exogeneity\(\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon \mid X] = 0\)Errors have zero conditional mean — no omitted variable bias.
GM3 No perfect multicollinearity\(\operatorname{rank}(X) = k\)\(X\) has full column rank; no column is a linear combo of others.
GM4 Homoscedasticity\(\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_i \mid X) = \sigma^2\)Every error has the same (unknown) variance.
GM5 No autocorrelation\(\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon_i, \varepsilon_j \mid X) = 0,\ i \ne j\)Errors are uncorrelated with each other.

Assumptions GM4 + GM5 together mean: \(\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon \mid X) = \sigma^2 I_n\). Note that normality is not required — the theorem is purely about first and second moments.

⚠️ Common Misconception

Gauss-Markov does NOT require normal errors. Normality is needed for exact t/F distributions in small samples, but not for BLUE. OLS is still BLUE with any error distribution satisfying GM1–GM5.

3
The Proof: Variance Comparison Argument

The central proof strategy: take any alternative linear unbiased estimator \(\tilde{\beta}\), write it as \(\tilde{\beta} = (C + D)y\) where \(C = (X^\top X)^{-1} X^\top\) is OLS, then show \(\operatorname{Var}(\tilde{\beta}) - \operatorname{Var}(\hat{\beta}_{OLS}) \succeq 0\) (positive semi-definite).

Step-by-Step Derivation

Step 1: Any linear estimator can be written as \(\tilde{\beta} = Ay\) for some matrix \(A\). Write \(A = C + D\) where \(C = (X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top\).

$$\tilde{\beta} = (C + D)y = Cy + Dy = \hat{\beta}_{OLS} + Dy$$

Step 2: Unbiasedness constraint. We need \(\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\beta}] = \beta\):

$$\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\beta}] = (C+D)X\beta = CX\beta + DX\beta = \beta + DX\beta$$

For this to equal \(\beta\) for all \(\beta\), we need \(DX = 0\).

Step 3: Compute the variance of \(\tilde{\beta}\):

$$\operatorname{Var}(\tilde{\beta}) = (C+D)\operatorname{Var}(y)(C+D)^\top = \sigma^2(C+D)(C+D)^\top$$
$$= \sigma^2(CC^\top + CD^\top + DC^\top + DD^\top)$$

Step 4: Use \(DX = 0\) to simplify cross-terms. Note \(C = (X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top\), so \(CX = I\). Therefore \(DC^\top = DX(X^\top X)^{-T} = 0\). Similarly \(CD^\top = (DC^\top)^\top = 0\).

$$\operatorname{Var}(\tilde{\beta}) = \sigma^2(CC^\top + DD^\top) = \operatorname{Var}(\hat{\beta}_{OLS}) + \sigma^2 DD^\top$$

Step 5: The key conclusion:

$$\operatorname{Var}(\tilde{\beta}) - \operatorname{Var}(\hat{\beta}_{OLS}) = \sigma^2 DD^\top \succeq 0$$
✅ Why DD⊤ ≥ 0 (PSD)

For any matrix \(D\) and any vector \(v\): \(v^\top (DD^\top) v = \|D^\top v\|_2^2 \geq 0\). So \(DD^\top\) is always positive semi-definite. Equality holds iff \(D = 0\) (i.e., \(\tilde{\beta} = \hat{\beta}_{OLS}\)).

Numerical Trace: 2×2 Example

Let \(n=3\), \(k=2\), with design matrix and response:

$$X = \begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \\ 1 & 3\end{bmatrix}, \quad y = \begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 4 \\ 5\end{bmatrix}$$

Step 1: Compute \(X^\top X\) and its inverse:

$$X^\top X = \begin{bmatrix}3 & 6 \\ 6 & 14\end{bmatrix}, \quad (X^\top X)^{-1} = \frac{1}{6}\begin{bmatrix}14 & -6 \\ -6 & 3\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}7/3 & -1 \\ -1 & 1/2\end{bmatrix}$$

Step 2: OLS variance matrix (up to \(\sigma^2\)):

$$\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\beta}_{OLS}) = \sigma^2(X^\top X)^{-1} = \sigma^2\begin{bmatrix}7/3 & -1 \\ -1 & 1/2\end{bmatrix}$$

Step 3: Suppose an alternative estimator has \(D = \begin{bmatrix}0.1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0.05\end{bmatrix}X^\perp\) satisfying \(DX=0\). Then:

$$\sigma^2 DD^\top = \sigma^2 \begin{bmatrix}0.01 & 0 \\ 0 & 0.0025\end{bmatrix} \succeq 0$$

The diagonal shows that \(\operatorname{Var}(\tilde{\beta}_1) = \sigma^2(7/3 + 0.01)\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(\tilde{\beta}_2) = \sigma^2(0.5 + 0.0025)\) — both strictly larger than OLS.

Key Numbers

OLS variance for \(\beta_2\): \(\sigma^2 \cdot 0.5\). Alternative variance: \(\sigma^2 \cdot 0.5025\). The "extra variance" is \(\sigma^2 \cdot 0.0025 = \sigma^2 (DD^\top)_{22} \geq 0\). OLS always wins or ties.

6
Key Takeaways

When GM assumptions hold: Use OLS — it's provably optimal in the linear unbiased class. No other linear unbiased method can reduce variance further.

When assumptions break:

  • Heteroscedasticity (GM4 violated) → GLS / WLS is BLUE
  • Autocorrelation (GM5 violated) → GLS is BLUE
  • Endogeneity (GM2 violated) → IV / 2SLS
  • Multicollinearity (GM3 near-violated) → Ridge (biased but lower MSE)
⚠️ BLUE ≠ Best Overall

BLUE restricts to linear unbiased estimators. Biased estimators (Ridge, Lasso) or nonlinear estimators can have lower MSE when assumptions are weakly violated. Gauss-Markov is a claim within a restricted class.
