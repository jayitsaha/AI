BOHB — Bayesian Optimization + HyperBand

Combining early-stopping efficiency with principled probabilistic search for hyperparameter optimisation

Advanced HPO · Classical ML Part III O(log B · n) configs
1
Understanding BOHB

Training a neural network involves choosing dozens of settings before you even start — learning rate, batch size, dropout rate, number of layers. Trying every combination is impossibly expensive. BOHB solves this with two interlocking ideas.

The first idea (HyperBand): don't run bad configurations to completion. Give each candidate a tiny budget (e.g. 1 epoch). Keep only the top fraction. Give survivors more budget. Repeat. This is like a sports tournament — most players are eliminated in early rounds, saving time for meaningful matches later.

The second idea (Bayesian Optimisation with TPE): instead of picking candidates randomly, build a probabilistic model of "which hyperparameter values tend to score well?" and sample from the promising region. The model is a Tree-structured Parzen Estimator — it fits two density estimates: one over the good configs and one over the bad, then proposes configs where the good/bad ratio is highest.

💡 The One-Liner

BOHB = HyperBand's tournament pruning (fast early kill of bad configs) + TPE's intelligent sampling (propose configs from a learned probability model instead of random). You get speed and quality.

Real-world analogy: Imagine a talent show. Random search is picking contestants blindly from the crowd. HyperBand kicks out bad acts after 30 seconds instead of letting them perform for an hour. BOHB also learns from previous auditions — if tall singers in a certain key tend to win, it auditions more of them next round.

How BOHB Works Internally

HyperBand inner loop. A bracket starts with \(n\) configs at minimum budget \(b_{\min}\). After evaluation, the top \(\lfloor n/\eta \rfloor\) proceed to \(\eta \cdot b_{\min}\) budget. This repeats until one config reaches maximum budget \(b_{\max}\). The total work across a bracket is bounded regardless of how many configs it starts with.

$$s_{\max} = \lfloor \log_\eta(b_{\max}/b_{\min}) \rfloor$$
$$n_i = \left\lceil \frac{s_{\max}+1}{\eta^{s_{\max}-i}} \right\rceil, \quad b_i = b_{\max} \cdot \eta^{i-s_{\max}}$$

where \(i\) is the rung index (0 = cheapest). Typical values: \(\eta = 3\), \(b_{\min} = 1\) epoch, \(b_{\max} = 81\) epochs → 5 rungs (1, 3, 9, 27, 81).

TPE Bayesian model. After enough observations accumulate at a given budget level, BOHB switches from random sampling to TPE. Split all evaluated configs into the best \(\gamma\) fraction (call it set \(\mathcal{G}\)) and the rest (\(\mathcal{B}\)). Fit kernel density estimates:

$$\ell(\lambda) = p(\lambda \mid y \le y_\gamma), \quad g(\lambda) = p(\lambda \mid y > y_\gamma)$$

The acquisition function is the ratio \(\ell(\lambda)/g(\lambda)\). Maximising this ratio finds hyperparameter values that are probable among good runs and improbable among bad runs.

🔑 Key Design Decision

BOHB fits one TPE model per budget level, not a single global model. This avoids contaminating the model with cheap-budget observations that don't generalise to high-budget performance. Configs sampled for a new bracket come from the highest budget rung that has ≥ N_min observations.

⚠️ Common Confusion

BOHB is not Bayesian Optimisation followed by HyperBand. They are interleaved: BO generates candidates, HyperBand evaluates and prunes them, BO then updates its model with the results of each rung — including partially-trained results.

The Mathematics

HyperBand budget analysis. In bracket \(s\), the total compute spent is bounded:

$$B_s = \sum_{i=0}^{s} \underbrace{n_i}_{\text{configs at rung }i} \cdot b_i \leq (s_{\max}+1) \cdot b_{\max}$$

This is the elegant guarantee: each bracket uses at most \((s_{\max}+1) \cdot b_{\max}\) compute, regardless of how many configs it started with. HyperBand runs \(s_{\max}+1\) brackets in parallel (one per aggressiveness level), so total budget is \(\mathcal{O}((s_{\max}+1)^2 \cdot b_{\max})\).

TPE acquisition derivation. We want \(\lambda^* = \underset{\lambda}{\operatorname{argmax}}\; \mathbb{E}[\text{improvement}]\). Under the TPE model:

$$\mathbb{E}[\text{improvement} \mid \lambda] \propto \frac{\ell(\lambda)}{g(\lambda)}$$

Proof sketch: Expected improvement \(\mathbb{E}[I(\lambda)]\) where \(I = \max(0, y^* - f(\lambda))\) decomposes via Bayes as:

$$\mathbb{E}[I(\lambda)] = \int_{-\infty}^{y^*} (y^* - y)\, p(y \mid \lambda)\, dy$$

Under the split \(p(y \mid \lambda) = \ell(\lambda) \cdot p(y \mid y \le y^*)\gamma^{-1}\) for \(y \le y^*\), maximising this integral reduces to maximising \(\ell(\lambda)/g(\lambda)\).

KDE construction. For each hyperparameter \(\lambda_k\) (treated independently — the "tree" in TPE), fit separate KDEs for \(\mathcal{G}\) and \(\mathcal{B}\). For continuous params use Gaussian kernels; for categoricals use Aitchison–Aitken smoothed categoricals. Bandwidth via Silverman's rule: \(h = 1.06 \hat\sigma n^{-1/5}\).

📐 KDE Bandwidth

$$h = 1.06 \cdot \hat{\sigma} \cdot n^{-1/5}$$ where \(\hat\sigma\) is the sample std of observations in \(\mathcal{G}\) or \(\mathcal{B}\), and \(n\) is the count. BOHB uses a magic number of \(N_{\min}=2(d+1)\) before switching from random to TPE, where \(d\) is the number of hyperparameters.

🎯 Decision Guide

Use BOHB when: you have a neural network, continuous HPO space, moderate parallelism, and ≥20 trials to amortise the model. Use ASHA when: you have hundreds of workers and purely async execution matters more than sample quality. Use GP-BO when: each evaluation takes hours (not minutes) and you can only afford ~50 trials.

6
Key Takeaways

BOHB is the default recommendation for neural network HPO in most ML research toolkits (Auto-PyTorch, SMAC3, Ray Tune). Its core innovation is recognising that early-stopping and intelligent sampling are complementary, not competing, ideas.

  • HyperBand gives \(\mathcal{O}(\log B)\) speedup over running configs to full budget
  • TPE gives \(\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{T})\) regret vs. random search's \(\mathcal{O}(T)\) in practice
  • The combination achieves near-GP quality at HyperBand speed
  • Works best with ≥ 10 parallel workers; degrades gracefully to sequential
⚠️ Limitations

BOHB assumes monotonicity: configs that perform well at low budget tend to rank similarly at high budget. If this doesn't hold (e.g., some architectures need many epochs to warm up), the early-stopping can hurt. Also, the TPE independence assumption breaks for strongly correlated hyperparameters — consider SMAC (GP-based) instead.
