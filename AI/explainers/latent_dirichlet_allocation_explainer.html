Latent Dirichlet Allocation

A generative story for how documents are "written": pick a topic mixture, then for every word, pick a topic and pick a word from that topic — Dirichlet priors all the way down.

Advanced Probabilistic ML Bayesian · Generative Topic Modeling
1
Understanding the Concept
▾

LDA reads a corpus of documents and reverse-engineers a small set of latent topics — without any labels — by assuming every document was secretly generated by a simple two-stage random process. First, roll a biased die to decide the document's topic mixture (e.g. 70% sports, 30% finance). Then, for every single word slot, roll that die to pick a topic, and roll a second (topic-specific) die to pick an actual word.

Think of a cookbook where every recipe is secretly a blend of a few "flavor profiles" (spicy, sweet, savory), and each profile is itself a fixed recipe of ingredients. You never see the flavor labels — only the ingredient list. LDA is the detective that works backward from ingredient lists across the whole cookbook to recover both the flavor recipes and how much of each flavor is in every dish.

Insight

A document isn't assigned to one topic — it's a probabilistic blend of all topics. This "mixed membership" is what separates LDA from hard clustering (like k-means on documents).

2
How It Works Internally
▾

Fix the number of topics \(K\) in advance. For every topic \(k\), draw a word distribution \(\phi_k \sim \operatorname{Dir}(\beta)\) — a vector over the vocabulary that says how likely each word is under that topic. Then, for every document \(d\), draw a topic-mixture \(\theta_d \sim \operatorname{Dir}(\alpha)\). For every word position \(n\) in that document: draw a topic \(z_{d,n} \sim \operatorname{Cat}(\theta_d)\), then draw the observed word \(w_{d,n} \sim \operatorname{Cat}(\phi_{z_{d,n}})\).

Only the words \(w_{d,n}\) are ever observed. The topic mixtures \(\theta_d\), the topic-word tables \(\phi_k\), and the per-token topic labels \(z_{d,n}\) are all latent — inference means running this story backward: given only the words, recover the most plausible \(\theta\), \(\phi\), and \(z\) that could have generated them. This is done with either mean-field variational inference (fast, approximate, optimizes a lower bound) or collapsed Gibbs sampling (simple to implement, samples topic assignments directly using Dirichlet-Multinomial conjugacy).

Key Concept

Using Dirichlet priors on both \(\theta\) and \(\phi\) is the crucial design choice: Dirichlet is conjugate to the categorical/multinomial likelihood, so the posterior stays tractable — this is exactly what makes fast approximate inference possible.

3
Why It Works
▾

Bag-of-words models treat documents as exchangeable — word order inside a document doesn't matter, and documents themselves are exchangeable within the corpus. De Finetti's theorem guarantees that any such exchangeable sequence can be represented as a mixture over some latent parameter drawn from a fixed distribution — that latent parameter is exactly \(\theta_d\), and its distribution is the Dirichlet prior. This is the formal reason LDA must look like a hierarchical mixture model.

The Dirichlet prior also acts as automatic smoothing: without it (as in pLSA), a topic that never co-occurred with a word in training gets probability exactly zero for that word, which is often wrong and brittle. The Dirichlet's pseudo-counts (\(\alpha\), \(\beta\)) guarantee every word/topic combination keeps some nonzero probability mass.

Formula

Posterior point estimates after fitting: \(\hat\theta_{d,k} = \dfrac{n_{d,k} + \alpha_k}{N_d + \sum_k \alpha_k}\),   \(\hat\phi_{k,w} = \dfrac{n_{k,w} + \beta_w}{n_k + \sum_v \beta_v}\) — Dirichlet posterior means, i.e. observed counts smoothed by the prior pseudo-counts.

Key Takeaways
▾
  • LDA is a fully generative, Bayesian model: every document is a random mixture of topics, every topic is a random mixture of words, both drawn from Dirichlet priors.
  • It fixed pLSA's core flaw — no proper prior over document-topic mixtures — by making the parameter count independent of corpus size and giving a principled way to handle unseen documents.
  • Exact inference is intractable (coupling of continuous \(\theta,\phi\) and discrete \(z\)); use mean-field variational EM or collapsed Gibbs sampling in practice.
  • Use it for interpretable, sparse topic discovery on long-form text; avoid it for short texts or when word order/semantics matter — prefer embedding-based neural topic models there.
  • Don't confuse it with Linear Discriminant Analysis — same acronym, unrelated: one is unsupervised generative topic modeling, the other is supervised classification-oriented dimensionality reduction.
