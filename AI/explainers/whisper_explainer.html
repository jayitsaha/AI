One encoder-decoder Transformer, 680,000 hours of noisy web audio, and task tokens instead of task-specific heads
Advanced Speech / Audio Transformer O(n²) attention
1
Understanding Whisper
▼

What is it? Whisper is an audio-in, text-out Transformer trained by OpenAI on 680,000 hours of audio paired with whatever transcripts existed for it on the internet — subtitles, auto-captions, translations, all of it, largely unfiltered by hand. Instead of a speech-recognition-only model, Whisper is trained as one giant multitask system: the same weights transcribe English, translate 96 other languages into English, detect what language is being spoken, and predict word-level timestamps — all selected by special tokens fed into the decoder, not by separate model heads.

Analogy: Think of a single multilingual court stenographer who has sat in on a million hours of speeches, phone calls, lectures, and arguments in noisy rooms. Nobody carefully corrected every word she wrote — she just absorbed the patterns from sheer volume and repetition. Now you can hand her any recording and a sticky note that says "transcribe in French" or "translate to English," and she adapts on the spot, no retraining required.

💡 If you remember one thing

Whisper turns speech recognition into sequence-to-sequence prediction with a text prompt: log-mel spectrogram frames go into a Transformer encoder, and a Transformer decoder autoregressively emits text tokens conditioned on both the audio and a prefix of special "task tokens" like \(\texttt{<|en|>}\), \(\texttt{<|transcribe|>}\), \(\texttt{<|translate|>}\). Scale of weak, diverse supervision — not architectural novelty — is the core contribution.

How Whisper Works Internally
▼

From Waveform to Log-Mel Spectrogram

Raw audio is resampled to 16 kHz and chunked into 30-second windows. Each chunk is converted via a Short-Time Fourier Transform (25 ms windows, 10 ms hop) into an 80-channel log-mel spectrogram — a compact, perceptually-motivated time-frequency picture of the sound, shaped roughly \(80 \times 3000\) frames. This is the ONLY audio feature engineering step; everything after it is a plain Transformer.

The Encoder: Two Conv Layers, Then Pure Self-Attention

The log-mel input first passes through two 1-D convolutions (stride 2 on the second) with GELU activations, downsampling time resolution by 2× and projecting into the model's hidden dimension. Sinusoidal positional embeddings are added, and the result feeds a stack of standard pre-norm Transformer encoder blocks — bidirectional self-attention, since the whole audio chunk is visible at once. Every encoder position can attend to every other position: early frames "hear" later context and vice versa.

The Decoder: Task Tokens Steer a Shared Model

The decoder is a causal Transformer LM over a ~51,864-token BPE vocabulary. Instead of separate models per task, Whisper prepends a short sequence of special control tokens to the target text at training time: \(\texttt{<|sot|>}\) (start of transcript), a language tag like \(\texttt{<|en|>}\), a task tag \(\texttt{<|transcribe|>}\) or \(\texttt{<|translate|>}\), and optionally \(\texttt{<|notimestamps|>}\). Each decoder step performs causal self-attention over tokens generated so far, then cross-attention into the encoder's audio memory, then a feed-forward block — exactly the original "Attention Is All You Need" decoder, with the audio standing in for the source sentence in machine translation.

Design Decision: Why Multitask Tokens Instead of Separate Heads?

An alternative would be training four separate models (ASR, translation, language ID, timestamp prediction). Whisper instead unifies all of them into one decoder vocabulary and lets the prompt select behavior. This means the acoustic encoder is shared and improves from every task simultaneously, and new capabilities (e.g. voice activity detection) are added by inventing a new special token rather than a new architecture.

🔑 Key Property

Whisper has zero task-specific parameters. The entire behavioral difference between "transcribe this Japanese audio" and "translate this Japanese audio to English" is a single token swapped in the decoder prompt — \(\texttt{<|transcribe|>}\) vs \(\texttt{<|translate|>}\) — conditioning the same weights.

Why It Works — The Math
▼

Log-Mel Feature Extraction

For a windowed audio frame, the power spectrum \(|X(f)|^2\) is projected through a mel filterbank \(M\) and log-compressed:

$$\text{logmel} = \log\big(M \cdot |\text{STFT}(x)|^2 + \epsilon\big)$$

The mel scale compresses high frequencies (where human pitch discrimination is coarse) and expands low frequencies — a fixed, differentiable-free preprocessing step, not a learned one.

Self-Attention (Encoder) and Cross-Attention (Decoder)

Both use the same scaled dot-product form. Given queries \(Q\), keys \(K\), values \(V\):

$$\text{Attention}(Q,K,V) = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V$$

In the encoder, \(Q, K, V\) all come from audio frame representations — every frame attends to every frame (no mask). In decoder cross-attention, \(Q\) comes from the partially-generated text while \(K, V\) come from the encoder's final audio representations — this is exactly how the decoder "listens" to the audio at every generation step.

Autoregressive Factorization & Training Objective

Whisper maximizes the log-probability of the reference transcript (with control tokens prepended) under teacher forcing:

$$\mathcal{L} = -\sum_{t=1}^{T} \log P(y_t \mid y_{

where \(y_{}\) should shift the output distribution toward English words regardless of source language.

Next-Token Distribution

At inference, each step turns decoder hidden state \(h_t\) into logits via the (tied) output embedding matrix \(W_{emb}\), then a softmax:

$$P(y_t = v \mid \cdot) = \frac{\exp(h_t^\top w_v)}{\sum_{v'} \exp(h_t^\top w_{v'})}$$

Greedy decoding picks \(\arg\max_v P(y_t=v)\); Whisper's released inference code instead uses beam search plus temperature-fallback and heuristics (compression-ratio and no-speech thresholds) to suppress hallucinated repetition loops — a practical patch for a purely likelihood-trained model with no explicit "I don't know" mechanism.

⚠️ Common Pitfall

Whisper is trained with plain cross-entropy on noisy weak labels, not with a WER-aware or CTC-alignment loss. It has no explicit mechanism preventing hallucination — on silence or non-speech audio it can still confidently emit fluent, wrong text, because the objective only ever rewards "predict a plausible next token," never "abstain."

Key Takeaways
▼

When Whisper shines:

  • Zero-shot robustness to accents, background noise, and technical vocabulary, thanks to sheer diversity of the 680k-hour training mix
  • One checkpoint handles transcription, X→English translation, and language ID for 96+ languages
  • No fine-tuning needed for most out-of-domain audio — it already generalizes broadly

When NOT to reach for it as-is:

  • Low-latency streaming ASR — Whisper decodes in 30-second chunks, not natively streaming
  • Domains needing guaranteed low hallucination (e.g. legal/medical transcripts) without human review
  • Extremely low-resource languages barely represented in the weakly-labeled web data
💡 Hierarchy

Specialist ASR (hand-curated data, task-specific heads) ⊂ Wav2Vec2/HuBERT-style self-supervised + fine-tune (learn representations, then adapt) ⊂ Whisper (weak supervision at massive scale, one model, prompt-selected multitask behavior, no fine-tuning required).

import whisper
model = whisper.load_model("large-v3")
# 1. Load + pad/trim audio to 30s, compute 80-channel log-mel
audio = whisper.load_audio("call.wav")
audio = whisper.pad_or_trim(audio)
mel = whisper.log_mel_spectrogram(audio).to(model.device)
# 2. Encoder: conv stem + positional emb + self-attn blocks
audio_features = model.encoder(mel.unsqueeze(0))
# audio_features.shape == (1, n_audio_ctx, n_state)
# 3. Build the decoder prompt from special tokens
tokenizer = whisper.tokenizer.get_tokenizer(multilingual=True)
prompt = [tokenizer.sot, tokenizer.language_token,
tokenizer.transcribe, tokenizer.no_timestamps]
# 4. Autoregressive decode: causal self-attn + cross-attn per step
tokens = torch.tensor([prompt])
for _ in range(max_len):
logits = model.decoder(tokens, audio_features)
next_token = logits[:, -1].argmax(dim=-1) # greedy
tokens = torch.cat([tokens, next_token[:, None]], dim=-1)
if next_token == tokenizer.eot: break
# High-level convenience wrapper does all of the above:
result = model.transcribe("call.wav", task="transcribe", language="en")
print(result["text"])
