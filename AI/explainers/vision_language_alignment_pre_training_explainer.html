Vision-Language Alignment Pre-training

Teaching a connector to speak the LLM's language before teaching the model to follow instructions
Advanced Multimodal LLM Stage 1 of 2
1
Understanding Vision-Language Alignment Pre-training
▼

What is it? A model like LLaVA or BLIP-2 is bolted together from three pieces: a vision encoder that already knows how to "see" (e.g. a CLIP ViT), a language model that already knows how to "talk" (e.g. Vicuna or LLaMA), and a small connector module sitting between them that starts out knowing nothing. Vision-language alignment pre-training is the first training stage, and its only job is to teach that connector to turn "what the vision encoder sees" into a vector that looks — to the frozen language model — just like one of its own word embeddings.

Analogy: Imagine hiring a translator to sit between a world-class photographer (the vision encoder) and a brilliant novelist (the LLM) who don't speak each other's language. Instead of sending photographer and novelist both back to school together — risking that the novelist's prose degrades while learning to "see" — you freeze both experts exactly as they are and train only the translator, showing it millions of photo-caption pairs until its translations are fluent enough that the novelist can't tell a translated photo-description from a native sentence.

Summary: Freeze the vision encoder and the LLM; train only a lightweight connector (a linear layer, an MLP, or an attention-based resampler) on image-caption pairs with a captioning loss, so visual features land inside the LLM's existing token-embedding space before any instruction-following behavior is ever taught.

💡 If you remember one thing

Stage-1 alignment is regression, not language learning: the connector's only job is $$h_v = f_\phi(v)$$ such that \(h_v\) lands inside the region of \(\mathbb{R}^{d}\) the frozen LLM already treats as meaningful tokens — nothing about "vision" or "language" is learned here, only a mapping between two fixed, pretrained spaces.

2
How It Works Internally
▼

The Three Pieces — Two Frozen, One Trainable

A frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 turns an image into a grid of patch embeddings (e.g. 576 patches of dimension \(d_v=1024\)). The connector \(f_\phi\) maps each patch embedding into the LLM's hidden size (e.g. \(d_{llm}=4096\)) — this can be as simple as one linear layer \(W v + b\), a 2-layer MLP with GELU (LLaVA-1.5), or an attention-based resampler that also compresses hundreds of patches down to a handful of learned query tokens (BLIP-2's Q-Former, Flamingo's Perceiver Resampler). The frozen decoder-only LLM then treats these "visual tokens" as if they were a prefix of ordinary text embeddings.

Forward Pass, Step by Step

Image → frozen CLIP ViT → patch embeddings \([576,\,1024]\) → connector \(W v+b\) → visual tokens \([576,\,4096]\) → prepended to the tokenized caption's embeddings → frozen LLM produces next-token logits → cross-entropy loss against the ground-truth caption → gradients flow backward through every frozen LLM layer (without updating any of its weights) until they reach the connector → AdamW updates \(\phi\) only.

Design Decision: Why freeze both the encoder and the LLM?

Freezing only the LLM (training encoder + connector) risks corrupting CLIP's contrastively-pretrained feature space, which is expensive to re-derive. Freezing only the encoder (training connector + LLM) reintroduces catastrophic-forgetting risk, because gradients from a near-random connector still perturb the LLM's billions of parameters directly. Freezing both restricts learnable capacity to just the connector — a few million to tens of millions of parameters instead of billions — so it (a) needs far less paired data to converge, (b) trains in hours on a handful of GPUs instead of weeks, and (c) structurally cannot damage either pretrained model, since gradients never update their weights.

🔑 Key Property

Stage-1 training is a constrained optimization: minimize the captioning loss over connector parameters \(\phi\) only, subject to \(\theta_{vision}\) and \(\theta_{LLM}\) held fixed. Equivalently: find the function that maps vision-feature-space onto LLM-token-embedding-space, using the frozen LLM's own next-token prediction as the supervisory signal.

3
Why It Works — The Math
▼

The Captioning Loss and Where Gradients Flow

Let \(v = \text{Enc}_{\text{frozen}}(\text{image})\) and \(h_v = f_\phi(v)\) be the connector's output ("visual tokens"). The caption \(y_1,\dots,y_T\) is generated autoregressively, conditioned on \(h_v\):

$$\mathcal{L}(\phi) = -\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} \log P_\theta\big(y_t \mid h_v,\, y_{

Because only \(\phi\) is trainable, the update rule only ever touches the connector:

$$\nabla_\phi \mathcal{L} = \sum_{t=1}^{T} \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h_v}\cdot \frac{\partial h_v}{\partial \phi}, \qquad \theta \leftarrow \theta \ \text{(never updated)}$$

Note the subtlety: \(\mathcal{L}\) depends on \(\theta\) through the frozen LLM's forward computation, so computing \(\partial \mathcal{L}/\partial h_v\) still requires backpropagating through every frozen LLM layer. "Frozen" means the optimizer skips updating \(\theta\) — it does not mean gradients stop flowing through it.

Why a Linear Connector Is Often Enough

If \(f_\phi(v) = Wv+b\), then in the regime where LLM token embeddings for visually-groundable concepts lie approximately on a locally linear manifold, the optimal \(W,b\) solve a regression problem in disguise — align image features to the (implicit) targets induced by the LLM's own embedding table. More expressive connectors (an MLP, or an attention-based Q-Former / Perceiver Resampler) trade a few million extra parameters for the ability to capture non-linear or many-to-few mappings, e.g. compressing 576 patch tokens down to 32 learned query tokens.

⚠️ Common Pitfall

Alignment loss can look great numerically while the connector has merely learned to reproduce generic captions ("a photo of...") regardless of image content — a failure mode resembling posterior collapse. Always sanity-check that gradients genuinely depend on image content (e.g. ablate the image and confirm the loss rises) before trusting a low Stage-1 loss as evidence of true grounding.

5
Key Takeaways
▼

When to use two-stage (align-then-instruct) training: whenever bolting a new modality onto an already-expensive pretrained LLM, rather than pretraining everything jointly from scratch (which costs orders of magnitude more compute than reusing frozen experts).

How to decide what to freeze/unfreeze:

  • Small paired dataset, expensive base LLM → freeze both, train only a tiny connector (LLaVA / MiniGPT-4 Stage 1)
  • Connector already aligned, have instruction data → unfreeze the LLM (or attach LoRA) at a low learning rate (Stage 2 instruction tuning)
  • Need the best possible visual grounding and can afford it → also unfreeze the vision encoder late, at a very low LR (LLaVA-1.5 / Qwen-VL style)
  • LLM must stay 100% frozen even at inference-time scale → add new cross-attention layers instead of a simple prefix connector (Flamingo)

Connector families in the literature: LLaVA uses a single linear layer (v1) or 2-layer MLP (v1.5) — cheapest, assumes a near-linear mapping. BLIP-2's Q-Former is a small transformer with learned query tokens trained first with contrastive/matching/captioning losses, then bridged to the LLM — compresses many patches into few tokens. Flamingo's Perceiver Resampler plays a similar compressing role, feeding gated cross-attention layers interleaved inside an otherwise frozen LLM.

💡 Hierarchy

Joint end-to-end multimodal pretraining ⊃ Two-stage (align → instruct) ⊃ Frozen-everything zero-shot prompting. Two-stage training is the practical sweet spot: nearly all the sample-efficiency of reusing frozen pretrained experts, with almost all the flexibility of full fine-tuning, at a small fraction of the compute.

Code Trace
▼
import torch, torch.nn as nn
# --- Stage 1: freeze encoder + LLM, train only the connector ---
vision_encoder = CLIPVisionModel.from_pretrained(CLIP_ID).eval()
llm = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained(LLM_ID)
connector = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(1024, 4096), nn.GELU(),
nn.Linear(4096, 4096))
for p in vision_encoder.parameters(): p.requires_grad = False
for p in llm.parameters(): p.requires_grad = False
for p in connector.parameters(): p.requires_grad = True
optim = torch.optim.AdamW(connector.parameters(), lr=1e-3)
for images, captions in caption_loader: # e.g. CC3M / LAION subset
with torch.no_grad():
v = vision_encoder(images).last_hidden_state # [B, 576, 1024]
h_v = connector(v) # [B, 576, 4096] -- grad flows here
text_emb = llm.get_input_embeddings()(captions.input_ids)
inputs_embeds = torch.cat([h_v, text_emb], dim=1)
out = llm(inputs_embeds=inputs_embeds, labels=captions.labels)
out.loss.backward() # grads reach connector THROUGH the frozen llm
optim.step(); optim.zero_grad() # llm & vision_encoder weights: untouched
# --- Stage 2: instruction tuning (separate script) ---
for p in llm.parameters(): p.requires_grad = True # unfreeze, low LR / LoRA
# connector is now pre-aligned -- fine-tune jointly on multimodal instructions
