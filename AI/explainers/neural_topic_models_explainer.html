1
Understanding the Concept

What is a topic model? Imagine you have millions of articles. A topic model automatically discovers recurring themes — clusters of words that co-occur — without any human labels. Classic LDA does this through slow iterative sampling. Neural topic models do the same thing, but use a neural network to make the process fast and scalable.

💡 The Key Insight

Instead of running expensive MCMC or variational EM on each document separately, train a neural encoder (the "amortization network") that maps any document's bag-of-words directly to a topic distribution in one forward pass. The encoder learns to be a universal inference function.

Analogy. Think of LDA as a bespoke tailor who re-measures every customer from scratch. The neural encoder is a sizing algorithm trained on thousands of customers — it can immediately predict your measurements from a photo. Same output, but massively faster at test time.

🔑 ProdLDA's Twist

ProdLDA replaces the Dirichlet prior with a logistic-normal distribution (product-of-experts). This gives a Gaussian variational family (easier to optimize) while still producing sparse, coherent topics — often sharper than classic LDA.

2
Architecture Deep Dive

Both NTM and ProdLDA follow a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) blueprint applied to text. Here's the full information flow:

📐 VAE-for-Topics Blueprint

Encoder \(q_\phi(\theta \mid \mathbf{w})\): a feed-forward MLP that takes a document's normalized BoW vector \(\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^V\) and outputs the parameters \((\mu, \sigma^2)\) of an approximate posterior over the \(K\)-dimensional topic proportions \(\theta\).

Reparameterization: \(\theta_\text{raw} = \mu + \sigma \odot \epsilon,\; \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)\), then \(\theta = \text{softmax}(\theta_\text{raw})\) (NTM) or logistic-normal (ProdLDA).

Decoder \(p_\beta(\mathbf{w} \mid \theta)\): reconstructs word probabilities as \(\hat{\mathbf{w}} = \text{softmax}(\beta \theta)\), where \(\beta \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times V}\) is the topic–word matrix (each row = one topic's word distribution).

NTM vs ProdLDA key difference: NTM uses a Dirichlet-like prior approximated in the Gaussian space (Laplace approximation to Dirichlet). ProdLDA uses a logistic-normal prior: \(\log \theta_k \propto \mathcal{N}(\mu_k, \sigma_k^2)\) normalized via softmax — this is the product-of-experts construction where each factor gates a topic.

Training objective (ELBO):

$$\mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(\theta|\mathbf{w})}\!\left[\log p_\beta(\mathbf{w}|\theta)\right] - \mathrm{KL}\!\left(q_\phi(\theta|\mathbf{w}) \| p(\theta)\right)$$

The reconstruction term is a categorical cross-entropy over the vocabulary. The KL term regularizes the encoder toward the prior. For Gaussian \(q\) and Gaussian prior:

$$\mathrm{KL} = -\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^K \left(1 + \log\sigma_k^2 - \mu_k^2 - \sigma_k^2\right)$$
⚠️ Collapsed Topics (Common Failure Mode)

If the KL term dominates early in training, the encoder collapses to the prior and all topics become identical. Fix: KL annealing — linearly scale the KL term from 0 → 1 over the first few thousand steps.

3
Why It Works — Mathematical Guarantee

The ELBO is a lower bound on the log marginal likelihood \(\log p(\mathbf{w})\). Maximizing it simultaneously:

  1. Pushes the decoder to assign high probability to the observed words (reconstruction)
  2. Keeps the inferred topic distribution close to the prior (regularization)

The decomposition:

$$\log p(\mathbf{w}) = \mathcal{L}(\phi,\beta) + \mathrm{KL}(q_\phi(\theta|\mathbf{w}) \| p(\theta|\mathbf{w})) \geq \mathcal{L}(\phi,\beta)$$

The gap equals the posterior KL, which is always \(\geq 0\), so \(\mathcal{L}\) is always a lower bound. The tighter it is, the better the encoder approximates the true posterior.

Logistic-Normal prior (ProdLDA): If \(\mathbf{z} \sim \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_0, \text{diag}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_0^2))\) and \(\theta = \text{softmax}(\mathbf{z})\), this induces a logistic-normal distribution on the simplex. Unlike Dirichlet, it supports negative correlations between topics and has full-covariance flexibility when a non-diagonal prior is used.

🎯 Decision Guide

Use ProdLDA when you want LDA-like interpretability but with faster training and sharper topics on large corpora.
Use NTM as a stepping stone; ProdLDA usually wins on coherence.
Use BERTopic when pre-trained language model quality matters more than the probabilistic model's interpretability.
Use classic LDA when you need well-understood probabilistic semantics and a small corpus fits in memory.

6
Key Takeaways
  • Amortized inference — the encoder is trained once; at test time, any document gets a topic distribution in a single forward pass (O(1) vs O(document × iterations) for LDA).
  • ProdLDA's logistic-normal supports negative topic correlations and richer posterior geometry than the Dirichlet simplex.
  • KL annealing is critical — without it, topics collapse. Ramp KL weight from 0 to 1 over the first epoch.
  • Evaluation: use topic coherence (NPMI) on held-out words, not perplexity alone — neural models can have lower perplexity but worse coherence.
  • These models connect to VAEs broadly: every insight about posterior collapse, decoder capacity, and ELBO applies here too.
