Understanding Prophet
▼

What is it? Prophet is an open-source forecasting procedure developed by Facebook (Meta) that is designed for forecasting univariate time series data. It excels with time series that exhibit strong seasonal effects, have several seasons of historical data, and often contain missing values or outliers. Prophet is particularly useful for business forecasting scenarios where interpretability and ease of use are paramount.

Analogy: Imagine predicting daily ice cream sales. You know sales generally increase over the years (trend), peak every summer (yearly seasonality), are higher on weekends (weekly seasonality), and spike on national holidays like July 4th (holiday effects). Prophet is like a very organized accountant who breaks down your sales into these distinct, interpretable components, then sums them up to give you a future prediction.

💡 If you remember one thing

Prophet uses an additive model to decompose a time series into three main components: a non-linear trend, multiple periodic seasonalities (e.g., weekly, yearly), and custom holiday effects. These components are then summed to produce a forecast: $$y(t) = g(t) + s(t) + h(t) + \epsilon_t$$ where \(g(t)\) is trend, \(s(t)\) is seasonality, \(h(t)\) is holidays, and \(\epsilon_t\) is the error term.

2
How Prophet Works Internally
▼

1. The Trend Component \(g(t)\)

Prophet models the trend using either a piecewise linear or a logistic growth model. The piecewise linear trend is most common and involves automatically detecting "changepoints" — points in time where the growth rate of the trend changes. Between these changepoints, the trend is a simple linear function. The model fits these linear segments and their slope adjustments using a sparse prior, effectively allowing the trend to adapt to changes in the data's underlying growth or decay.

2. The Seasonality Component \(s(t)\)

Seasonality (e.g., daily, weekly, yearly) is modeled using Fourier series. A Fourier series can approximate any periodic function by summing sines and cosines of different frequencies. Prophet allows you to specify the "order" of the Fourier series for each seasonality, controlling its flexibility. A higher order allows for more complex, wigglier seasonal patterns, while a lower order results in smoother patterns. This makes it robust to complex cyclical behaviors without requiring manual feature engineering.

3. The Holiday Component \(h(t)\)

Holidays and other irregular but predictable events (e.g., product launches, major sporting events) are incorporated by providing a custom list of dates. Prophet treats these as dummy variables (indicator functions) in the model, allowing each holiday to have its own estimated additive effect on the forecast. This prevents these one-off events from distorting the trend or seasonality components.

Design Decision: Why an Additive Model and Not ARIMA/Exponential Smoothing?

Traditional time series models like ARIMA or Exponential Smoothing often struggle with missing data, irregular time steps, and multiple seasonalities. Prophet's additive model is more robust because each component is modeled independently and then summed. This makes it easier to handle missing data (it's just missing inputs for some components), allows for flexible changepoints, and simplifies the inclusion of multiple seasonalities and holiday effects without complex model re-specification. It's designed for scale, with sensible defaults and minimal hyperparameter tuning.

🔑 Key Property

Prophet's strength lies in its modularity and interpretability. Each component (trend, seasonality, holidays) is estimated separately, allowing users to understand the contribution of each factor to the overall forecast. This is particularly valuable in business contexts where explaining "why" a forecast is what it is, is as important as the forecast itself.

3
Why It Works — The Math
▼

The Additive Model

The core of Prophet is a decomposable time series model: \(y(t) = g(t) + s(t) + h(t) + \epsilon_t\). This is a generalized additive model (GAM) where each component is modeled with flexibility.

Piecewise Linear Trend \(g(t)\)

The trend component models a constant rate of growth, \(k\), with \(S\) possible changepoints at times \(c_j\). The rate can change by \(\delta_j\) at each changepoint. The trend is defined as:

$$g(t) = (k + \sum_{j=1}^S a_j \delta_j) t + (m + \sum_{j=1}^S a_j \gamma_j)$$

where \(a_j = 1\) if \(t > c_j\) and \(0\) otherwise. The \(\gamma_j\) terms are offsets to make the function continuous at changepoints. This can be rewritten more compactly using initial rate \(k_0\) and offset \(m_0\):

$$g(t) = k_0 t + m_0 + \sum_{j=1}^S \delta_j (t - c_j)^+$$

where \((t-c_j)^+ = \max(0, t-c_j)\). Prophet places a sparse prior (e.g., Laplace) on \(\delta_j\) to prevent overfitting, meaning most \(\delta_j\) will be zero, effectively selecting only the most impactful changepoints.

Seasonal Component \(s(t)\) with Fourier Series

Periodic seasonality is modeled using a standard Fourier series. For a period \(P\) (e.g., \(P=7\) for weekly seasonality, \(P=365.25\) for yearly), the seasonal component is a sum of sine and cosine terms:

$$s(t) = \sum_{k=1}^N \left( A_k \cos\left(\frac{2\pi kt}{P}\right) + B_k \sin\left(\frac{2\pi kt}{P}\right) \right)$$

Here, \(N\) is the Fourier order, which controls the complexity of the seasonality. Higher \(N\) allows for more complex, non-sinusoidal patterns. The coefficients \(A_k\) and \(B_k\) are parameters to be estimated. Prophet fits these coefficients using a weak prior (e.g., Normal) to prevent overfitting.

⚠️ Common Pitfall

Prophet assumes an additive model. If your time series exhibits multiplicative seasonality (e.g., seasonal peaks grow larger as the overall trend increases), you should transform your data (e.g., take the logarithm) before applying Prophet. Prophet can also handle multiplicative seasonality by setting seasonality_mode='multiplicative' in the model, which transforms the seasonalities to be proportional to the trend.

Key Takeaways
▼

When to use Prophet:

  • Time series with strong seasonal effects (yearly, weekly, daily).
  • Several seasons of historical data (at least 2 years for yearly seasonality).
  • Irregular historical data with missing values or large outliers.
  • When interpretability of trend, seasonality, and holiday effects is important.
  • When you need automated forecasting with minimal manual tuning.

When NOT to use Prophet:

  • Short time series (less than one full seasonal cycle).
  • Time series without clear seasonality or trend (e.g., pure white noise).
  • When the underlying data generating process is highly complex and non-additive (e.g., strong interactions between components).
  • For very high-frequency data (e.g., seconds/minutes) where the seasonal patterns might be too fine-grained for Fourier series.
  • When you need a deep causal understanding of relationships between multiple variables (Prophet is univariate).
💡 Practical Advice

Prophet is a "batteries-included" solution for many common business forecasting problems. It provides reasonable forecasts with minimal effort, making it an excellent baseline model. Don't underestimate its utility in production systems where robustness and ease of maintenance often outweigh marginal improvements in predictive accuracy from more complex models.

import pandas as pd
from prophet import Prophet
# 1. Prepare your data: must have 'ds' (datetime) and 'y' (value) columns
# Example: daily sales data for 2 years
data = {
'ds': pd.to_datetime(pd.date_range(start='2022-01-01', periods=730, freq='D')),
'y': [100 + i*0.1 + (i%7)*5 + (i%365)*8 + (5*np.random.randn()) for i in range(730)]
}
df = pd.DataFrame(data)
# 2. Instantiate and fit the model
# Adjust changepoint_prior_scale for trend flexibility (default 0.05)
# Adjust seasonality_prior_scale for seasonality strength (default 10.0)
# Add custom holidays (optional)
holidays = pd.DataFrame({
'holiday': 'christmas',
'ds': pd.to_datetime(['2022-12-25', '2023-12-25']),
'lower_window': -1, 'upper_window': 1 # effect spans 3 days
})
m = Prophet(
changepoint_prior_scale=0.1, # higher for more flexible trend
seasonality_mode='additive',
holidays=holidays
)
m.add_seasonality(name='monthly', period=30.5, fourier_order=5) # custom seasonality
m.fit(df)
# 3. Make future dataframe and predict
future = m.make_future_dataframe(periods=30) # forecast next 30 days
forecast = m.predict(future)
