1
Understanding Multi-Task Learning in RecSys
▼

What is it? A production recommender rarely optimizes one objective. A feed ranker must predict whether a user will click (CTR) and, separately, whether — having clicked — they will convert (purchase, watch-to-completion, install): CVR. Training two independent models throws away shared signal ("users who click tend to convert for similar reasons"). Naively bolting both tasks onto one shared trunk (a shared-bottom model) creates a different problem: when tasks disagree about which features matter, gradients from one task drag down the other — negative transfer.

The other landmine — sample selection bias (SSB). CVR is only observable for the sliver of impressions that were clicked (often <10%). A CVR model trained solely on clicked traffic learns a distribution that does not match the full impression space it must serve at inference time — its inputs are systematically biased toward "clickable" items, and its training set is tiny and sparse.

Analogy: Think of a hospital. A general physician (shared bottom / experts) sees every patient and takes notes. Specialists (task towers) make the final calls for their domain. ESMM, MMOE, and PLE each answer a different version of: how much of the general physician's notes should each specialist trust, and does each specialist need dedicated staff of their own?

💡 If you remember one thing

ESMM fixes sample selection bias by modeling <\(pCTCVR = pCTR \times pCVR\)> over the entire impression space. MMOE fixes negative transfer by routing shared experts through per-task gates. PLE goes further, giving each task its own experts plus shared ones, so gates never have to fight over fully-shared parameters.

2
How ESMM, MMOE, and PLE Work Internally
▼

ESMM — Entire Space Multi-Task Model

ESMM keeps the architecture simple: one shared embedding layer feeds two towers, a CTR tower and a CVR tower. The trick is entirely in the training objective. Instead of training the CVR tower only on clicked samples with label "converted or not," ESMM multiplies the two tower outputs to form \(pCTCVR = pCTR \times pCVR\), and supervises this product against the observed click-and-convert label over every impression. Because every impression has a well-defined (possibly zero) CTCVR label, the CVR tower now receives gradient signal from the full, unbiased space — solving SSB and data sparsity without ever training CVR in isolation.

MMOE — Multi-gate Mixture-of-Experts

MMOE replaces the single shared trunk with \(K\) parallel "expert" sub-networks that all see the same input. Each task gets its own lightweight gating network that looks at the input and outputs a softmax distribution over the experts — essentially "how much should I trust each expert for my task?" The task's representation is the gate-weighted sum of expert outputs, then passed through a task-specific tower. Because each task learns its own gate, correlated tasks can converge to similar gate distributions (implicit heavy sharing) while conflicting tasks can diverge to different experts (implicit isolation) — all learned end-to-end, no manual architecture search.

PLE — Progressive Layered Extraction

MMOE still lets every expert be reachable by every task's gate — a task can accidentally get "polluted" by an expert another task dominates, since nothing stops the gate from routing there. PLE's Customized Gate Control (CGC) layer explicitly partitions experts into task-specific groups (only that task's gate may route to them) and a shared group (every task's gate may route to it). Stacking several CGC layers ("progressive" extraction) lets shared and specific representations be refined jointly across layers before the final towers, which empirically reduces the seesaw effect (one task's metric improving only at another's expense) more than MMOE.

🔑 Key Property

All three are answers to the same question at increasing sophistication: ESMM fixes the label/space mismatch between tasks; MMOE fixes hard parameter sharing with learned soft routing; PLE fixes MMOE's remaining risk of cross-task interference by making some experts structurally off-limits to other tasks.

3
Why It Works — The Math
▼

ESMM Objective

Let \(z\) be the click label and \(y\) the conversion label (only meaningful when \(z=1\)). ESMM defines the joint loss over all impressions:

$$L = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \ell\big(z_i,\, pCTR(x_i)\big) \;+\; \sum_{i=1}^{N} \ell\big(z_i y_i,\, pCTR(x_i)\times pCVR(x_i)\big)$$

The second term supervises \(pCTCVR = pCTR \times pCVR\) against the observed \(z_i y_i\) label, which is well-defined for every impression (it is simply 0 when there was no click). This is what lets the CVR tower's weights receive gradient from non-clicked impressions too — indirectly, through the chain rule on the product.

MMOE Gating

With \(K\) experts \(E_1,\dots,E_K\) and task \(k\), a gate produces a softmax distribution over experts from a linear projection of the input:

$$g^k(x) = \mathrm{softmax}(W_g^k x), \qquad f^k(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{K} g^k(x)_i \, E_i(x), \qquad \hat{y}^k = h^k\big(f^k(x)\big)$$

\(h^k\) is the task-specific tower. Because \(W_g^k\) differs per task, two tasks can learn arbitrarily different mixtures over the very same pool of experts — from nearly identical (high sharing) to nearly disjoint (near-isolation).

PLE / CGC Layer

PLE partitions experts into a shared set \(S\) and per-task sets \(T^k\). Task \(k\)'s gate may route only to \(S \cup T^k\):

$$g^k(x) = \mathrm{softmax}\big(W_g^k x\big) \text{ over } S \cup T^k, \qquad f^k(x) = \sum_{i \in S \cup T^k} g^k(x)_i \, E_i(x)$$

Stacking \(L\) such CGC layers means the representation fed to the final towers has been progressively refined: \(x^{(1)} \to f^{(1)} \to x^{(2)} \to f^{(2)} \to \cdots \to \hat{y}^k\), where each layer's shared and task-specific experts are recombined before the next layer's gates re-route.

⚠️ Common Pitfall

ESMM does not train a CVR model on clicked data at all — trying to read \(pCVR\) off the tower and evaluate it only on clicked traffic, or clipping/renormalizing it, breaks the identity \(pCTCVR = pCTR \times pCVR\) the whole method relies on. Likewise, MMOE gates are cheap (a single linear layer) — the "seesaw" effect can still appear if all experts are shared and two tasks are strongly anti-correlated, which is exactly the gap PLE was built to close.

Key Takeaways
▼
ModelSolvesKey mechanismLimitation
Shared-BottomParameter efficiencyOne trunk, two towersNegative transfer when tasks conflict
ESMMSample selection bias in CVR\(pCTCVR=pCTR\times pCVR\) over entire spaceStill hard-shares the bottom; no explicit gating
MMOENegative transfer via soft routingPer-task softmax gates over shared expertsAll experts remain reachable by all tasks — seesaw can persist
PLEResidual cross-task interferenceTask-specific + shared experts, progressive layersMore parameters & a design choice (how many specific/shared experts)

When to reach for each:

  • Only a click→conversion funnel with severe sparsity on the downstream label → ESMM (or ESMM combined with MMOE/PLE towers in production systems).
  • Several loosely related tasks (e.g. CTR + dwell time) where you're unsure how correlated they are → MMOE, let the gates discover it.
  • Tasks known to sometimes conflict (e.g. clicks vs. long-term satisfaction) and you can afford the extra parameters → PLE.
💡 Hierarchy

These are orthogonal ideas, not mutually exclusive: real systems often use PLE-style expert routing inside an ESMM-style entire-space training objective, giving you unbiased CVR supervision and negative-transfer-resistant sharing at the same time.

# ---- Shared-Bottom baseline ----
h = shared_bottom(x) # one trunk for everyone

# ---- ESMM: entire-space training ----
p_ctr = ctr_tower(embed(x)).sigmoid()
p_cvr = cvr_tower(embed(x)).sigmoid()
p_ctcvr = p_ctr * p_cvr # supervised on ALL impressions
loss = bce(p_ctr, click_label) + bce(p_ctcvr, click_and_convert_label)
# ---- MMOE: experts + per-task gates ----
experts = torch.stack([E_i(x) for E_i in experts], dim=1) # [B, K]
gate_ctr = F.softmax(W_gate_ctr(x), dim=-1) # [B, K]
gate_cvr = F.softmax(W_gate_cvr(x), dim=-1)
f_ctr = (gate_ctr * experts).sum(-1)
f_cvr = (gate_cvr * experts).sum(-1)
p_ctr, p_cvr = ctr_tower(f_ctr).sigmoid(), cvr_tower(f_cvr).sigmoid()
# ---- PLE: task-specific + shared experts, per CGC layer ----
shared_out = torch.stack([E(x) for E in shared_experts], dim=1)
ctr_out = torch.stack([E(x) for E in ctr_experts], dim=1)
cvr_out = torch.stack([E(x) for E in cvr_experts], dim=1)
gate_ctr = F.softmax(W_gate_ctr(x), dim=-1) # over shared_out ⊕ ctr_out only
gate_cvr = F.softmax(W_gate_cvr(x), dim=-1) # over shared_out ⊕ cvr_out only
x = next_cgc_layer(x) # stack layers, then towers
# All three: same eval signature
print(f"pCTR={p_ctr:.3f} pCVR={p_cvr:.3f} pCTCVR={p_ctr*p_cvr:.3f}")
