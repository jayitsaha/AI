Outer, Kronecker & Hadamard Products

Three distinct matrix products — each with its own geometry, dimension rule, and application domain
Foundational Linear Algebra Math & Stats All \(O(mn)\) or \(O(m^2 n^2)\)
1
Understanding the Three Products
▼

What are these? Ordinary matrix multiplication is just one way to combine matrices. Three other products appear constantly in ML math — they produce results with very different shapes and meanings.

Outer Product — "column times row"

Take a column vector \(\mathbf{u} \in \mathbb{R}^m\) and a row vector \(\mathbf{v}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^n\). Their outer product \(\mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}^\top\) is an \(m \times n\) matrix where cell \((i,j) = u_i \cdot v_j\). Think of it as a multiplication table: each row of the result is a scaled copy of \(\mathbf{v}\).

Kronecker Product — "block expansion"

Given matrices \(A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}\) and \(B \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times q}\), the Kronecker product \(A \otimes B\) is an \((mp) \times (nq)\) block matrix — each element \(a_{ij}\) of \(A\) is replaced by the entire scaled block \(a_{ij} B\). It's a structured, hierarchical expansion.

Hadamard Product — "elementwise"

The Hadamard (element-wise) product \(A \odot B\) requires \(A\) and \(B\) to have the same shape \(m \times n\). Each cell is simply \((A \odot B)_{ij} = a_{ij} \cdot b_{ij}\). It's the simplest — multiply corresponding entries.

💡 If you remember one thing

Outer product: \(m\)-vec × \(n\)-vec → \(m \times n\) matrix (all pairs). Kronecker: \(m{\times}n\) block-scales a \(p{\times}q\) matrix → \(mp \times nq\) (structured blocks). Hadamard: same-shape matrices → same-shape result (pointwise multiply). Different shapes, different semantics, completely different uses.

2
How Each Product Works Internally
▼

Outer Product: Rank-1 Matrices

Every outer product produces a rank-1 matrix. This is geometrically significant: every row is a scalar multiple of \(\mathbf{v}^\top\), so the row space is spanned by a single vector. This is why outer products appear in dyadic decompositions, covariance estimation (\(\mathbf{x}\mathbf{x}^\top\)), and the update step of the BFGS optimizer.

$$(\mathbf{u} \otimes_{\text{outer}} \mathbf{v}^\top)_{ij} = u_i \cdot v_j \quad \Rightarrow \quad \text{rank} = 1 \text{ always}$$

Kronecker Product: Mixed-Product Property

The Kronecker product satisfies the mixed-product property: \((A \otimes B)(C \otimes D) = (AC) \otimes (BD)\) whenever the inner dimensions match. This is why it's the natural tool for expressing tensor-product structures, vectorisation identities like \(\text{vec}(AXB) = (B^\top \otimes A)\,\text{vec}(X)\), and multi-dimensional convolutions.

$$(A \otimes B)_{(i-1)p+k,\,(j-1)q+l} = a_{ij} \cdot b_{kl}$$

Hadamard Product: Commutative & Associative

Hadamard product is commutative (\(A \odot B = B \odot A\)), associative, and distributes over addition. Because it operates entry-by-entry, it is a linear map in each factor separately but NOT bilinear jointly in the same way as standard multiplication. It appears in LSTM gates (element-wise gating), image masks, and the self-attention computation when masking positions.

$$(A \odot B)_{ij} = a_{ij} \cdot b_{ij}, \quad A,B \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$$
🔑 Key Property

The three products produce matrices of fundamentally different sizes: Outer: \(m \times n\). Kronecker: \(mp \times nq\). Hadamard: \(m \times n\) (same as inputs, which must match). Only Hadamard requires identical input shapes.

3
The Math — Definitions, Properties & Key Identities
▼

Outer Product — Formal Definition

Let \(\mathbf{u} \in \mathbb{R}^m\), \(\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^n\). The outer product is the matrix product of the column vector by the row vector:

$$\mathbf{u} \mathbf{v}^\top = \begin{bmatrix} u_1 \\ u_2 \\ \vdots \\ u_m \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} v_1 & v_2 & \cdots & v_n \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} u_1 v_1 & u_1 v_2 & \cdots & u_1 v_n \\ u_2 v_1 & u_2 v_2 & \cdots & u_2 v_n \\ \vdots & & \ddots & \vdots \\ u_m v_1 & u_m v_2 & \cdots & u_m v_n \end{bmatrix}$$

Key: \(\text{rank}(\mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}^\top) = 1\) for any nonzero \(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\). Sum-of-rank-1 matrices (SVD): \(A = \sum_k \sigma_k \mathbf{u}_k \mathbf{v}_k^\top\).

Kronecker Product — Formal Definition

For \(A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}\), \(B \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times q}\):

$$A \otimes B = \begin{bmatrix} a_{11}B & a_{12}B & \cdots & a_{1n}B \\ a_{21}B & a_{22}B & \cdots & a_{2n}B \\ \vdots & & \ddots & \vdots \\ a_{m1}B & a_{m2}B & \cdots & a_{mn}B \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{mp \times nq}$$

Crucial identities: \((A \otimes B)^\top = A^\top \otimes B^\top\). If \(A, B\) invertible: \((A \otimes B)^{-1} = A^{-1} \otimes B^{-1}\). The vec trick: for conformable \(A,X,B\):

$$\text{vec}(AXB) = (B^\top \otimes A)\,\text{vec}(X)$$

Hadamard Product — Formal Definition

For \(A, B \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}\):

$$(A \odot B)_{ij} = a_{ij} b_{ij}$$

Schur product theorem: if \(A\) and \(B\) are positive semidefinite, then \(A \odot B\) is also PSD. Relation to trace: \(\text{tr}(A^\top B) = \mathbf{1}^\top (A \odot B)\,\mathbf{1} = \sum_{ij} a_{ij} b_{ij}\).

⚠️ Common Confusion

In NumPy, np.outer(u,v) gives the outer product, np.kron(A,B) gives Kronecker, and A * B (or np.multiply(A,B)) gives Hadamard. Do NOT confuse A @ B (matrix multiply) with any of these. The ⊗ symbol is also used for tensor products — in linear algebra context it usually means Kronecker; in ML/physics it often means tensor product (which generalizes Kronecker).

Key Takeaways & When to Use Each
▼
Dimension Outer Product Kronecker Product Hadamard Product
Notation \(\mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}^\top\) \(A \otimes B\) \(A \odot B\)
Input shapes \(m\)-vec, \(n\)-vec \(m{\times}n\) and \(p{\times}q\) \(m{\times}n\) and \(m{\times}n\) (must match)
Output shape \(m \times n\) \(mp \times nq\) \(m \times n\)
Rank of result Always 1 \(\text{rank}(A)\cdot\text{rank}(B)\) Up to \(\min(m,n)\)
Key ML use Covariance, BFGS, SVD, attention outer Multi-dim conv, Kronecker factored optimizers (K-FAC) LSTM/GRU gates, attention masks, LoRA merge
NumPy np.outer(u, v) np.kron(A, B) A * B
💡 Decision Guide

Use outer when you need to build a rank-1 matrix from two vectors — covariance updates, gradient outer products in natural gradient methods, or attention logit matrices (\(\mathbf{q}\mathbf{k}^\top\)). Use Kronecker when you need structured block expansions — multi-linear maps, tensor network contractions, or K-FAC approximate curvature. Use Hadamard when you need per-element gating or masking — LSTM forget gates, attention masks, mixture weights, or residual scaling.

Code — From Scratch & NumPy
▼

From Scratch (Pure Python)

# Outer product: u (m,) × v (n,) → (m, n)
def outer(u, v):
    return [[u[i] * v[j] for j in range(len(v))] for i in range(len(u))]

# Kronecker product: A (m,n) ⊗ B (p,q) → (mp, nq)
def kronecker(A, B):
    m, n = len(A), len(A[0])
    p, q = len(B), len(B[0])
    C = [[0.0]*(n*q) for _ in range(m*p)]
    for i in range(m):
        for j in range(n):
            for k in range(p):
                for l in range(q):
                    C[i*p+k][j*q+l] = A[i][j] * B[k][l]
    return C

# Hadamard product: A (m,n) ⊙ B (m,n) → (m,n)  (shapes must match)
def hadamard(A, B):
    assert len(A)==len(B) and len(A[0])==len(B[0]), "shapes must match"
    return [[A[i][j]*B[i][j] for j in range(len(A[0]))] for i in range(len(A))]

NumPy & PyTorch

import numpy as np, torch

u = np.array([1, 2, 3]);  v = np.array([2, 4, 6])
A = np.array([[1,2],[3,4]]);  B = np.array([[5,6],[7,8]])
C = np.array([[1,0,1],[2,3,0],[0,1,2]])

# Outer product
np.outer(u, v)          # (3,3)
u[:, None] @ v[None, :] # equivalent using broadcasting
torch.outer(torch.tensor(u, dtype=torch.float), torch.tensor(v, dtype=torch.float))

# Kronecker product
np.kron(A, B)           # (4,4)
# PyTorch: no built-in, but torch.kron added in 1.10
torch.kron(torch.tensor(A, dtype=torch.float), torch.tensor(B, dtype=torch.float))

# Hadamard product
C * C                   # (3,3) — Python * is elementwise for arrays
np.multiply(C, C)       # explicit
torch.tensor(C, dtype=torch.float) * torch.tensor(C, dtype=torch.float)
🚨 Gotcha

In PyTorch, A * B is Hadamard (elementwise). A @ B is matrix multiply. torch.outer requires 1-D tensors. Broadcasting can silently compute something other than the outer product if shapes aren't exactly (m,1) × (1,n) — always verify output shape.
