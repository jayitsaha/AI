Greedy, Top-k & Top-p (Nucleus) Decoding

How language models turn a probability distribution into the next token
Foundational LLMs Generation / Decoding O(V log V)
1
Understanding Decoding Strategies
▼

What is it? At every step of text generation, a language model outputs a score (a "logit") for every possible next word in its vocabulary — tens of thousands of candidates. Softmax turns those scores into a probability distribution. Decoding is the rulebook for turning that distribution into one actual chosen word. Greedy decoding, top-k, and top-p (nucleus) sampling are three different rulebooks, and temperature is a dial that reshapes the distribution before any of them are applied.

Analogy: Imagine a chef presenting you a tray of dishes, each with a "likely to satisfy you" score. Greedy means you always take the single highest-scoring dish, every time — safe, but repetitive. Top-k means the chef only lets you choose among the k best dishes, then you pick randomly weighted by score — some variety, but bounded. Top-p means the chef keeps adding dishes to your choices, best first, until they've covered p% of your total appetite, then lets you choose — the tray gets bigger when many dishes are appealing and smaller when one dish dominates. Temperature is how hungry vs. picky you are: low temperature makes you desperate for the top dish only, high temperature makes you willing to try almost anything.

💡 If you remember one thing

Temperature reshapes the distribution (\(z / T\) before softmax), then greedy/top-k/top-p decide which tokens are even allowed to compete, and sampling makes the final random draw among the survivors. Greedy is the special case of top-k with \(k=1\), and equivalently \(T \to 0\).

2
How It Works Internally
▼

Stage 1 — Logits to Probabilities (with Temperature)

The model's final layer produces one logit \(z_i\) per vocabulary token \(i\). Temperature \(T\) rescales the logits before softmax:

$$p_i = \frac{\exp(z_i / T)}{\sum_j \exp(z_j / T)}$$

As \(T \to 0\), the largest logit dominates and \(p\) collapses onto a one-hot vector (pure argmax). As \(T \to \infty\), all exponentials shrink toward 1 and \(p\) approaches a uniform distribution. \(T=1\) leaves the model's raw calibration untouched.

Stage 2 — Truncate the Candidate Pool

Greedy decoding takes \(\operatorname{argmax}_i p_i\) — no randomness, fully deterministic, equivalent to top-k with \(k=1\).

Top-k sampling sorts tokens by probability descending, keeps only the first \(k\), zeroes out everything else, and renormalizes the remaining \(k\) probabilities so they sum to 1 before sampling. \(k\) is a fixed integer regardless of how peaked or flat the distribution is.

Top-p (nucleus) sampling (Holtzman et al., 2020) sorts tokens descending, then keeps adding tokens to the "nucleus" set until their cumulative probability first reaches or exceeds threshold \(p\), then renormalizes just that set. Unlike top-k, the size of the surviving set adapts every step: a confident, peaked distribution needs only 1–2 tokens to reach \(p\); a flat, uncertain distribution needs dozens.

Stage 3 — Draw a Sample

Once the survivor set and its renormalized probabilities are fixed, the final token is drawn via weighted random sampling (inverse-CDF / roulette-wheel selection) — except for greedy decoding, which skips sampling entirely and always emits the single highest-probability survivor.

Design Decision: Why Not Just Always Use Top-k?

Fixed \(k\) is a blunt instrument. When the model is very confident (e.g. finishing "Paris is the capital of ___" with "France" at 0.95 probability), top-k=40 still lets 39 nonsense tokens compete, occasionally producing garbage. When the model is genuinely uncertain among many plausible continuations, top-k=40 may cut off reasonable options. Top-p's adaptive cutoff — "keep the smallest set that explains p of the probability mass" — was designed to fix exactly this: it shrinks to 1 token when the model is sure, and grows to hundreds when the model is unsure.

🔑 Key Property

Greedy decoding is deterministic and reproducible but prone to repetition loops (degenerate text). Top-k and top-p introduce controlled randomness that trades determinism for diversity — top-p specifically adapts its cutoff width to the model's per-step confidence, which is why it is the default in most production chat systems, often combined with a moderate temperature (0.7–1.0) and top-k as a safety cap.

3
Why It Works — The Math
▼

Temperature as an Entropy Dial

The Shannon entropy of the resulting distribution, \(H(p) = -\sum_i p_i \log p_i\), is monotonically increasing in \(T\). Formally, as \(T \to 0^+\):

$$\lim_{T \to 0^+} \frac{\exp(z_i/T)}{\sum_j \exp(z_j/T)} = \mathbb{1}[i = \operatorname{argmax}_j z_j]$$

This is exactly the greedy rule — greedy decoding is the \(T \to 0\) limit of temperature-scaled softmax, and also the \(k=1\) special case of top-k. All three concepts collapse to the same deterministic point.

Top-k as Truncated Renormalization

Let \(S_k\) be the indices of the \(k\) largest \(p_i\). Top-k sampling draws from:

$$p_i^{(k)} = \begin{cases} \dfrac{p_i}{\sum_{j \in S_k} p_j} & i \in S_k \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

Top-p as an Adaptive Nucleus

Sort probabilities descending: \(p_{(1)} \ge p_{(2)} \ge \dots\). Define the nucleus \(V^{(p)}\) as the smallest prefix set satisfying:

$$\sum_{i \in V^{(p)}} p_{(i)} \ge p, \qquad V^{(p)} = \arg\min_{n} \left\{ \sum_{i=1}^{n} p_{(i)} \ge p \right\}$$

Then renormalize exactly as with top-k, but over \(V^{(p)}\) instead of \(S_k\). Because \(|V^{(p)}|\) depends on the shape of the distribution at that timestep, top-p is scale-adaptive: it needs no re-tuning across prompts of varying certainty, whereas a fixed \(k\) does.

⚠️ Common Misconception

Temperature and top-k/top-p are not interchangeable — they compose. In practice you apply temperature first (reshape the whole distribution), then truncate with top-k and/or top-p, then renormalize and sample. Applying them in the wrong order, or applying temperature after truncation, changes the relative weights of the surviving tokens and gives different results than production libraries (e.g. HuggingFace) implement.

Key Takeaways
▼

When to use each:

  • Greedy — deterministic tasks: code completion, translation, extraction, anything needing reproducibility.
  • Top-k — cheap, simple diversity knob; good baseline, but the fixed cutoff can feel arbitrary across contexts.
  • Top-p (nucleus) — the default for open-ended generation (chat, stories, brainstorming) because it adapts to model confidence automatically.
  • Temperature — orthogonal dial almost always used alongside top-k/top-p, typically 0.7–1.0 for creative text, near 0 for factual/deterministic tasks.

When not to rely on sampling alone: low-stakes single-answer factual QA, or any pipeline that scores/parses the output programmatically — use greedy or very low temperature there.

🎯 Decision Rule

Need reproducible, safe output → greedy (\(T{\to}0\)). Need bounded, fast diversity → top-k. Need diversity that adapts to how confident the model is at each step → top-p, usually with \(T \approx 0.7\text{–}1.0\) and often combined with a top-k safety cap (e.g. top-k=50, top-p=0.9) to guard against the rare very-flat distribution.

From-Scratch Code
▼

A minimal, dependency-free implementation matching exactly what the visualization above computes:

import numpy as np
def softmax(logits, T=1.0):
z = np.array(logits) / T # temperature scaling
z = z - z.max() # numerical stability
e = np.exp(z)
return e / e.sum()
def top_k_filter(probs, k):
idx = np.argsort(probs)[::-1][:k] # indices of k largest
mask = np.zeros_like(probs)
mask[idx] = probs[idx]
return mask / mask.sum() # renormalize survivors
def top_p_filter(probs, p):
order = np.argsort(probs)[::-1]
sorted_p = probs[order]
cum = np.cumsum(sorted_p)
cutoff = np.searchsorted(cum, p) + 1 # smallest n with cum >= p
keep = order[:cutoff]
mask = np.zeros_like(probs)
mask[keep] = probs[keep]
return mask / mask.sum()
def decode(logits, T=1.0, k=None, p=None, greedy=False, rng=np.random):
probs = softmax(logits, T)
if greedy:
return int(np.argmax(probs)) # deterministic argmax
if k is not None:
probs = top_k_filter(probs, k)
if p is not None:
probs = top_p_filter(probs, p)
return int(rng.choice(len(probs), p=probs)) # weighted random draw
⚠️ Production Gotcha

In HuggingFace generate(), temperature and top_p/top_k only take effect when do_sample=True. With do_sample=False (the default), generation is pure greedy and these parameters are silently ignored — a very common source of "why isn't temperature doing anything?" bugs.
