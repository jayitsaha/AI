1
Understanding Topic Modeling
▼

What is it? Topic modeling is a type of unsupervised machine learning that discovers the abstract "topics" that occur in a collection of documents. It helps organize, understand, and summarize large archives of text by identifying recurring themes.

Analogy: Imagine you have a massive library of books, but no one has categorized them. Topic modeling is like an automated librarian that reads through all the books and figures out what each book is "about" (its topics) and what words are most characteristic of those topics. It doesn't know the topic names, but it can tell you "this cluster of books talks a lot about 'engines', 'wheels', 'roads'" (cars) and "that cluster talks about 'pages', 'chapters', 'stories'" (books).

💡 If you remember one thing

Topic modeling assumes that each document is a mixture of several topics, and each topic is a mixture of several words. The goal is to reverse-engineer these mixtures from the observed words in the documents.

We'll explore three prominent methods: Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA), Non-negative Matrix Factorization (NMF), and BERTopic.

2
How Each Method Works
▼

Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA)

LDA is a probabilistic, generative model. It assumes a process by which documents are created:

  1. Choose a distribution over topics for the document (e.g., this document is 80% "cars", 20% "books").
  2. For each word in the document:
    1. Randomly choose a topic from the document's topic distribution.
    2. Randomly choose a word from the chosen topic's word distribution (e.g., if "cars" topic, pick "engine" or "wheel").

LDA then tries to reverse this process, inferring the underlying topic distributions for documents and word distributions for topics. It uses Dirichlet priors to ensure these distributions are sparse and well-behaved.

Non-negative Matrix Factorization (NMF)

NMF is a linear algebra technique. It takes a document-term matrix (where rows are documents, columns are words, and values are word counts or TF-IDF scores) and factorizes it into two smaller matrices:

  1. A document-topic matrix (rows are documents, columns are topics).
  2. A topic-word matrix (rows are topics, columns are words).

The key constraint is that all values in these matrices must be non-negative. This makes the resulting "parts" (topics and their word weights) interpretable as additive components, much like how a document is an additive combination of topics.

BERTopic

BERTopic is a more recent, embedding-based approach that leverages advancements in neural networks:

  1. Embeddings: Uses pre-trained language models (like BERT) to convert each document into a high-dimensional numerical vector (embedding).
  2. Dimensionality Reduction: Applies techniques like UMAP to reduce the dimensionality of these embeddings while preserving their local structure.
  3. Clustering: Uses HDBSCAN to cluster the reduced embeddings. Each cluster is considered a topic.
  4. Topic Representation: Extracts characteristic words for each cluster (topic) using a TF-IDF-like approach (c-TF-IDF).

This approach often yields higher quality, more coherent topics, especially for shorter or more nuanced documents, and doesn't require pre-defining the number of topics.

🔑 Key Differences

LDA is probabilistic and generative, NMF is algebraic and decompositional, and BERTopic is embedding-based and clustering-driven. Each has strengths depending on data characteristics and interpretability goals.

3
Why It Works — The Math
▼

Non-negative Matrix Factorization (NMF)

Given a document-term matrix \(V\) of size \(M \times N\) (M documents, N words), NMF aims to find two matrices, \(W\) (M documents, K topics) and \(H\) (K topics, N words), such that:

$$V \approx WH$$

where \(K\) is the predefined number of topics, and all elements in \(W\) and \(H\) are non-negative (\(W_{ij} \ge 0, H_{jk} \ge 0\)). This approximation is typically achieved by minimizing an objective function, often the Frobenius norm of the difference:

$$\min_{W,H} \|V - WH\|_F^2$$

subject to the non-negativity constraints. The columns of \(W\) represent the topic mixtures for each document, and the rows of \(H\) represent the word distributions for each topic. The non-negativity ensures that topics are additive and interpretable.

Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA)

LDA models a document \(d\) as a mixture of topics, and each topic \(z\) as a mixture of words. The probability of observing a word \(w\) in a document \(d\) is given by:

$$P(w|d) = \sum_{z=1}^{K} P(w|z) P(z|d)$$

Here, \(P(z|d)\) is the probability that document \(d\) belongs to topic \(z\), and \(P(w|z)\) is the probability that word \(w\) is generated by topic \(z\). These probabilities are drawn from Dirichlet distributions:

  • Document-topic distributions: \(\theta_d \sim \text{Dirichlet}(\alpha)\) for each document \(d\).
  • Topic-word distributions: \(\phi_z \sim \text{Dirichlet}(\beta)\) for each topic \(z\).

The goal is to infer \(\theta_d\) and \(\phi_z\) given the observed documents. This is typically done using variational inference or Gibbs sampling.

➗ Mathematical Intuition

NMF is like finding the "basis vectors" (topics) that can linearly combine to reconstruct your document-word data. LDA is like finding the "hidden causes" (topics) that probabilistically generate your observed words, guided by prior beliefs (Dirichlet distributions).

Key Takeaways
▼

When to use LDA:

  • You need a probabilistic interpretation of topic assignments.
  • You have a relatively small to medium-sized dataset.
  • You want a generative model for documents.
  • You are comfortable tuning hyperparameters like \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\).

When to use NMF:

  • You need a simple, interpretable decomposition of your document-term matrix.
  • Your data is sparse (many zero entries), which NMF handles well.
  • You prefer a linear algebraic approach over a probabilistic one.
  • You want to use topics as features for downstream tasks (e.g., classification).

When to use BERTopic:

  • You have a large, complex dataset with nuanced language.
  • You want to leverage state-of-the-art language models (like BERT) for better semantic understanding.
  • You don't want to pre-define the number of topics (HDBSCAN can discover them).
  • You need highly coherent and distinct topics.
💡 Choosing a Method

The "best" topic model depends on your data, computational resources, and specific goals. LDA and NMF are robust baselines, while BERTopic offers cutting-edge performance, especially with modern embeddings.

Code Trace

import numpy as np
from sklearn.feature_extraction.text import TfidfVectorizer
from sklearn.decomposition import NMF
from gensim.models import LdaModel
from gensim.corpora import Dictionary
from bertopic import BERTopic
# Sample documents
documents = [
"The car engine is powerful and fast on the road.",
"I love reading books, especially science fiction stories.",
"The road trip was long, but the car performed well.",
"This book has a great story with interesting characters.",
"Engines need regular maintenance to stay powerful."
]
# --- Preprocessing (TF-IDF) ---
vectorizer = TfidfVectorizer(stop_words='english')
V = vectorizer.fit_transform(documents) # Document-Term Matrix
feature_names = vectorizer.get_feature_names_out()
# --- NMF Topic Modeling ---
num_topics_nmf = 2
nmf_model = NMF(n_components=num_topics_nmf, random_state=42)
W = nmf_model.fit_transform(V) # Document-Topic Matrix
H = nmf_model.components_ # Topic-Word Matrix
print("NMF Topics:")
for topic_idx, topic in enumerate(H):
top_words_idx = topic.argsort()[:-10 - 1:-1]
top_words = [feature_names[i] for i in top_words_idx]
print(f"Topic {topic_idx+1}: {', '.join(top_words)}")
# --- LDA Topic Modeling ---
tokenized_docs = [doc.lower().split() for doc in documents]
dictionary = Dictionary(tokenized_docs)
corpus = [dictionary.doc2bow(doc) for doc in tokenized_docs]
num_topics_lda = 2
lda_model = LdaModel(corpus=corpus, id2word=dictionary,
num_topics=num_topics_lda, random_state=42, passes=10)
print("\nLDA Topics:")
for topic_idx, topic_words in lda_model.show_topics(num_topics=num_topics_lda, num_words=10, formatted=False):
words = [word for word, prob in topic_words]
print(f"Topic {topic_idx+1}: {', '.join(words)}")
# --- BERTopic Topic Modeling ---
# BERTopic handles preprocessing internally, just needs raw documents
bertopic_model = BERTopic(verbose=False, nr_topics="auto") # nr_topics="auto" for HDBSCAN
topics, probs = bertopic_model.fit_transform(documents)
print("\nBERTopic Topics:")
for topic_id in bertopic_model.get_topics():
if topic_id == -1: continue # -1 is noise topic
topic_words = bertopic_model.get_topic(topic_id)
words = [word for word, score in topic_words[:10]]
print(f"Topic {topic_id+1}: {', '.join(words)}")
