1
Understanding Audio Generation
▼

What is it? Text-to-audio and text-to-music models turn a natural-language prompt — "a golden retriever barking while children laugh in a park" or "an uptempo funk bassline with a wah guitar" — directly into a raw waveform. Two families dominate: autoregressive token models (AudioGen, MusicGen) that generate discrete audio codes one step at a time, and latent diffusion models (AudioLDM) that iteratively denoise a compressed spectrogram.

Analogy: AudioGen/MusicGen are like a player piano punching one note-token at a time, strictly left to right, never revising what's already been punched. AudioLDM is the opposite: it scribbles an entire page of noise first, then makes many full passes over the whole page, slowly erasing static until the score for the whole piece emerges at once.

Summary: Neither model generates raw 16-48kHz samples directly — both first compress audio into a short sequence of discrete or latent "codes," and all the interesting generative modeling happens in that compressed space.

💡 If you remember one thing

A 10-second clip at 32kHz is ~320,000 raw samples — far too long to model token-by-token. A neural codec (EnCodec) or spectrogram VAE first shrinks this to a few hundred discrete tokens or a small latent grid. Generation (autoregressive or diffusion) happens entirely in that compressed space; a decoder then expands the result back to audio.

2
How It Works Internally
▼

EnCodec — the shared audio tokenizer

AudioGen and MusicGen both build on EnCodec, a convolutional encoder-decoder that downsamples raw audio (e.g. 32kHz) by a large factor (e.g. 640×) to a ~50Hz sequence of continuous latent frames. Each frame is then quantized by Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ): codebook 1 quantizes the frame, codebook 2 quantizes the *leftover residual*, codebook 3 quantizes what's still left, and so on for \(Q\) codebooks (typically 4, each with 1024–2048 entries). This yields \(Q\) parallel streams of discrete integer tokens per audio frame — coarse-to-fine, like JPEG's DC + AC coefficients.

MusicGen / AudioGen — autoregressive transformer + delay pattern

A decoder-only transformer, conditioned on a frozen text encoder (T5) via cross-attention, predicts these codebook tokens causally. Naively flattening \(Q\) codebooks over \(T\) frames needs \(Q \times T\) serial steps — far too slow. MusicGen's key trick is the delay (interleaving) pattern: codebook \(k\) is shifted right by \(k\) time steps before flattening, so at each transformer step the model emits exactly one token per codebook, each conditioned on the codebooks that causally precede it, in only \(T + Q - 1\) steps instead of \(Q \times T\).

AudioLDM — latent diffusion on a mel spectrogram

AudioLDM takes the opposite path: audio → mel-spectrogram → a small latent grid compressed by a spectrogram VAE (the audio analogue of Stable Diffusion's image VAE). A U-Net is trained to denoise this latent over many timesteps, conditioned on CLAP or text embeddings via cross-attention and classifier-free guidance. Sampling starts from pure Gaussian noise and iteratively denoises (often with only 25–200 DDIM steps at inference); the clean latent is decoded by the VAE back to a mel spectrogram, then a vocoder (HiFi-GAN) turns the mel into a waveform.

🔑 Design Decision: discrete tokens vs. continuous diffusion

MusicGen/AudioGen reuse the exact recipe that made text LLMs work — cross-entropy over a discrete vocabulary — giving exact likelihoods, easy conditioning, and natural extension to melody/chord conditioning. AudioLDM's diffusion process instead models a continuous latent, giving strong global coherence and non-autoregressive sampling, at the cost of needing dozens of full network passes over the *entire* spectrogram per generation.

3
Why It Works — The Math
▼

Residual Vector Quantization

Given a continuous encoder frame \(z \in \mathbb{R}^d\), RVQ recursively quantizes the residual:

$$r_0 = z, \qquad c_k = \underset{i}{\operatorname{argmin}} \; \lVert r_{k-1} - e_i^{(k)} \rVert_2, \qquad r_k = r_{k-1} - e_{c_k}^{(k)}$$

for \(k = 1, \dots, Q\), where \(e_i^{(k)}\) is the \(i\)-th entry of codebook \(k\). The quantized frame is \(\hat z = \sum_{k=1}^{Q} e_{c_k}^{(k)}\). Each successive codebook only has to represent the shrinking residual, so early codebooks capture coarse structure and later ones capture fine detail — exactly why the delay pattern can treat codebook order as a causal dependency.

Diffusion forward & reverse process

AudioLDM defines a forward noising process on the mel latent \(z_0\) with variance schedule \(\beta_1,\dots,\beta_N\), \(\alpha_t = 1-\beta_t\), \(\bar\alpha_t = \prod_{i=1}^{t}\alpha_i\):

$$q(z_t \mid z_0) = \mathcal{N}\big(z_t;\ \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\, z_0,\ (1-\bar\alpha_t) I\big)$$

Training minimizes the simplified noise-prediction objective, and sampling reverses one step at a time with classifier-free guidance strength \(w\):

$$\mathcal{L}_{simple} = \mathbb{E}_{z_0,\epsilon,t}\Big[\big\lVert \epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(z_t, t, \tau)\big\rVert^2\Big], \qquad \hat\epsilon = \epsilon_\theta(z_t,t,\varnothing) + w\big(\epsilon_\theta(z_t,t,\tau) - \epsilon_\theta(z_t,t,\varnothing)\big)$$
$$z_{t-1} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}\Big(z_t - \frac{\beta_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}}\hat\epsilon\Big) + \sigma_t \mathbf{z}, \quad \mathbf{z}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$$
⚠️ Common Pitfall

Low token cross-entropy or low diffusion MSE does not guarantee perceptually good audio — both losses are proxies. The field instead reports Fréchet Audio Distance (FAD) and CLAP score. On the RVQ side, unused ("dead") codebook entries silently cap fidelity unless commitment loss + EMA codebook updates are tuned carefully.

Key Takeaways
▼

When to reach for autoregressive codec models (MusicGen, AudioGen):

  • You need melody/chord conditioning, fast greedy/sampling generation, or streaming-style generation
  • You want an LLM-style training recipe (cross-entropy, exact likelihood) reused directly for audio
  • Text-to-music with strong controllability over structure is the priority

When to reach for latent diffusion (AudioLDM):

  • General text-to-audio (sound effects, ambience) where global coherence matters more than fine sequential control
  • You can afford dozens of full denoising passes instead of thousands of tiny AR steps
  • You want easy inpainting / style-transfer style edits, which diffusion supports naturally
💡 Hierarchy

Raw-waveform models (WaveNet) → codec-token autoregressive (AudioGen, MusicGen, over EnCodec RVQ) → latent diffusion (AudioLDM, over a mel VAE). Each generation compresses more aggressively before modeling, trading raw fidelity control for tractability and speed.

return frame if 0 <= frame < T else None
for s in range(S): # one transformer forward pass per s
logits = transformer(prev_tokens, text_emb) # (Q, vocab)

frame = emit(s, k)

# ---- AudioLDM-style: DDPM sampling loop on a mel latent ----
N = 1000
betas = np.linspace(1e-4, 0.02, N)
alphas = 1.0 - betas
alpha_bar = np.cumprod(alphas)
z = np.random.randn(*latent_shape) # z_N ~ N(0, I)
for t in reversed(range(N)):
eps_uncond = unet(z, t, text_emb=None)
eps_cond = unet(z, t, text_emb=tau)
eps = eps_uncond + w * (eps_cond - eps_uncond) # classifier-free guidance
mean = (z - betas[t]/np.sqrt(1-alpha_bar[t]) * eps) / np.sqrt(alphas[t])
z = mean + (np.sqrt(betas[t]) * np.random.randn(*latent_shape) if t > 0 else 0)
mel = vae_decoder(z) # latent -> mel spectrogram
waveform = hifigan_vocoder(mel) # mel -> raw audio
