BERTopic

Neural topic modelling via embedding, dimensionality reduction, clustering, and class-based TF-IDF

Advanced Classical ML · Topic Models O(n log n) clustering NLP
1
Understanding the Concept

What is BERTopic? BERTopic is a topic modelling technique that discovers recurring themes inside a large collection of documents — without any labels. It leverages pre-trained sentence embeddings (SBERT) to represent semantic meaning, then groups similar documents using density-based clustering, and finally names each group by the words that are statistically characteristic of that cluster.

💡 The core idea

Old topic models (LDA) treat words as bags with no order and no meaning outside the document. BERTopic lets a transformer read every document first, turning it into a rich 768-dimensional meaning vector, then clusters those vectors — finding groups of documents that talk about the same thing even when they use different words.

Real-world analogy. Imagine sorting a pile of 10,000 newspaper clippings. Instead of reading every word, you summarise each clipping into a Post-it note capturing its gist (= SBERT embedding), stick all Post-it notes on a large board, look for clusters of similar-looking notes (= HDBSCAN), and then scan only the notes inside each cluster to find the defining words (= c-TF-IDF). The resulting label for each cluster is the topic.

Key Concept

BERTopic decouples semantic grouping (handled by embeddings + clustering) from topic labelling (handled by c-TF-IDF). Either module can be swapped independently — this is the source of its modularity.

2
Pipeline — How It Works Internally

BERTopic is a 5-stage pipeline. Each stage is a pluggable module. Step through all five below.

📄
Stage 1 — Document Embedding (SBERT)
Each document \(d_i\) is mapped to a dense vector \(\mathbf{e}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{768}\) by a Sentence-BERT encoder. The encoder's pooling (mean/CLS) captures the semantic gist of the whole document.
Input: raw text corpus Output: matrix \(\mathbf{E} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times 768}\)
↓
🗜️
Stage 2 — Dimensionality Reduction (UMAP)
UMAP compresses 768-D embeddings to 2–10 dimensions, preserving local neighbourhood structure. Lowering dimensionality makes density estimation tractable for HDBSCAN and reduces the curse of dimensionality.
Input: \(\mathbf{E} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times 768}\) Output: \(\tilde{\mathbf{E}} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d_r}\), \(d_r \ll 768\)
↓
🔵
Stage 3 — Density-Based Clustering (HDBSCAN)
HDBSCAN groups the reduced embeddings into clusters of arbitrary shape. Crucially, it labels low-density points as noise (topic −1) rather than forcing them into a cluster — this yields cleaner, more coherent topics.
Output: cluster label \(c_i \in \{-1, 0, 1, \ldots, K\}\) per document
↓
📊
Stage 4 — Topic Representation (c-TF-IDF)
All documents in cluster \(k\) are concatenated into one "super-document" \(D_k\). c-TF-IDF then finds words that are frequent within \(D_k\) but rare across other clusters — the same intuition as TF-IDF but applied at cluster level.
$$\text{c-TF-IDF}_{w,k} = \frac{f_{w,k}}{|D_k|} \cdot \log\!\left(1 + \frac{A}{\sum_{k'} f_{w,k'}}\right)$$
Output: top-\(N\) keywords per topic
↓
🏷️
Stage 5 — (Optional) Topic Refinement
Merge near-identical topics using cosine similarity of c-TF-IDF vectors. Apply Maximal Marginal Relevance to diversify keywords. Or substitute a GPT/LLM representation model to write human-readable topic labels.
Output: final interpretable topics
⚠️ Design Decision — Why HDBSCAN, not k-means?

k-means requires specifying \(K\) in advance and assigns every point to a cluster, even outliers. HDBSCAN automatically determines the number of topics and marks noisy documents as topic −1, preventing low-quality documents from polluting real topics.

The Math Behind c-TF-IDF

The key mathematical innovation in BERTopic is class-based TF-IDF (c-TF-IDF). Let \(n\) = number of documents, \(K\) = number of topics, \(f_{w,k}\) = frequency of word \(w\) in cluster \(k\), \(A\) = average number of words per class.

$$\text{c-TF}_{w,k} = \frac{f_{w,k}}{|D_k|}$$

This is simply the within-cluster term frequency — how often word \(w\) appears relative to the cluster's total word count.

$$\text{c-IDF}_{w} = \log\!\left(1 + \frac{A}{\sum_{k'=1}^{K} f_{w,k'}}\right)$$

The IDF term penalises words that appear across all clusters (common words). \(A = \frac{1}{K}\sum_k |D_k|\) is the mean cluster word count (a normalisation constant to make IDF scale-invariant).

$$\text{c-TF-IDF}_{w,k} = \text{c-TF}_{w,k} \times \text{c-IDF}_{w} = \frac{f_{w,k}}{|D_k|} \cdot \log\!\left(1 + \frac{A}{\sum_{k'} f_{w,k'}}\right)$$

Numerical example. Suppose \(K=2\) topics, \(A=100\), and the word "gradient" appears 40 times in cluster 0 (\(|D_0|=80\)) and 2 times in cluster 1 (\(|D_1|=120\)):

$$\text{c-TF-IDF}_{\text{gradient},0} = \frac{40}{80} \cdot \log\!\left(1+\frac{100}{42}\right) = 0.5 \cdot \log(3.38) \approx 0.5 \times 1.217 = 0.609$$
$$\text{c-TF-IDF}_{\text{gradient},1} = \frac{2}{120} \cdot \log(3.38) \approx 0.0167 \times 1.217 = 0.020$$

So "gradient" scores 30× higher in the ML topic than in the climate topic — it will appear near the top of Topic 0's keyword list.

UMAP Objective (Simplified)

UMAP minimises the cross-entropy between the high-dimensional fuzzy simplicial set \(P\) (neighbourhood graph) and its low-dimensional approximation \(Q\):

\(\mathcal{L} = \sum_{e\in E} \left[ P_e \log\frac{P_e}{Q_e} + (1-P_e)\log\frac{1-P_e}{1-Q_e} \right]\)

This preserves local topology while allowing global compression — far better than PCA for non-linear manifolds such as document embedding spaces.

⚠️ Common Misconception

c-TF-IDF is NOT applied to individual documents — it operates on the concatenated super-document of each cluster. This is what makes it a "class-based" (cluster-level) statistic rather than a document-level one.

Key Takeaways
✅ When to Use BERTopic
  • Short or medium-length texts (tweets, news headlines, reviews) — SBERT handles these well
  • When the number of topics is unknown a priori — HDBSCAN auto-detects \(K\)
  • When semantic coherence matters more than speed
  • When you want a modular pipeline (swap UMAP for PCA, swap HDBSCAN for k-means)
  • Dynamic topics: track how a topic's language shifts over time
⚠️ Limitations
  • Very long documents degrade SBERT's fixed-length pooling
  • UMAP + HDBSCAN are non-deterministic — results vary across runs unless seeds are fixed
  • Computationally heavier than LDA at inference time
  • Noise cluster (−1) may be large if data is highly diverse
