1
Understanding the Challenge
▼

What is it? Generative AI has revolutionized image editing (e.g., Stable Diffusion, DALL-E). InstructPix2Pix (I2P) takes this further: given an input image and a text instruction ("make it a cyberpunk city"), it generates an edited image. The challenge arises when applying this to video: simply editing each frame independently with I2P results in severe temporal flickering and inconsistency, as each frame's edit is unique and unrelated to its neighbors.

TokenFlow is a technique designed to solve this temporal inconsistency. It enables generative models, like I2P, to produce videos where edits are smooth and coherent across time, making the generated content look natural and stable.

Analogy: Imagine you're editing a flipbook animation. If you edit each page individually, trying to "make the character smile" on every page, you might end up with a character whose smile jitters, disappears, and reappears across pages. That's naive frame-by-frame editing. TokenFlow is like having a master template for the character's smile that you carefully "stamp" onto the right place on each page, ensuring it stays consistent and smooth as the character moves.

💡 If you remember one thing

Applying image-to-image editing models (like InstructPix2Pix) to video frame-by-frame causes flicker. TokenFlow fixes this by explicitly propagating attention features (tokens) from previous or reference frames to guide the generation of the current frame, ensuring semantic consistency.

2
InstructPix2Pix & TokenFlow Mechanisms
▼

InstructPix2Pix (I2P) for Image Editing

I2P is a conditional diffusion model fine-tuned for instruction-guided image editing. It takes three inputs: (1) a noisy version of the input image, (2) the original input image (via a conditioning mechanism), and (3) a text instruction (e.g., "turn him into a robot"). It learns to denoise the image according to the instruction. The key is its training dataset: triplets of \((x_{original}, \text{instruction}, x_{edited})\) generated by prompting a large language model and a text-to-image model.

The model's internal architecture is a U-Net, where the text instruction and the input image are injected as conditions, often through cross-attention layers. These attention layers are crucial as they allow the model to "focus" on specific parts of the image based on the text prompt.

The Video Flicker Problem

When I2P is applied to each frame of a video independently, even for the same instruction, the stochastic nature of diffusion sampling and the subtle differences between frames lead to variations in the generated content. A small change in the background in one frame might cause a different "semantic interpretation" in the diffusion model, leading to objects flickering in color, texture, or even shape. This temporal discontinuity breaks the illusion of motion.

TokenFlow: Propagating Consistency

TokenFlow addresses this by leveraging the attention mechanism within the diffusion U-Net. Instead of generating each frame from scratch, TokenFlow propagates the attention features (often referred to as "tokens" in the context of transformers, representing semantic information at different spatial locations) from a reference frame (typically the previous frame) to the current frame. This propagation is guided by optical flow, which estimates pixel-wise motion between frames.

Specifically, the attention queries \((Q)\) and keys \((K)\) in the U-Net are modified. For a given frame \(t\), instead of computing \(Q_t\) and \(K_t\) entirely from \(x_t\), TokenFlow warps the \(Q_{t-1}\) and \(K_{t-1}\) from the previous frame using optical flow and blends them with the newly computed \(Q_t\) and \(K_t\). This ensures that the attention patterns for corresponding semantic regions across frames remain consistent, forcing the generative model to produce temporally stable edits.

🔑 Key Property

TokenFlow operates by enforcing consistency in the attention mechanism of the diffusion model. By propagating and blending attention features (tokens) across frames, it ensures that the model's "focus" and semantic understanding of corresponding regions remain stable, preventing flicker.

3
From Diffusion to Consistent Attention
▼

The Core of InstructPix2Pix: Conditional Diffusion

InstructPix2Pix builds upon the denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) framework. A DDPM learns to reverse a gradual noisy process, ultimately generating data from pure noise. In I2P, the denoising process is conditioned on both the input image \(x_{in}\) and a text instruction \(c_T\). The objective is to learn a noise predictor \(\epsilon_\theta\):

$$L_{I2P} = \mathbb{E}_{x_0, \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I), t \sim U(0,1)} [ || \epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, x_{in}, c_T) ||^2 ]$$

where \(x_t\) is a noisy version of the target image \(x_0\), and \(t\) is the timestep. The model learns to predict the noise \(\epsilon\) given the noisy image \(x_t\), the original input image \(x_{in}\) (often through concatenation or cross-attention), and the text instruction \(c_T\) (via cross-attention).

The Attention Mechanism in Diffusion U-Nets

Within the U-Net architecture of diffusion models, cross-attention layers are crucial for integrating conditioning information (like text prompts or input images). For a given feature map \(F \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times D}\) (where \(H, W\) are spatial dimensions, \(D\) is feature depth), the attention mechanism computes queries \(Q\), keys \(K\), and values \(V\):

$$Q = W_Q F, \quad K = W_K F, \quad V = W_V F$$

The output of the attention block is then given by \(\text{softmax}(QK^\top / \sqrt{D})V\). The \(Q\) and \(K\) matrices essentially encode the "semantic content" and "query" for different spatial locations. Temporal flicker arises when these \(Q\) and \(K\) features are inconsistent between frames.

TokenFlow: Propagating Attention Features

TokenFlow enforces temporal consistency by propagating the \(Q\) and \(K\) features (or "tokens") from a reference frame \(t-1\) to the current frame \(t\). Let \(Q_{t-1}, K_{t-1}\) be the attention features from the previous frame. For the current frame \(t\), we first compute an optical flow field \(\mathcal{F}_{t-1 \to t}\) that maps pixels from \(t-1\) to \(t\). We then warp the previous frame's features:

$$Q'_{t} = \text{Warp}(Q_{t-1}, \mathcal{F}_{t-1 \to t}), \quad K'_{t} = \text{Warp}(K_{t-1}, \mathcal{F}_{t-1 \to t})$$

These warped features \(Q'_t, K'_t\) are then blended with the newly computed features from the current frame \(Q_t, K_t\) (derived from \(W_Q F_t, W_K F_t\)). A common blending strategy is a simple convex combination:

$$Q_{t, \text{final}} = (1-\lambda)Q_t + \lambda Q'_t, \quad K_{t, \text{final}} = (1-\lambda)K_t + \lambda K'_t$$

where \(\lambda \in [0,1]\) is a blending factor. By using \(Q_{t, \text{final}}\) and \(K_{t, \text{final}}\) in the attention calculation, the semantic information from the previous frame is explicitly carried forward, making the generated edits temporally consistent.

⚠️ Common Pitfall

Optical flow estimation is never perfect. Errors in flow can lead to artifacts (e.g., "ghosting" or misalignment) when warping features. The blending factor \(\lambda\) is crucial: too high, and flow errors dominate; too low, and consistency is lost. Advanced methods use robust flow estimation or more adaptive blending strategies.

Key Takeaways
▼

When to use InstructPix2Pix + TokenFlow:

  • When you need to apply instruction-guided edits (e.g., "make it a watercolor painting," "change the car to red") to videos.
  • For tasks requiring high temporal consistency in generative outputs, such as video style transfer, object appearance modification, or scene manipulation.
  • When working with diffusion models where attention mechanisms play a critical role in semantic understanding and generation.

When NOT to use:

  • For simple, non-generative video edits (e.g., cropping, color grading) where traditional video editing tools are more efficient.
  • If the input video has extremely fast motion, severe occlusions, or large camera movements, which can make optical flow estimation unreliable. Poor optical flow leads to poor token warping and can introduce new artifacts.
  • If the desired edit fundamentally changes the identity or structure of objects in a way that breaks correspondence (e.g., making a car disappear and reappear as a boat). TokenFlow aims for consistent *edits* on *existing* content.
💡 Broader Implications

The core idea of propagating latent representations or attention features using optical flow is a powerful paradigm for achieving temporal consistency in various video generation and editing tasks, extending beyond InstructPix2Pix to other conditional generative models.
