End-to-End ASR: CTC vs Attention (LAS)

Two ways to turn a sequence of audio frames directly into text — no phonemes, no HMMs, no forced alignment
Advanced Speech / Audio Sequence-to-Sequence O(T·S) DP
1
Understanding End-to-End ASR
▼

What is it? Classical speech recognizers were pipelines: acoustic model → pronunciation dictionary → HMM alignment → language model, each trained separately. End-to-end (E2E) ASR replaces the whole pipeline with a single neural network that reads raw acoustic frames and outputs characters (or subwords) directly — trained with one loss function, end to end.

Analogy: Imagine transcribing a song by ear. One strategy (CTC, used by DeepSpeech and QuartzNet) is to guess a letter for every tiny audio slice independently, then squeeze out the duplicates and silences afterward — like typing one key per metronome tick and cleaning up the mess later. The other strategy (attention, used by Listen-Attend-Spell) is to listen to the whole clip first, then write the transcript one letter at a time, glancing back at whichever part of the audio is relevant for the letter you're currently writing — like a stenographer who can re-read their notes.

💡 If you remember one thing

CTC assumes each output frame is conditionally independent given the encoder states and repairs this with a blank symbol + collapsing function; attention-based LAS drops that independence assumption entirely and models the full joint distribution of the output sequence via an autoregressive decoder that looks back at the encoder with a learned soft alignment.

2
How It Works Internally
▼

CTC (Connectionist Temporal Classification) — DeepSpeech, QuartzNet

A shared encoder (stacked convolutions/RNNs) turns \(T\) audio frames into \(T\) hidden vectors. A linear+softmax head produces, at every single frame, a probability distribution over the vocabulary plus a special blank token \(\varnothing\). Because \(T\) (frames) is much larger than \(U\) (label length), most frames must emit blank or repeat the previous character. A deterministic collapse rule — merge adjacent repeats, then strip blanks — maps any of the exponentially many frame-level paths onto a single label string. Training marginalizes over all paths that collapse to the correct label using a forward-backward dynamic program, so no manual frame-to-letter alignment is ever needed.

Attention-based Seq2Seq — Listen, Attend and Spell (LAS)

The Listener is a pyramidal bidirectional LSTM encoder that halves the time resolution at each layer (reducing 1000s of frames to a manageable length). The Speller is an autoregressive RNN decoder: at each output step it forms a query from its previous hidden state, computes attention weights over all encoder frames, forms a context vector as their weighted sum, and predicts the next character conditioned on the context and all previously emitted characters. There is no blank token and no independence assumption — the chain rule handles the full sequence probability directly.

🔑 Design decision: why blank at all?

Without blank, CTC could not represent "no character emitted here" nor distinguish two consecutive equal letters (e.g. "ll" in "hello") from one letter stretched across frames — collapsing would wrongly merge them. Blank acts as a separator the collapse rule can strip out.

Why It Works — The Math
▼

CTC objective

Let \(\pi\) be a length-\(T\) frame-level path over the extended alphabet (vocabulary \(\cup\) blank), and let \(\mathcal{B}(\pi)\) be the collapse function. The CTC likelihood of label \(y\) marginalizes over every path that collapses to it, assuming per-frame conditional independence given encoder outputs \(h_{1:T}\):

$$P(y \mid h_{1:T}) = \sum_{\pi \in \mathcal{B}^{-1}(y)} \prod_{t=1}^{T} P(\pi_t \mid h_t), \qquad \mathcal{L}_{CTC} = -\log P(y\mid h_{1:T})$$

Computing this sum naively is exponential in \(T\); the forward algorithm computes it in \(O(T \cdot |y'|)\) via a DP recursion over the blank-interleaved label \(y' = [\varnothing, y_1, \varnothing, y_2, \dots, \varnothing]\):

$$\alpha_t(s) = \Big(\alpha_{t-1}(s) + \alpha_{t-1}(s-1) + [\,y'_s \ne \varnothing \wedge y'_s \ne y'_{s-2}\,]\,\alpha_{t-1}(s-2)\Big)\cdot P(y'_s \mid h_t)$$

Key Takeaways
▼
DimensionCTC (DeepSpeech, QuartzNet)Attention / LAS

AlignmentImplicit, monotonic, marginalized by DPLearned soft attention, not guaranteed monotonic

When to use CTC: low-latency streaming, long utterances, simpler/faster inference (no autoregressive loop), or as an auxiliary loss to speed up attention model convergence.

When to use LAS / attention: offline transcription where the whole utterance is available, tasks that benefit from strong implicit language modeling, or as the stronger base for further improvements (e.g. combined CTC+attention hybrid, or Transformer/Conformer variants).

💡 Modern synthesis

Many state-of-the-art systems (e.g. Espnet hybrid CTC/Attention, Conformer-Transducer) combine both: CTC as an auxiliary loss/regularizer that enforces monotonic alignment, plus an attention or transducer decoder for the final joint output distribution — getting fast convergence and strong accuracy simultaneously.
