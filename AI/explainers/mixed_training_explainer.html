Mixed Training: Text + Multimodal Batches

Interleaving text-only and multimodal data to teach vision without forgetting language

1
Understanding Mixed Training
▼

What is it? When you take a pretrained language model and teach it to also understand images, the naive approach — fine-tuning only on image-caption / multimodal data — causes the model to forget how to write and reason in pure text. Mixed training fixes this by continuing to show the model text-only batches (books, code, dialogue) interleaved with the new multimodal batches, in a controlled ratio, throughout the whole training run.

Analogy: Imagine a novelist who takes a year-long detour to learn photography. If they stop writing entirely during that year, their prose skills atrophy. If instead they write a few pages every single day while studying photography, they pick up the new skill without losing the old one. The "pages per day" is the mixing ratio.

💡 If you remember one thing

Catastrophic forgetting happens when gradient updates are dominated by one data distribution for too long. Mixed training keeps the optimizer's gradient signal anchored to the original text distribution by sampling each minibatch as text-only with probability \(1-r\) or multimodal with probability \(r\), where \(r\) is the mixing ratio. $$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = (1-r)\,\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim D_{\text{text}}}[\ell(\theta;x,y)] + r\,\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim D_{\text{mm}}}[\ell(\theta;x,y)]$$

2
How Mixed Training Works Internally
▼

The Data Pipeline

Two (or more) data loaders run concurrently: a text-only stream \(D_{\text{text}}\) (the original pretraining corpus, or a curated replay buffer) and a multimodal stream \(D_{\text{mm}}\) (image-text pairs, interleaved documents, video-caption pairs, etc.). At every optimizer step, a batch-type is drawn: \(b_t \sim \text{Bernoulli}(r)\). If \(b_t = 0\), a batch is pulled from \(D_{\text{text}}\); if \(b_t = 1\), from \(D_{\text{mm}}\). The two batch types can even be concatenated within a single physical batch (token-level mixing) rather than alternated (batch-level mixing) — both are used in practice (PaLI, Flamingo, Idefics, LLaVA-continual-pretrain all mix at the batch or shard level).

Design Decision: Why Not Just Freeze the Language Weights?

An alternative to mixing is to freeze the LLM backbone and only train new vision-adapter parameters (as in the original Flamingo gated cross-attention, or LLaVA's projector-only stage). This guarantees zero forgetting but also caps how deeply visual information can be fused into the model's reasoning — frozen backbones tend to plateau below what full fine-tuning can reach on visual reasoning benchmarks. Mixed training instead unfreezes everything but rate-limits the multimodal gradient's influence via \(r\), trading a small amount of forgetting risk for much deeper cross-modal integration.

Curriculum & Scheduling

In practice \(r\) is rarely fixed: many recipes ramp \(r\) up from 0 (pure text warm-start) to a target value over the first portion of multimodal training, then hold it constant. Some recipes anneal \(r\) back down near the end of training to "consolidate" language quality before release — mirroring how the novelist might spend the final week of the year writing more and photographing less, to make sure their voice is sharp again.

🔑 Key Property

The mixing ratio \(r\) is a direct knob on a Pareto frontier between language retention and multimodal capability. There is no free lunch: increasing \(r\) can only be "free" up to the point where the gradient from \(D_{\text{mm}}\) starts to systematically conflict with the gradient from \(D_{\text{text}}\) (negative transfer), after which language quality degrades — often sharply once a critical ratio is crossed.

3
Why It Works — The Math
▼

Mixture Objective

Mixed training minimizes a convex combination of two expected losses:

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta; r) = (1-r)\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{text}}(\theta) + r\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{mm}}(\theta)$$

The stochastic gradient at each step is an unbiased estimator of \(\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}(\theta;r)\) because the batch source is itself sampled with probability \(r\): \(\mathbb{E}[\hat{g}_t] = (1-r)\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\text{text}} + r\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\text{mm}} = \nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta;r)\).

Gradient Interference & the Forgetting Mechanism

Write the two per-domain gradients as \(g_{\text{text}} = \nabla\mathcal{L}_{\text{text}}\) and \(g_{\text{mm}} = \nabla\mathcal{L}_{\text{mm}}\). The change in text loss after one mixed step of learning rate \(\eta\) is, to first order:

$$\Delta \mathcal{L}_{\text{text}} \approx -\eta\,g_{\text{text}}^\top \big[(1-r)\,g_{\text{text}} + r\,g_{\text{mm}}\big] = -\eta(1-r)\|g_{\text{text}}\|^2 - \eta r \, (g_{\text{text}}^\top g_{\text{mm}})$$

Language loss improves only if the second term does not dominate — i.e. as long as \(g_{\text{text}}^\top g_{\text{mm}} \geq -\frac{(1-r)}{r}\|g_{\text{text}}\|^2\). When multimodal gradients are strongly anti-aligned with text gradients (\(g_{\text{text}}^\top g_{\text{mm}} \ll 0\), i.e. negative transfer) and \(r\) is large, this bound is violated and the text loss rises every step: this is catastrophic forgetting, and it is why the language-benchmark curve below has a knee rather than a straight line — small \(r\) is nearly free, but past a critical ratio the anti-aligned component overwhelms the shrinking \((1-r)\) weight on text.

⚠️ Common Pitfall

Choosing \(r\) purely by "what fraction of my tokens are multimodal in the wild" is a mistake — that conflates data availability with the optimal training mixture. Real pipelines tune \(r\) against held-out language and multimodal benchmarks jointly, exactly like the slider below: the correct value is wherever the joint (combined) score is maximized, not wherever either single benchmark is maximized alone.

Key Takeaways
▼

When to use mixed training:

  • Adapting a strong pretrained LLM into a vision-language model without sacrificing its text abilities (chat, reasoning, coding)
  • Any continual-learning setting where a new domain/task is added to a model that must retain old competencies
  • Full end-to-end fine-tuning (not just adapter/projector training), where forgetting risk is highest

When NOT to rely on it alone:

  • If text-only replay data is unavailable or low quality — mixing garbage text does not protect language ability
  • Extremely long multimodal-only training runs — even a good \(r\) accumulates drift over enough steps; periodic re-anchoring / lower learning rate on backbone helps
  • As a substitute for architectural protections (e.g. gated cross-attention, LoRA adapters) when zero forgetting is a hard requirement
💡 Hierarchy

Frozen backbone (zero forgetting, capped fusion) ⊂ Mixed training (tunable forgetting/fusion tradeoff via \(r\)) ⊂ Naive multimodal-only fine-tuning (maximum fusion speed, maximum forgetting risk). Mixed training is the practical middle ground used by nearly every modern VLM recipe.

Code Trace

import random
# r = mixing ratio: probability a given step uses a multimodal batch
r = 0.4
text_loader = InfiniteLoader(text_only_dataset, batch_size=B)
mm_loader = InfiniteLoader(multimodal_dataset, batch_size=B)
for step in range(num_steps):
# sample which stream feeds this step
if random.random() < r:
batch = next(mm_loader) # image-text pair(s)
loss = model.forward_multimodal(batch)
else:
batch = next(text_loader) # text-only tokens
loss = model.forward_text(batch)
loss.backward()
optimizer.step()
optimizer.zero_grad()
if step % eval_every == 0:
lang_score = evaluate(model, mmlu_or_similar)
mm_score = evaluate(model, vqa_or_similar)
# tune r on a small grid using these two curves
log(step, r, lang_score, mm_score)
