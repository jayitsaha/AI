Duplicate Detection & Deduplication

Finding — and merging — records that refer to the same real-world thing
Foundational Data Quality Entity Resolution O(n) with blocking + LSH
1
Understanding Deduplication
▼

What is it? Deduplication is the process of finding records — rows, documents, images, customer profiles — that represent the same real-world entity, even when they are not byte-for-byte identical. Two customer signups for "Jon Smith" and "Jonathan Smith" at the same address are almost certainly the same person, but a naive equality check (row1 == row2) will never catch it.

Analogy: Imagine sorting a box of family photos where some are exact reprints (easy — identical file hashes) and others are the same moment shot a second apart by two different cameras (hard — visually similar but not identical pixels). Exact-duplicate detection only catches the reprints. Real-world data is full of the second kind: typos, abbreviations, reformatted phone numbers, re-ordered words.

💡 If you remember one thing

Deduplication is a spectrum, not a binary: exact match (hash equality) → fuzzy match (similarity above a threshold, e.g. $$J(A,B) = \frac{|A \cap B|}{|A \cup B|} \geq \tau$$) → entity resolution (cluster all matching records, then merge into one canonical record).

How Deduplication Works Internally
▼

The Naive Approach Doesn't Scale

The obvious algorithm — compare every record to every other record — costs \(O(n^2)\) similarity computations. At \(n = 10\) million records that is \(5 \times 10^{13}\) pairs: computationally hopeless. Production pipelines instead run a funnel of cheap-to-expensive stages, discarding non-candidates as early as possible.

Stage 1 — Blocking

Partition records into blocks using a cheap key that true duplicates are very likely to share (same ZIP code, same first-3-letters-of-surname, same phone area code). Only pairs within the same block are ever compared. This turns \(O(n^2)\) into roughly \(O(n^2/k)\) for \(k\) blocks — often orders of magnitude fewer comparisons, at the small risk of missing a duplicate whose block key happens to differ (a "false negative" bucket miss).

Stage 2 — Shingling + Jaccard Similarity

To compare two strings fuzzily, break each into overlapping \(k\)-character substrings ("shingles" or \(k\)-grams), e.g. "smith" → {"smi","mit","ith"}. Two strings that share many shingles are similar even if never identical. Jaccard similarity quantifies the overlap: intersection size over union size.

Stage 3 — Edit Distance for Field-Level Fuzziness

Levenshtein (edit) distance counts the minimum insertions, deletions, and substitutions to turn one string into another — ideal for catching typos and small variants ("mgarcia" → "mgarcia99" is 2 insertions away).

Stage 4 — MinHash + LSH for Scale

Computing exact Jaccard for every within-block pair is still \(O(n^2)\) inside big blocks. MinHash compresses each shingle set into a small signature such that the probability two signatures agree at a given position equals the true Jaccard similarity. Locality-Sensitive Hashing (LSH) then bands the signatures and hashes each band into buckets — records that collide in any band become candidate pairs, which is the only \(O(n)\)-ish step needed before final exact verification.

🔑 Key Property

The pipeline is a funnel: blocking (cheap, coarse) → LSH candidate generation (cheap, approximate) → exact similarity verification (expensive, precise) → clustering (union matched pairs into groups) → canonicalization (merge each group into one golden record). Each stage discards non-matches so the next, more expensive stage only sees a small pool.

3
Why It Works — The Math
▼

Jaccard Similarity on Shingle Sets

For shingle sets \(A\) and \(B\):

$$J(A,B) = \frac{|A \cap B|}{|A \cup B|} \in [0,1]$$

\(J = 1\) means identical shingle sets; \(J = 0\) means no shared \(k\)-grams. A typical fuzzy-duplicate threshold is \(J \geq 0.3\)–\(0.5\) depending on how noisy the field is.

MinHash: Why the Minimum Hash Value Estimates Jaccard

Pick a random permutation \(\pi\) of the universe of all possible shingles, and define \(h_{\min}(S) = \arg\min_{x \in S} \pi(x)\). A beautiful fact: the probability that two sets get the same minimum element under a random permutation equals their Jaccard similarity:

$$\Pr[h_{\min}(A) = h_{\min}(B)] = \frac{|A \cap B|}{|A \cup B|} = J(A,B)$$

In practice we simulate many random permutations with independent hash functions \(h_i(x) = (a_i x + b_i) \bmod p\) and average agreement across \(n_h\) of them to get an unbiased, low-variance estimate of \(J(A,B)\) — using a fixed-size signature instead of the full (variable-size) shingle sets.

LSH Banding: Turning "Similar" into "Collides in a Bucket"

Split an \(n_h\)-row signature into \(b\) bands of \(r\) rows each (\(b \cdot r = n_h\)). Two documents become candidates if they match exactly in at least one full band. For true similarity \(s\), the probability of becoming a candidate is:

$$P(\text{candidate}) = 1 - (1 - s^r)^b$$

This S-curve is the whole trick of LSH: pairs with high \(s\) are caught with near-certainty, pairs with low \(s\) are almost never even compared — all without ever touching most of the \(O(n^2)\) pairs.

Levenshtein Edit Distance (Recurrence)

$$D(i,j) = \min \begin{cases} D(i-1,j) + 1 & \text{(delete)} \\ D(i,j-1) + 1 & \text{(insert)} \\ D(i-1,j-1) + \mathbb{1}[a_i \neq b_j] & \text{(substitute / match)} \end{cases}$$
⚠️ Common Pitfall

Blocking and LSH are recall-limiting, not precision-limiting: they can only cause you to miss a true duplicate (false negative), never to wrongly merge two distinct entities on their own — that risk comes from the similarity threshold \(\tau\) itself. Set \(\tau\) too low and unrelated records ("Jon Smith" vs "Joan Smith" vs a totally different "Smith") get fused into one bad canonical record.

Key Takeaways
▼

When to reach for each technique:

  • Exact hash match — cheap first pass; catches copy-paste duplicates and re-submitted forms
  • Blocking — always, before any pairwise comparison, once \(n\) exceeds a few thousand records
  • Jaccard / shingling — free-text fields: names, addresses, product titles, document bodies
  • Edit distance — short structured fields prone to typos: emails, IDs, phone numbers
  • MinHash + LSH — web-scale near-duplicate detection (crawled pages, plagiarism, image/document corpora) where even blocked comparisons are too many

Common pitfalls:

  • Choosing a blocking key that's too coarse (huge blocks, no speedup) or too strict (misses true duplicates across blocks)
  • Merging without keeping provenance — canonicalization should be reversible / auditable, not destructive
  • Treating similarity as transitive: A≈B and B≈C does not always imply A≈C ("chaining" can silently merge unrelated entities)
💡 Hierarchy

Deduplication (same source, same schema) ⊂ Record linkage (matching across two different sources) ⊂ Entity resolution (clustering + canonicalizing across many sources into one golden record per entity). Same core similarity + blocking + clustering toolkit, increasing scope.
