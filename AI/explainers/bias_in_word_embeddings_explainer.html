Bias in Word Embeddings

How gender and racial stereotypes get baked into vector space — and how we try to remove them
Intermediate NLP Responsible AI O(d) per word
1
Understanding the Concept
▼

What is it? Word embeddings represent words as vectors — lists of numbers — placed in a space where "similar" words end up near each other. That geometry is learned entirely from how words co-occur in huge text corpora (Wikipedia, news, books). The problem: those corpora reflect the world's social biases, so the geometry ends up encoding them too. The classic finding, from Bolukbasi et al. (2016), is that word2vec's analogy solver answers "man is to computer programmer as woman is to X" with X = homemaker.

Analogy: Imagine a survey of "who talks about what" scraped from decades of newspapers, then used to build a mental map of concepts. If newspapers historically paired "he" with "doctor" and "she" with "nurse" far more often than reality warrants, the resulting map will place "doctor" suspiciously close to "he" — not because doctors are inherently male, but because the training text was.

💡 If you remember one thing

Bias in embeddings is not a bug in the algorithm — it's a faithful reflection of biased training data, geometrically visible as a consistent gender/racial direction \(g\) in vector space. Debiasing means finding \(g\) and mathematically projecting it out of words that shouldn't be gendered.

2
How Bias Gets In — and How Debiasing Works
▼

Where the bias comes from

Models like word2vec (skip-gram) and GloVe learn a vector \(v_w \in \mathbb{R}^d\) for every word \(w\) such that words appearing in similar contexts get similar vectors (distributional hypothesis: "a word is characterized by the company it keeps"). Training text over-represents certain co-occurrences — "he" near "engineer", "she" near "nurse" — at rates that don't reflect any real property of engineering or nursing. The optimization has no notion of fairness; it just fits the statistics it's given, so it reproduces them faithfully, including the skew.

Detecting it: the gender direction

Bolukbasi et al. show the bias is not scattered randomly — it concentrates along a single, low-dimensional direction. Take several word pairs that differ mainly by gender (he–she, man–woman, king–queen, father–mother) and compute their difference vectors. These differences are nearly parallel; their top principal component (or simple average) defines a unit "gender direction" \(g\). Any word's bias is then its scalar projection onto \(g\): \(\operatorname{proj}(w) = v_w \cdot g\). Gender-neutral words like "doctor" or "programmer" should ideally have \(\operatorname{proj}(w) \approx 0\), but empirically they don't.

Removing it: neutralize and equalize

The hard-debiasing algorithm has two steps. Neutralize: for every word that should be gender-neutral (occupations, adjectives, etc.), subtract its component along \(g\), leaving only the orthogonal, "gender-free" part of the vector. Equalize: for word pairs that are legitimately gendered (grandmother/grandfather, actress/actor), force them to be equidistant from every neutral word by making their non-gender components identical and only their \(g\)-component differ — so no neutral word is accidentally closer to one member of the pair than the other.

🔑 Key Property

Neutralizing is an orthogonal projection: \(v_w' = v_w - (v_w \cdot g)\,g\) removes exactly the gender-aligned component while preserving all other semantic content, since \(v_w'\) is by construction orthogonal to \(g\).

3
Why It Works — The Math
▼

Defining the gender direction

Given \(k\) defining pairs \((w_i^{(m)}, w_i^{(f)})\) such as (man, woman), (he, she), (king, queen), compute the difference vectors and average (or take the PCA top component, for more pairs):

$$g = \frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k} \big(v_{w_i^{(m)}} - v_{w_i^{(f)}}\big), \qquad \hat{g} = \frac{g}{\|g\|_2}$$

The bias of any word \(w\) is its scalar projection onto this unit direction:

$$\operatorname{proj}_{\hat g}(v_w) = v_w \cdot \hat g$$

Neutralize

For a word \(w\) in the neutral set \(N\) (should carry no gender signal), remove the component parallel to \(\hat g\):

$$v_w' = v_w - (v_w \cdot \hat g)\,\hat g$$

This is orthogonal projection onto the hyperplane \(\hat g^{\perp}\). Proof that it works: \(v_w' \cdot \hat g = v_w\cdot\hat g - (v_w\cdot \hat g)(\hat g \cdot \hat g) = v_w\cdot\hat g - v_w\cdot\hat g = 0\), using \(\hat g \cdot \hat g = 1\). So the debiased vector is guaranteed to have exactly zero gender projection.

Equalize

For a legitimately gendered pair \((a,b)\) (e.g. grandmother/grandfather) and their mean non-gender component \(\mu = \tfrac{1}{2}\big[(v_a - (v_a\cdot\hat g)\hat g) + (v_b - (v_b\cdot\hat g)\hat g)\big]\), the equalized vectors are set to share \(\mu\) exactly and differ only along \(\hat g\), with magnitude chosen so both remain unit-norm:

$$a' = \mu + \sqrt{1-\|\mu\|^2}\,\hat g, \qquad b' = \mu - \sqrt{1-\|\mu\|^2}\,\hat g$$
⚠️ Common Pitfall

Hard debiasing only removes direct bias (the linear \(\hat g\) component). Gonen & Goldberg (2019) showed indirect bias survives: debiased "male" and "female" occupation words still cluster together and remain distinguishable by a classifier, because bias is smeared across many nonlinear directions, not just \(\hat g\). Debiasing embeddings does not guarantee debiased downstream models.

Key Takeaways
▼

Use debiasing when:

  • Embeddings feed directly into decisions (resume screening, search ranking, sentiment scoring) where stereotype leakage causes real harm
  • You need an auditable, geometrically-explainable bias metric (projection onto \(\hat g\))
  • You can enumerate the defining pairs / neutral word list for the bias axis you care about (gender, race, age, ...)

Limitations to keep in mind:

  • Only removes linear, known-direction bias — nonlinear and unlisted biases persist (Gonen & Goldberg, 2019)
  • Choosing the neutral-word list is itself a value judgment and can be incomplete or inconsistent
  • Modern contextual embeddings (BERT, GPT) encode bias context-dependently, so a single static \(\hat g\) per word is a simplification — analogous projection-based methods (e.g. INLP, sent-debias) are used instead
  • Debiasing can slightly hurt task performance if gender signal was legitimately useful (e.g. "he/she" pronoun resolution)
💡 Big Picture

Embedding debiasing treats bias as geometry you can measure and subtract. It's a useful diagnostic and a partial fix, but fairness is ultimately a property of data, labels, and downstream decisions — not just of one vector space.
