1
Understanding Text-to-Mel TTS
▼

What is it? A text-to-speech (TTS) system converts written text into an audible waveform. Modern neural TTS splits this into two stages: (1) a text-to-mel acoustic model turns phonemes into a mel-spectrogram — a time-frequency "image" of speech energy — and (2) a vocoder (e.g. HiFi-GAN, WaveGlow) turns that mel-spectrogram into raw audio samples. Tacotron 2 and FastSpeech 2 are the two most influential designs for stage (1), and they solve it in fundamentally different ways.

Analogy: Tacotron 2 reads the sentence aloud one instant at a time, deciding on the fly — based on everything spoken so far — exactly where to "look" next in the text via attention. It's flexible and natural-sounding, but occasionally loses its place (skipping or repeating a word), and it must think sequentially, instant by instant. FastSpeech 2 is more like a conductor who first writes down exactly how many beats every note gets (a duration per phoneme), then the whole orchestra performs the entire score simultaneously from that fixed timing sheet — fast and reliable, but it needs someone to have supplied the timings in advance.

💡 If you remember one thing

Tacotron 2 learns where to look via soft attention, one mel frame at a time (autoregressive). FastSpeech 2 predicts how long each phoneme lasts up front, then expands and decodes the entire spectrogram in a single parallel pass (non-autoregressive). Both output the same kind of object — a mel-spectrogram \(M \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times n_{mel}}\) — via opposite philosophies of alignment.

2
How It Works Internally
▼

Tacotron 2 — Autoregressive Attention Decoder

The encoder is 3 convolutional layers followed by a bidirectional LSTM, turning phoneme (or character) embeddings into contextual hidden states \(h_1, \dots, h_L\). The decoder is a 2-layer autoregressive LSTM equipped with location-sensitive attention: at every output timestep \(t\), it computes an alignment \(\alpha_t\) over the encoder states, forms a context vector \(c_t = \sum_i \alpha_{t,i} h_i\), and predicts one 80-dimensional mel frame. A small "prenet" bottleneck on the previous frame prevents the model from just memorizing its own output, forcing it to rely on the attention context. A learned stop token tells the model when to stop. A 5-layer convolutional postnet residually refines the raw mel output. Because every frame is conditioned on the previous frame and attention state, decoding is inherently sequential: \(T\) output frames require \(T\) sequential network evaluations.

FastSpeech 2 — Non-Autoregressive Duration Model

The encoder is a stack of Feed-Forward Transformer (FFT) blocks (self-attention + 1D convolution) over phonemes — fully parallel, no recurrence. A Variance Adaptor then predicts, per phoneme and entirely in parallel: (1) an integer duration \(d_i\) (how many mel frames phoneme \(i\) should last), (2) a pitch value, and (3) an energy value. Predicted pitch and energy are quantized, embedded, and added back into the hidden states — this directly fixes TTS's "one-to-many" problem (the same text can be spoken with many valid prosodies; without pitch/energy conditioning the model averages them into a blurry, over-smoothed mel output). The Length Regulator then deterministically repeats each phoneme's hidden vector \(d_i\) times, producing a frame-level sequence of length \(T = \sum_i d_i\) with zero recurrence. A mirrored stack of FFT blocks decodes this entire sequence into mel frames in one parallel forward pass.

🔑 Design Decision: Why Not Just Reuse Tacotron 2's Attention?

Attention is a soft, learned alignment computed sequentially at inference — and it can wander, causing repeated or skipped words. FastSpeech distills hard, monotonic duration targets from an external aligner (originally Tacotron 2's attention; FastSpeech 2 uses forced alignment, e.g. Montreal Forced Aligner, directly on audio) so the phoneme→frame mapping can be computed once and then expanded in parallel — trading a sliver of alignment flexibility for large gains in speed, robustness, and explicit prosody control.

3
Why It Works — The Math
▼

Tacotron 2: Location-Sensitive Attention

At decoder step \(t\), location-sensitive attention combines content and location information so the alignment progresses roughly monotonically:

$$e_{t,i} = w^\top \tanh\!\big(W s_t + V h_i + U f_{t,i} + b\big), \qquad f_t = F * \textstyle\sum_{j \lt t}\alpha_j$$
$$\alpha_t = \operatorname{softmax}(e_t), \qquad c_t = \sum_{i=1}^{L} \alpha_{t,i}\, h_i$$

where \(s_t\) is decoder state, \(h_i\) encoder states, and \(f_t\) a convolved history of past alignments (the "location" feature) that discourages jumping backward or stalling. Training minimizes:

$$\mathcal{L}_{Tacotron2} = \underbrace{\|M - \hat M_{pre}\|_2^2 + \|M - \hat M_{post}\|_2^2}_{\text{mel L2, before/after postnet}} + \underbrace{\text{BCE}(stop, \hat{stop})}_{\text{stop token}}$$

FastSpeech 2: Duration, Pitch, Energy Regression

The duration predictor regresses log-durations against integer targets \(d_i\) extracted by forced alignment (avoids the huge dynamic range of raw frame counts):

$$\mathcal{L}_{dur} = \big\| \log(d_i+1) - \hat{d}_i \big\|_2^2, \qquad \mathcal{L}_{pitch}, \mathcal{L}_{energy} \text{ analogous MSE terms}$$

The Length Regulator is a deterministic (non-parametric, no gradient-bearing weights) expansion operator:

$$\operatorname{LR}(H, D) = \big[\underbrace{h_1,\dots,h_1}_{d_1},\ \underbrace{h_2,\dots,h_2}_{d_2},\ \dots,\ \underbrace{h_L,\dots,h_L}_{d_L}\big], \qquad T=\textstyle\sum_i d_i$$

Total loss is additive over all four terms, all computed directly against ground-truth targets in one parallel pass — no teacher forcing loop is needed at either train or inference time:

$$\mathcal{L}_{FastSpeech2} = \mathcal{L}_{mel} + \lambda_1\mathcal{L}_{dur} + \lambda_2\mathcal{L}_{pitch} + \lambda_3\mathcal{L}_{energy}$$
⚠️ Common Misconception

"Non-autoregressive means no sequence modeling happens." False — FastSpeech 2 still models the sequence richly via self-attention and convolution over positions. Non-autoregressive only means the decoder does not condition each output frame on previously generated frames — the entire frame sequence is produced in one shot from the expanded hidden states, not frame-by-frame from its own past predictions.

Key Takeaways & Comparison
▼
DimensionTacotron 2 (AR, attention)FastSpeech 2 (NAR, duration)
AlignmentSoft, learned attentionHard, predicted durations
Decoder steps\(O(T)\) sequential\(O(1)\) parallel pass
RobustnessCan skip / repeat wordsMonotonic by construction
Prosody controlImplicit onlyExplicit pitch/energy/duration knobs
Training needsMel + stop-token onlyExternal duration targets (aligner)
Inference latencySlower, RNN-boundMuch faster, GPU-parallel

When to use Tacotron 2: research settings prioritizing peak naturalness/prosodic variety over speed, where occasional attention failures can be mitigated (guided-attention loss, forced-monotonic attention) or manually filtered.

When to use FastSpeech 2: production and low-latency serving (voice assistants, IVR, accessibility tools), or whenever you need explicit, controllable speech rate / pitch / energy — e.g. slowing speech down for clarity, or shifting pitch for a different speaking style.

💡 The Evolution

Tacotron 2 (implicit, sequential attention) → FastSpeech (duration distilled from a Tacotron-style teacher, still needs one) → FastSpeech 2 (durations from forced alignment directly on audio, plus pitch/energy conditioning, fully parallel end-to-end, no teacher network required). Each generation trades a little attention "elegance" for speed, robustness, and controllability.

h_expanded = length_regulator(h, d_hat) # repeat h_i, d_hat[i] times
mel = decoder_fft_blocks(h_expanded) # ALL frames decoded in ONE pass
# Loss terms (training)
