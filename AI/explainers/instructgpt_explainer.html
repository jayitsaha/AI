1
Understanding InstructGPT
▼

What is it? GPT-3 was trained to predict the next word on internet text — it is very good at continuing text, but not at doing what a user actually asks. InstructGPT (Ouyang et al., OpenAI 2022) is GPT-3 fine-tuned with Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) so that its outputs match what humans actually want: helpful, honest, and harmless responses to instructions.

Analogy: Imagine a brilliant but literal-minded intern who was only ever trained by reading random books, never told what a "good answer" looks like. RLHF is like a manager who (1) shows the intern example answers to imitate, (2) has the intern write several drafts and ranks them best-to-worst, and (3) coaches the intern with feedback until their instincts match the manager's taste — without letting the intern drift into gibberish that merely games the reviewer.

💡 If you remember one thing

InstructGPT is trained in three stages: (1) Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on human demonstrations, (2) a Reward Model (RM) learned from human preference rankings, and (3) Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) that maximizes the reward model's score while a KL penalty keeps the policy close to the SFT model: $$\max_\pi \; \mathbb{E}_{x,y\sim\pi}\big[r_\phi(x,y)\big] - \beta \, D_{KL}\!\big(\pi(y|x)\,\|\,\pi^{SFT}(y|x)\big)$$

2
How RLHF Works Internally
▼

Stage 1 — Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

Labelers write high-quality demonstrations for real prompts submitted to the OpenAI API (roughly 13k prompts). GPT-3 is fine-tuned on these \((prompt, response)\) pairs with the standard next-token cross-entropy loss. This gives a policy \(\pi^{SFT}\) that already imitates good instruction-following behavior, but it is expensive to scale — every new behavior needs a fresh human-written example.

Stage 2 — Reward Model (RM) Training

Instead of writing more demonstrations, labelers now just rank \(K=4\)–\(9\) outputs sampled from \(\pi^{SFT}\) for the same prompt, from best to worst. A reward model \(r_\phi\) (a GPT-3 model with the unembedding layer replaced by a scalar head) is trained so that it assigns a higher score to the response a labeler preferred. Ranking is far cheaper to collect than writing full answers, so this scales human oversight much more efficiently than pure SFT.

Stage 3 — PPO Fine-Tuning

The reward model now acts as an automatic, learned proxy for "what humans want." The SFT policy is further optimized with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) to maximize \(r_\phi\), but with a per-token KL penalty against \(\pi^{SFT}\) so it cannot drift into text that scores high on the reward model while being incoherent or exploiting reward-model blind spots. This is often called "PPO-ptx" when a language-modeling loss on the original pretraining data is mixed in to prevent capability regressions.

🔑 Design decision: why not just do more SFT?

SFT can only teach behaviors humans explicitly demonstrate. RLHF instead teaches a reward function from comparative judgments — which are much easier and cheaper for humans to give than gold-standard writing — and then lets the policy explore and be optimized against that learned signal at scale.

3
Why It Works — The Math
▼

The Reward Model Loss (Bradley-Terry)

For a prompt \(x\) with two sampled completions \(y_w\) (preferred/"win") and \(y_l\) (rejected/"lose"), the reward model is trained with the pairwise logistic loss derived from the Bradley-Terry preference model:

$$\mathcal{L}(\phi) = -\,\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)\sim D}\Big[\log \sigma\big(r_\phi(x,y_w) - r_\phi(x,y_l)\big)\Big]$$

Minimizing this loss pushes \(r_\phi(x,y_w)\) above \(r_\phi(x,y_l)\); the sigmoid \(\sigma\) converts the reward gap into the probability that a human would have preferred \(y_w\). With \(K\) ranked outputs, all \(\binom{K}{2}\) pairs consistent with the ranking are used as training pairs in a single forward pass.

The PPO-with-KL-Penalty Objective

The RL objective maximized by PPO adds a per-token KL penalty to keep the new policy \(\pi_\theta\) close to \(\pi^{SFT}\):

$$\text{objective}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim \pi_\theta}\Big[r_\phi(x,y) - \beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y|x)}{\pi^{SFT}(y|x)}\Big] + \gamma\,\mathbb{E}_{x\sim D_{pretrain}}\big[\log \pi_\theta(x)\big]$$

The KL term is itself an expected log-ratio, i.e. exactly \(D_{KL}(\pi_\theta \| \pi^{SFT})\) in expectation. It serves two purposes: it is a variance-reducing regularizer (keeps PPO's trust region well-behaved) and it directly combats reward hacking — without it, the policy can find degenerate text that the reward model over-scores but humans would rate poorly. The optional third term (\(\gamma\), "PPO-ptx") mixes in the original pretraining log-likelihood to preserve general capabilities.

⚠️ Common Pitfall

The reward model is only a proxy for human preference, trained on a finite, imperfect sample of comparisons. Push \(\beta\) too low and PPO will over-optimize this proxy — Goodhart's law in action: "when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure." Push \(\beta\) too high and the policy barely moves from \(\pi^{SFT}\), forfeiting the alignment gains.

5
Key Takeaways
▼

What made InstructGPT work:

  • A 1.3B-parameter InstructGPT model was preferred by human raters over the 175B-parameter base GPT-3 — alignment can matter more than raw scale for instruction-following quality.
  • Cheap comparative human feedback (rank these 4 outputs) scales much better than expensive demonstration-writing.
  • The KL penalty against the SFT policy is what keeps PPO from degenerating into reward-model-gaming gibberish.
  • Mixing in the pretraining loss (PPO-ptx) reduces the "alignment tax" — performance regressions on standard NLP benchmarks.

Limitations: The reward model reflects the preferences of the specific labeler population, not humanity at large; it can still be gamed with enough optimization pressure; and RLHF does not guarantee truthfulness, only outputs that human raters tend to like.

💡 Legacy

InstructGPT's recipe — SFT → Reward Model → PPO — became the template for ChatGPT and most subsequent aligned LLMs, and directly motivated simpler successors like DPO that skip the explicit reward model and PPO loop by optimizing the same KL-regularized objective in closed form.
