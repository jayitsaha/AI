Understanding Time Series Foundation Models
▼

What is it? Until 2023, forecasting a new time series meant fitting a fresh model (ARIMA, Prophet, an LSTM) to that specific series. TimeGPT, Chronos and Moirai flip this: each is a single, large transformer pre-trained once on hundreds of thousands to millions of diverse series (retail, web traffic, energy, finance). At inference time you hand it a brand-new series it has never seen — zero-shot — and it forecasts immediately, the same way GPT-4 answers a question about a topic it was never explicitly fine-tuned on.

Analogy: A classical forecasting model is like a translator who only speaks the one language they studied for years. A time-series foundation model is like a polyglot who has absorbed thousands of languages and can produce a passable translation of a brand-new dialect on the spot — not perfect, but remarkably good with zero preparation, and improvable with a little fine-tuning.

One-sentence summary: Treat a numeric time series the way an LLM treats a sentence — as a sequence of tokens to be modeled and extended — and pre-train that sequence model on massive, diverse historical data so it generalizes to new series without retraining.

💡 If you remember one thing

The three models differ mainly in how they turn numbers into tokens: Chronos quantizes values into a fixed vocabulary and reuses a text-LM (T5) verbatim; Moirai keeps values continuous and embeds patches with an any-variate attention mechanism; TimeGPT is a closed, continuous-value transformer served only through an API.

2
How They Work Internally
▼

Chronos (Amazon, 2024) — "forecasting is language modeling"

Chronos scales each series with instance normalization, then quantizes the scaled values into one of ~4096 bins, turning the series into a sequence of discrete integer tokens — exactly like BPE turns text into token IDs. It then reuses an off-the-shelf T5 encoder-decoder unchanged, trained with the standard cross-entropy language-modeling loss over the token vocabulary. Forecasting becomes autoregressive decoding: sample the next token, dequantize it back to a real number, repeat.

Moirai (Salesforce, 2024) — "any-variate, any-frequency"

Moirai never discretizes values. Instead it slices each series into non-overlapping patches (e.g. 8–128 points depending on frequency) and linearly projects each patch into a continuous embedding — a "patch token", borrowed from PatchTST/ViT. Multiple variates of the same multivariate series are flattened into one sequence and attend to each other with an any-variate attention mechanism (binary relation-aware bias, not fixed variate ordering), so one model handles univariate or multivariate inputs of arbitrary frequency. The output head predicts a mixture of parametric distributions (e.g. Student-t + negative-binomial + log-normal components) per patch, so it never commits to one noise assumption.

TimeGPT (Nixtla, 2023) — closed, continuous, API-only

TimeGPT is proprietary: architecture details are not fully published, but it is a continuous-value transformer (no quantization) trained on Nixtla's large aggregated corpus of public and commercial series, exposed only through a hosted API call that returns point forecasts plus conformal-style prediction intervals, optionally conditioned on exogenous covariates.

🔑 Design decision: discretize or not?

Quantizing (Chronos) lets you reuse a battle-tested text-LM architecture and loss as-is, at the cost of a fixed resolution (quantization error). Staying continuous (Moirai, TimeGPT) needs a custom regression/distribution head but avoids that error entirely and is friendlier to very large or very small magnitudes.

3
Why It Works — The Math
▼

Instance normalization (all three models)

Given context window \(y_1,\dots,y_N\), compute \(\mu = \frac{1}{N}\sum_t y_t\), \(\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_t (y_t-\mu)^2}\), and scale \(z_t = (y_t-\mu)/\sigma\). This makes a model trained on retail sales and one trained on server CPU load comparable in the same numeric range.

$$z_t = \frac{y_t - \mu}{\sigma}$$

Chronos quantization

With \(B\) bins spanning \([z_{\min}, z_{\max}]\), the token id for value \(z\) is:

$$\text{tok}(z) = \operatorname{clip}\!\left(\operatorname{round}\!\left(\frac{z - z_{\min}}{z_{\max}-z_{\min}}(B-1)\right),\,0,\,B-1\right)$$

Dequantizing (bin center) back to a real number introduces a quantization error whose expected magnitude shrinks as \(1/B\) — more bins, less error, but a larger softmax output layer.

Causal / any-variate attention

Both the T5 decoder in Chronos and the any-variate encoder in Moirai score how much position \(i\) should attend to position \(j\) and normalize with softmax:

$$\alpha_{ij} = \frac{\exp(s_{ij})}{\sum_k \exp(s_{ik})} \qquad \hat{z}_i = \sum_j \alpha_{ij}\, z_j$$

Prediction intervals widen with the square-root of horizon

Under a local-trend + seasonal decomposition with i.i.d. residual variance \(\sigma_0^2\), the variance of a random-walk-style \(h\)-step-ahead forecast grows linearly, so the standard deviation grows as \(\sqrt{h}\):

$$\hat{y}_{N+h} = \hat{\ell} + \hat{\beta}h + \hat{s}_{(N+h)\bmod p}, \qquad \sigma_h = \sigma_0\sqrt{h}, \qquad \text{PI}_h = \hat{y}_{N+h} \pm z_{\alpha/2}\,\sigma_h$$
⚠️ Common pitfall

Zero-shot does not mean assumption-free. All three models still assume the new series' dynamics resemble something in the pre-training distribution. A series with a genuine regime break (a policy change, a pandemic-style shock) will fool all of them the same way it would fool a classical model — foundation models reduce variance from small-sample-per-series fitting, they do not repeal the fact that the future can look nothing like the past.

Use TimeGPT when you want a managed API with minimal setup and are fine with a closed model. Use Chronos when you want open weights, simple univariate zero-shot forecasts, and are comfortable with a discretization step. Use Moirai when your data is genuinely multivariate, mixed-frequency, or you need calibrated probabilistic output without picking a single noise distribution.

💡 Hierarchy of ideas

All three inherit the same lesson from LLMs: scale + diverse pre-training data + a sequence-modeling objective generalizes better than a bespoke model fit to one series. The remaining design space is just how to tokenize a real number.
