1
Understanding RLHF
▼

What is it? Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) is a technique to align large language models (LLMs) with human values and instructions. It's designed to make models more helpful, harmless, and honest by training them directly on human preferences, rather than just predicting the next token in a vast text corpus.

Analogy: Imagine you're teaching a highly intelligent but naive robot how to write helpful emails. Initially, it just mimics all the emails it's ever read. RLHF is like giving the robot specific feedback: "This email is better than that one because it's more concise," or "This response is harmful, avoid it." Over time, the robot learns to write emails that not only sound natural but also truly satisfy your preferences, even for new instructions.

💡 If you remember one thing

RLHF involves three main steps: (1) Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) of a pre-trained LLM on human-written demonstrations, (2) training a Reward Model (RM) to predict human preferences, and (3) using Reinforcement Learning (RL) (e.g., PPO) to optimize the SFT model against the RM's feedback, with a KL-divergence penalty to prevent policy drift.

2
How It Works Internally
▼

Step 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

The process begins with a pre-trained base LLM (e.g., GPT-3). This model has learned vast amounts of language patterns but isn't necessarily good at following specific instructions or being aligned. We collect a dataset of prompts and high-quality, human-written responses that exemplify desired behavior. The base LLM is then fine-tuned on this dataset using standard supervised learning (e.g., next-token prediction with cross-entropy loss). This SFT model becomes proficient at generating responses that are generally helpful and follow instructions, serving as a good starting point for further alignment.

Step 2: Training a Reward Model (RM)

To scale human feedback, we train a separate model, the "Reward Model." This model's job is to predict how much a human would prefer a given response. The training data for the RM is collected by taking prompts, generating several different responses from the SFT model, and asking humans to rank these responses from best to worst. The RM is typically another LLM (often a smaller version of the SFT model) that takes a prompt and a response as input and outputs a scalar score representing human preference. It's trained to accurately predict these human rankings.

Step 3: Reinforcement Learning (RL) with PPO

Finally, the SFT model is further fine-tuned using Reinforcement Learning. The SFT model acts as the "policy" (generating actions/responses), and the trained Reward Model acts as the "reward function" (providing feedback). Prompts are sampled, the policy generates responses, and the RM assigns a reward score to each response. An RL algorithm, commonly Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), is used to update the policy to maximize these rewards. A crucial component is a KL-divergence penalty, which prevents the policy from drifting too far from the original SFT model, ensuring it maintains fluency and doesn't "reward hack" by generating nonsensical but high-scoring outputs.

Design Decision: Why a separate Reward Model?

Directly using human feedback for RL would be prohibitively slow and expensive. The RM acts as a scalable proxy for human judgment, allowing the RL agent to receive millions of "preference scores" without needing constant human intervention. It effectively distills human preferences into an automated feedback loop.

🔑 Key Property

The Reward Model is the critical bridge, translating sparse, qualitative human feedback into a dense, quantitative signal that the RL algorithm can optimize against. The KL-divergence penalty is equally vital for maintaining model coherence and preventing catastrophic forgetting or undesirable behavior.

3
Why It Works — The Math
▼

1. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) Loss

The initial SFT step optimizes the language model's parameters \(\theta\) using a standard cross-entropy loss on a dataset \(D_{SFT}\) of prompts \(x\) and human-written responses \(y\):

$$L_{SFT}(\theta) = -\mathbb{E}_{(x,y) \sim D_{SFT}}[\log P_\theta(y|x)]$$

This trains the model to generate high-quality, instruction-following text, providing a strong foundation for the subsequent RL phase.

2. Reward Model (RM) Training Loss

The Reward Model \(\phi\) learns to predict human preferences. Given a prompt \(x\) and two responses \(y_k\) and \(y_j\), where \(y_k\) is preferred over \(y_j\), the RM is trained with a pairwise ranking loss. It aims to make the score for the preferred response higher than the score for the less preferred one:

$$L_{RM}(\phi) = -\mathbb{E}_{(x, y_k, y_j) \sim D_{RM}}[\log(\sigma(r_\phi(x, y_k) - r_\phi(x, y_j)))]$$

Here, \(r_\phi(x, y)\) is the scalar reward predicted by the RM for response \(y\) given prompt \(x\), and \(\sigma\) is the sigmoid function. This loss maximizes the probability that the preferred response gets a higher score.

3. Reinforcement Learning (RL) with PPO Objective

The SFT model (now the policy \(\pi_\theta\)) is fine-tuned using PPO to maximize the rewards from the RM. The PPO objective function balances maximizing the RM reward with a KL-divergence penalty to prevent the policy from deviating too much from the original SFT model \(\pi_{ref}\) (which is the initial SFT model before RL):

$$L_{PPO}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{(x, y) \sim D_{RL}}[\min(r_t(\theta) A_t, \text{clip}(r_t(\theta), 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon) A_t) - \beta D_{KL}(P_\theta || P_{ref})]$$

Where \(r_t(\theta) = P_\theta(y|x) / P_{old}(y|x)\) is the ratio of the new policy's probability to the old policy's probability for generating response \(y\), \(A_t\) is the advantage function (often estimated using the RM score), \(\epsilon\) is the PPO clipping parameter, and \(\beta\) is the coefficient for the KL-divergence penalty. The KL penalty ensures the model doesn't sacrifice coherence or safety for higher RM scores.

⚠️ Common Pitfall

The quality of the Reward Model is paramount. If the RM is poorly trained, biased, or susceptible to "reward hacking" (where the model generates outputs that score highly but are not truly aligned with human intent), the entire RLHF pipeline can fail, leading to misaligned or even harmful models.

Key Takeaways
▼

When to use RLHF:

  • When fine-tuning large language models to align with complex, subjective human preferences (e.g., helpfulness, safety, writing style).
  • To reduce undesirable behaviors (e.g., toxicity, hallucination) that are hard to specify with explicit rules.
  • To make models better at following open-ended instructions and engaging in dialogue.
  • When supervised fine-tuning alone doesn't capture the nuances of human judgment.

When NOT to use / Challenges:

  • When human feedback is scarce, expensive, or inconsistent. RLHF relies heavily on high-quality human data.
  • For tasks where objective metrics are sufficient and simpler fine-tuning methods achieve desired performance.
  • If the Reward Model is misaligned or easily "gamed," the RL policy can optimize for unintended behaviors.
  • The computational cost is significantly higher than basic supervised fine-tuning due to the multi-stage process and RL training.
💡 Big Picture

RLHF is a powerful paradigm for injecting human values into AI systems, moving beyond purely data-driven learning. It's a key technique behind the success of models like InstructGPT and ChatGPT, enabling them to be more useful and safe in real-world interactions.

Code Trace (Conceptual PyTorch-like)

import torch
from transformers import AutoModelForCausalLM, AutoTokenizer
from trl import SFTTrainer, RewardTrainer, PPOTrainer
from datasets import load_dataset
# --- 0. Load Pre-trained Base LLM ---
base_model = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained("gpt2")
tokenizer = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained("gpt2")
# --- 1. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) ---
sft_dataset = load_dataset("local_sft_data.json") # Prompts + human responses
sft_trainer = SFTTrainer(
model=base_model,
train_dataset=sft_dataset,
tokenizer=tokenizer,
max_seq_length=512,
)
sft_trainer.train()
sft_model = sft_trainer.model # This is now the SFT-tuned policy
# --- 2. Train Reward Model (RM) ---
reward_model = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained("gpt2-small") # Smaller LLM
rm_dataset = load_dataset("local_rm_data.json") # Prompts + ranked responses
rm_trainer = RewardTrainer(
model=reward_model,
tokenizer=tokenizer,
train_dataset=rm_dataset,
)
rm_trainer.train()
