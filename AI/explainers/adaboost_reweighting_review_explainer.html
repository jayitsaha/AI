AdaBoost: Sample Reweighting & Exponential Loss

How misclassified examples grow heavier each round, and why the ensemble minimises exponential loss
Intermediate Classical ML Boosting O(T · n · d)
1
The Core Idea
▼

What is AdaBoost? Adaptive Boosting builds a strong classifier from a sequence of weak learners — each trained on a reweighted version of the dataset. After round \(t\), examples the ensemble still gets wrong receive a higher weight, so the next learner focuses on the hard cases. The final answer is a confidence-weighted vote:

$$H(x) = \text{sign}\!\left(\sum_{t=1}^T \alpha_t h_t(x)\right)$$

Analogy: Imagine a study group where, after each practice test, you spend proportionally more time on the questions you missed. Each tutor (weak learner) specialises in a different subset of hard questions. The final exam score is a weighted consensus, trusting tutors who were more accurate.

💡 One-line summary

AdaBoost reweights samples so each new weak learner attacks the previous mistakes; the weight \(\alpha_t = \tfrac{1}{2}\ln\tfrac{1-\varepsilon_t}{\varepsilon_t}\) is the closed-form step that minimises exponential loss \(\sum_i e^{-y_i F(x_i)}\) at round \(t\).

2
Algorithm & Weight Update Rule
▼

Initialisation

Begin with uniform weights: \(w_i^{(1)} = 1/n\) for all \(i\). Every example is treated equally at the start — AdaBoost has no prior belief about which examples are hard.

Per-Round Update

At round \(t\):

  1. Train \(h_t = \arg\min_{h} \sum_i w_i^{(t)} \mathbf{1}[h(x_i) \neq y_i]\) on the weighted dataset.
  2. Compute weighted error: \(\varepsilon_t = \sum_i w_i^{(t)} \mathbf{1}[h_t(x_i) \neq y_i]\).
  3. Compute learner weight: \(\alpha_t = \tfrac{1}{2}\ln\tfrac{1-\varepsilon_t}{\varepsilon_t}\).
  4. Update sample weights: \(w_i^{(t+1)} \propto w_i^{(t)} \exp\!\bigl(-\alpha_t y_i h_t(x_i)\bigr)\).
  5. Renormalise: divide all \(w_i^{(t+1)}\) by their sum.

Why does the exponent work?

When \(h_t\) is correct: \(y_i h_t(x_i) = +1\), so the exponent is \(-\alpha_t < 0\), shrinking the weight. When wrong: \(y_i h_t(x_i) = -1\), exponent is \(+\alpha_t > 0\), growing the weight. A confident weak learner (small \(\varepsilon_t\)) has large \(\alpha_t\), causing a bigger weight swing.

📐 Alpha formula decoded

\(\alpha_t = \tfrac{1}{2}\ln\tfrac{1-\varepsilon_t}{\varepsilon_t}\).
At \(\varepsilon_t = 0.01\): \(\alpha_t \approx 2.30\) (very confident, big influence).
At \(\varepsilon_t = 0.45\): \(\alpha_t \approx 0.10\) (barely better than random, small influence).
At \(\varepsilon_t = 0.5\): \(\alpha_t = 0\) (random, ignored entirely).

⚠️ Weak-learner requirement

AdaBoost requires each weak learner to achieve \(\varepsilon_t < 0.5\) on the weighted distribution at every round. If this fails (e.g. very noisy labels), the algorithm can diverge. The theoretical guarantee holds only under this assumption.

3
Exponential Loss & Why \(\alpha_t\) Has That Form
▼

AdaBoost constructs \(F(x) = \sum_t \alpha_t h_t(x)\) by minimising the exponential loss:

$$L_{\exp}(F) = \sum_{i=1}^n e^{-y_i F(x_i)}$$

At round \(T\), given \(F_{T-1}\) fixed, we minimise over \((\alpha_T, h_T)\):

$$L = \sum_i \underbrace{e^{-y_i F_{T-1}(x_i)}}_{\tilde{w}_i} \cdot e^{-y_i \alpha_T h_T(x_i)}$$

Split into correct (\(W_+\)) and incorrect (\(W_-\)) sets under \(h_T\):

$$L = e^{-\alpha_T} W_+ + e^{+\alpha_T} W_-$$

Differentiating w.r.t. \(\alpha_T\) and solving:

$$\frac{dL}{d\alpha_T} = -e^{-\alpha_T}W_+ + e^{+\alpha_T}W_- = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; \alpha_T = \frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{W_+}{W_-} = \frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{1-\varepsilon_T}{\varepsilon_T}$$

The unnormalised weights \(\tilde{w}_i\) accumulate across rounds as \(\prod_{t} e^{-\alpha_t y_i h_t(x_i)}\). Normalising gives exactly the reweighting rule in the algorithm. The sample weights are the unnormalised exponential loss per point.

🔑 Unifying view

AdaBoost is stagewise gradient descent in function space under exponential loss. The reweighting is not a heuristic — it is the exact steepest-descent direction. Gradient Boosting generalises this to any differentiable loss by fitting residuals instead of reweighting.

Training Error Guarantee

$$\frac{1}{n}\sum_i \mathbf{1}[H(x_i) \neq y_i] \;\leq\; \prod_{t=1}^T 2\sqrt{\varepsilon_t(1-\varepsilon_t)} = \exp\!\Bigl(-2\sum_{t=1}^T \gamma_t^2\Bigr)$$

where \(\gamma_t = 0.5 - \varepsilon_t > 0\) is each weak learner's edge over random. Training error decays exponentially fast even when each \(\gamma_t\) is tiny.

Practical Notes & Pitfalls
▼

Strengths

  • No hyperparameter tuning beyond \(T\) — step sizes are computed analytically.
  • Remarkably resistant to overfitting in clean datasets; margin theory explains why.
  • Feature selection is implicit: stumps pick the most discriminative feature each round.
  • Interpretable individual stumps; each \(\alpha_t\) tells you how much to trust each.

Weaknesses / Pitfalls

  • Noise sensitivity: Mislabelled examples get exponentially large weights, dominating training.
  • Outlier fixation: A single hard outlier can absorb most of the weight budget.
  • Slow on large datasets: Each round is \(O(n \cdot d)\); \(T=500\) means 500 full passes.
  • Weak learner requirement: Every round must beat 50% on the current weighting — hard if data is very noisy.
💡 When to use AdaBoost vs XGBoost

AdaBoost: clean labels, interpretability valued, few features, educational baseline. XGBoost/LightGBM: large datasets, noisy labels, need for regularisation, production systems. Both are boosting; the key difference is loss function (exponential vs arbitrary) and tree depth (1 vs configurable).

Multiclass Extension: SAMME

For \(K > 2\) classes, the learner weight becomes \(\alpha_t = \ln\tfrac{1-\varepsilon_t}{\varepsilon_t} + \ln(K-1)\). The extra \(\ln(K-1)\) term compensates for the random baseline shifting from \(0.5\) to \(1/K\). SAMME.R uses class probability estimates instead of hard labels and typically converges faster.

6
Interview Quick-Fire
▼

Q: What loss does AdaBoost minimise?

Exponential loss \(\sum_i e^{-y_i F(x_i)}\). The reweighting update is not a heuristic — it is the exact closed-form step minimising this loss at each stage.

Q: Why is the weight update \(e^{-\alpha_t y_i h_t(x_i)}\)?

Correct: \(y_i h_t = +1 \Rightarrow e^{-\alpha_t} < 1\) (down-weight). Wrong: \(y_i h_t = -1 \Rightarrow e^{+\alpha_t} > 1\) (up-weight). The magnitude scales with \(\alpha_t\), so a more accurate learner causes a larger reweighting.

Q: Can AdaBoost overfit?

Yes, especially with noisy labels. Exponential loss gives unbounded weight to mislabelled points. In clean settings, training error keeps falling after 0% is reached (margins improve), delaying test-error degradation.

Q: How is AdaBoost related to gradient boosting?

AdaBoost is gradient boosting with exponential loss. The sample weights are the negative gradients of the exponential loss at each point. Gradient boosting replaces reweighting with fitting residuals (negative gradients), enabling arbitrary differentiable losses.

🔑 Exam anchor formula

$$\alpha_t = \frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{1-\varepsilon_t}{\varepsilon_t}, \quad w_i^{(t+1)} \propto w_i^{(t)} e^{-\alpha_t y_i h_t(x_i)}, \quad H(x) = \text{sign}\!\sum_t \alpha_t h_t(x)$$
