1
Understanding Leave-One-Out CV

Cross-validation estimates how well a model generalises to unseen data. Leave-One-Out CV (LOOCV) is the most extreme version: with \(n\) data points, you train \(n\) separate models, each time leaving exactly one point out as the test set and training on the remaining \(n-1\) points.

💡 Core Insight

LOOCV is K-fold cross-validation with K = n. Each "fold" has exactly 1 test point. The CV estimate is the mean squared error (or other loss) averaged across all n single-point test evaluations.

Analogy: Imagine grading a student's understanding by giving them 1000 different exams, each time withholding one practice problem from their study set and testing them on that held-out problem. You get a very precise estimate of their knowledge, but it costs 1000 grading sessions.

🔑 Key Formula

$$\text{LOO-CV} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(y_i - \hat{y}_{-i}\right)^2$$

where \(\hat{y}_{-i}\) is the prediction for point \(i\) from the model trained on all data except point \(i\).

2
How It Works — and the Hat Matrix Shortcut

The naïve algorithm requires fitting \(n\) models — \(\mathcal{O}(n)\) fits each of \(\mathcal{O}(n^2)\) or \(\mathcal{O}(n^3)\) cost. For linear models, a closed-form shortcut eliminates all re-fitting.

The Hat Matrix (Linear Models Only)

In ordinary least squares \(\hat{\mathbf{y}} = \mathbf{H}\mathbf{y}\) where the hat matrix is:

$$\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{X}(\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}^\top$$

\(H_{ii}\) — the \(i\)-th diagonal element — measures the leverage of point \(i\): how much influence it has on its own fitted value. This is the key to the shortcut.

⚡ LOOCV Shortcut (Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury)

For any linear smoother (OLS, ridge, polynomial, etc.) the LOO residual equals the ordinary residual scaled by one minus the leverage:

$$y_i - \hat{y}_{-i} = \frac{y_i - \hat{y}_i}{1 - H_{ii}}$$

So LOOCV collapses to a single model fit:

$$\text{LOO-CV} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{y_i - \hat{y}_i}{1 - H_{ii}}\right)^2$$

⚠️ Scope of the Shortcut

The shortcut applies to linear smoothers only — OLS, ridge regression, kernel smoothers, splines, and any model where \(\hat{\mathbf{y}} = \mathbf{H}\mathbf{y}\) for some fixed \(\mathbf{H}\). It does NOT apply to decision trees, neural networks, or any non-linear model.

Bias-Variance of LOOCV

Bias: Each fold trains on \(n-1\) points — nearly the full dataset. The model size barely changes between fold and full fit, so the performance estimate is nearly unbiased.

Variance: The \(n\) test errors are computed on individual points (size-1 sets). Each error is noisy by itself, and the \(n\) trained models are nearly identical (they share \(n-2\) training points), making their errors highly correlated. Averaging correlated quantities gives a high-variance estimator.

🚨 High Variance is Real

Arlot & Celisse (2010) and others show LOOCV can have higher variance than 5- or 10-fold CV in practice — the near-identical training sets make each fold's error correlated, amplifying variance without reducing bias enough to compensate. Prefer 5- or 10-fold CV for most tasks.

3
Mathematical Derivation — The Shortcut Proof

Why does the hat-matrix shortcut work? Here is the derivation via the Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury identity.

Setup

Let \(\mathbf{X}_{-i}\) be \(\mathbf{X}\) with row \(i\) removed, \(\mathbf{y}_{-i}\) be \(\mathbf{y}\) with element \(i\) removed. The LOO OLS estimate is:

$$\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{-i} = (\mathbf{X}_{-i}^\top \mathbf{X}_{-i})^{-1}\mathbf{X}_{-i}^\top \mathbf{y}_{-i}$$

Note that \(\mathbf{X}_{-i}^\top \mathbf{X}_{-i} = \mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X} - \mathbf{x}_i\mathbf{x}_i^\top\) (rank-1 downdate).

Sherman-Morrison Applied

For a rank-1 update \((\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}^\top)^{-1} = \mathbf{A}^{-1} + \frac{\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}^\top\mathbf{A}^{-1}}{1 - \mathbf{v}^\top\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{u}}\):

$$(\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X} - \mathbf{x}_i\mathbf{x}_i^\top)^{-1} = (\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X})^{-1} + \frac{(\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{x}_i\mathbf{x}_i^\top(\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X})^{-1}}{1 - H_{ii}}$$

LOO Prediction

After algebra (substituting the rank-1 inverse and simplifying \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{-i}\)):

$$\hat{y}_{-i} = \mathbf{x}_i^\top\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{-i} = \hat{y}_i - H_{ii}\cdot\frac{y_i - \hat{y}_i}{1 - H_{ii}}$$

Rearranging:

$$y_i - \hat{y}_{-i} = \frac{y_i - \hat{y}_i}{1-H_{ii}}$$

where \(H_{ii} = \mathbf{x}_i^\top(\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{x}_i\) is the \(i\)-th diagonal of the hat matrix. This holds for any linear smoother because \(\hat{\mathbf{y}} = \mathbf{H}\mathbf{y}\) with \(\mathbf{H}\) not depending on \(\mathbf{y}\).

📐 Generalized Cross-Validation (GCV)

GCV replaces \(H_{ii}\) by its average \(\bar{H} = \text{tr}(\mathbf{H})/n = p/n\), giving a simpler approximation:

$$\text{GCV} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{y_i-\hat{y}_i}{1 - p/n}\right)^2 = \frac{\text{RSS}/n}{(1-p/n)^2}$$

GCV requires only the hat matrix trace (the model degrees of freedom \(p\)), not the individual \(H_{ii}\) values. It is slightly biased but even cheaper to compute.

LOOCV vs. K-Fold vs. Other Strategies
Strategy K (folds) Bias Variance Cost When to Use
LOOCV n Lowest Highest n fits (or 1 with shortcut for linear) Small n; linear models; when bias is critical
10-fold CV 10 Low Low 10 fits Default choice for most tasks
5-fold CV 5 Moderate Lower 5 fits Faster than 10-fold; similar variance
Hold-out 1 High Moderate 1 fit Large n only; quick iterations
Repeated K-fold K×R Low Very low K×R fits When stability of estimate matters most
GCV n (approx.) Near-LOOCV Slightly lower 1 fit (trace only) Linear models — even faster than LOOCV shortcut
🎯 Decision Guide

Use LOOCV when: \(n < 50\) and you need a nearly unbiased estimate, OR you have a linear model and can use the hat-matrix shortcut.
Use 10-fold as the default for everything else.
Use GCV when you have a linear smoother and want the fastest approximate LOOCV.

6
Key Takeaways
  • LOOCV = K-fold with K = n; trains n models on n−1 points each
  • Nearly unbiased (each fold uses almost the full data) but high-variance (errors are highly correlated across folds)
  • Naïve cost is O(n) model fits; for linear smoothers the hat-matrix shortcut reduces this to 1 fit
  • The shortcut formula \((y_i - \hat{y}_i)/(1-H_{ii})\) follows from the Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury rank-1 downdate
  • GCV approximates LOOCV by replacing \(H_{ii}\) with \(\text{tr}(\mathbf{H})/n = p/n\), requiring only degrees of freedom
  • In practice, 5- or 10-fold CV is preferred due to lower variance and comparable bias
