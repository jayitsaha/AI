Divergence, Curl & Laplacian

How vector fields expand, rotate, and smooth — and why the Laplacian rules diffusion and AI
Intermediate Vector Calculus Math Foundations O(n) per grid cell
1
Understanding the Operators
▼

What is a vector field? Imagine wind on a weather map: at every point in space there is a vector (direction + speed). A vector field \(\mathbf{F}(x,y) = (P(x,y),\, Q(x,y))\) assigns such a vector to each point. Three differential operators tell us how that field behaves locally.

Divergence — net outflow

The divergence \(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F}\) measures how much the field is a source or sink at each point. Positive divergence = field lines spread outward (a source, like air from a balloon). Negative = lines converge inward (a sink, like a drain). Zero divergence = incompressible (like an ideal fluid).

$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} = \frac{\partial P}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial Q}{\partial y} \quad \text{(2-D)}$$

Curl — local rotation

The curl \(\nabla \times \mathbf{F}\) measures how much the field rotates locally. In 2-D it is a scalar: positive curl = counter-clockwise swirl; negative = clockwise. Think of a tiny paddle wheel placed in the field — does it spin?

$$(\nabla \times \mathbf{F})_z = \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} \quad \text{(2-D scalar curl)}$$

Laplacian — smoothness and diffusion

The Laplacian \(\nabla^2 f = \Delta f\) of a scalar function is the divergence of the gradient. It tells you whether a point is higher or lower than its neighborhood average — it drives diffusion, the heat equation, and the graph Laplacian in graph neural networks.

$$\nabla^2 f = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y^2}$$
If you remember one thing

Divergence = net outflow (sources/sinks). Curl = local rotation. Laplacian = deviation from local average → drives smoothing/diffusion. All three are assembled from the nabla operator \(\nabla = (\partial_x, \partial_y, \partial_z)\): div = \(\nabla\cdot\), curl = \(\nabla\times\), Laplacian = \(\nabla\cdot\nabla = \nabla^2\).

How Each Operator Works Internally
▼

Divergence via finite differences

Discretize on a grid with spacing \(h\). At grid point \((i,j)\), estimate how much the \(x\)-component grows in \(x\) and the \(y\)-component grows in \(y\):

$$(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F})_{i,j} \approx \frac{P_{i+1,j} - P_{i-1,j}}{2h} + \frac{Q_{i,j+1} - Q_{i,j-1}}{2h}$$

This is what convolutional filters in CNNs compute implicitly when they detect edges — gradient magnitude is the norm of the gradient, which is the "input" to divergence.

Curl as antisymmetric partial derivative

In 2-D, curl measures asymmetry in how the field varies across directions: if \(\partial Q/\partial x > \partial P/\partial y\), vectors rotate CCW. A conservative (gradient) field has zero curl everywhere — this is the test for path-independence of line integrals.

$$(\nabla \times \mathbf{F})_z = \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y}$$

Laplacian — five-point stencil

The discrete Laplacian at \((i,j)\) uses the four neighbors and the center value:

$$(\nabla^2 f)_{i,j} \approx \frac{f_{i+1,j} + f_{i-1,j} + f_{i,j+1} + f_{i,j-1} - 4f_{i,j}}{h^2}$$

This is exactly the graph Laplacian \(L = D - A\) on a grid graph — degree matrix minus adjacency matrix. Where \(\nabla^2 f < 0\), the value exceeds its average (a local peak that will diffuse outward). Where \(\nabla^2 f > 0\), the value is below its average (a valley that will fill in).

Key connection: Heat equation

The heat equation \(\partial_t f = \kappa \nabla^2 f\) says: the rate of change of temperature equals the Laplacian of temperature. Hot peaks spread out, cold valleys fill. In diffusion models (DDPM), the forward process adds Gaussian noise — this is exactly heat-equation diffusion in score-function space. Graph diffusion \(\dot{x} = -Lx\) uses the graph Laplacian the same way.

3
Why It Works — Integral Theorems
▼

Divergence Theorem (Gauss's Theorem)

The total divergence integrated over a volume equals the flux through the bounding surface:

$$\iiint_V (\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F})\, dV = \oiint_{\partial V} \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S}$$

Intuition: if a region is a net source, more field must leave through its boundary than enters. This is the foundation of Maxwell's equations (\(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E} = \rho/\varepsilon_0\)).

Stokes' Theorem

The integral of curl over a surface equals the line integral around its boundary:

$$\iint_S (\nabla \times \mathbf{F}) \cdot d\mathbf{S} = \oint_{\partial S} \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}$$

Intuition: local rotations inside a region, when summed, give the net circulation around the boundary. This generalises Green's theorem (2-D) and the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.

Spectral Laplacian

The Laplacian \(\nabla^2\) is self-adjoint with eigenfunctions \(e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\) on \(\mathbb{R}^n\):

$$\nabla^2 e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}} = -\|\mathbf{k}\|^2 e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}}$$

The eigenvalue \(-\|\mathbf{k}\|^2\) is always non-positive — Laplacian is negative semi-definite. High-frequency components (large \(\|\mathbf{k}\|\)) decay fastest under \(e^{\kappa\nabla^2 t}\), which is why Gaussian blur = heat diffusion in the Fourier basis.

Common misconception

Curl-free \(\neq\) zero divergence and vice versa. A conservative field has zero curl (gradient of a potential). A solenoidal field has zero divergence (like a magnetic field \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}=0\)). Helmholtz decomposition says ANY smooth vector field splits uniquely into curl-free + divergence-free parts.

Helmholtz Decomposition

$$\mathbf{F} = -\nabla\phi + \nabla\times\mathbf{A}$$

where \(\phi\) is the scalar potential (curl-free part) and \(\mathbf{A}\) is the vector potential (divergence-free part). This split matters for normalizing flows and neural ODEs.

Key Takeaways & ML Connections
▼

Where these operators appear in AI/ML

  • Graph Laplacian (GNNs): \(L = D - A\) is the discrete Laplacian on graphs. Spectral GNNs (ChebNet, GCN) use its eigendecomposition. Smooth signals have small \(x^\top L x\).
  • Diffusion Models (DDPM): The forward noising process is Gaussian diffusion — a solution to the heat equation in score space. The score function \(\nabla_x \log p(x)\) is literally the gradient of the log-density.
  • Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs): The Laplacian appears in the PDE loss: \(\mathcal{L}_\text{PDE} = \|\nabla^2 u_\theta - f\|^2\).
  • Image processing: Laplacian of Gaussian (LoG) = blob detector; divergence of gradient = edge sharpness.
Hierarchy of operators

Gradient \(\nabla f\) → direction of steepest ascent. Divergence \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{F}\) → net outflow of a vector field. Curl \(\nabla\times\mathbf{F}\) → local rotation. Laplacian \(\nabla^2 f = \nabla\cdot\nabla f\) → smoothness deficit. All live under the same nabla umbrella.

NumPy Code Trace
▼
import numpy as np
h = 0.1 # grid spacing
x = np.linspace(-2, 2, 41) # 41 points
y = np.linspace(-2, 2, 41)
X, Y = np.meshgrid(x, y)
# Example: F(x,y) = (x, y) — pure radial field
P = X # x-component
Q = Y # y-component
# Divergence = dP/dx + dQ/dy
dP_dx = np.gradient(P, h, axis=1) # central difference
dQ_dy = np.gradient(Q, h, axis=0)
div_F = dP_dx + dQ_dy # = 2 everywhere for F=(x,y)
# Curl (2-D scalar) = dQ/dx - dP/dy
dQ_dx = np.gradient(Q, h, axis=1)
dP_dy = np.gradient(P, h, axis=0)
curl_F = dQ_dx - dP_dy # = 0 everywhere (irrotational)
# Laplacian of scalar f = x^2 - y^2 (saddle)
f = X**2 - Y**2
d2f_dx2 = np.gradient(np.gradient(f, h, axis=1), h, axis=1)
d2f_dy2 = np.gradient(np.gradient(f, h, axis=0), h, axis=0)
lap_f = d2f_dx2 + d2f_dy2 # = 2 + (-2) = 0 (harmonic!)
# Heat equation step (explicit Euler, dt<h²/4 for stability)
dt = 0.001; kappa = 1.0
u = np.exp(-(X**2 + Y**2)) # initial hot spot
for _ in range(100):
lap_u = (np.roll(u,-1,1)+np.roll(u,1,1)+np.roll(u,-1,0)+np.roll(u,1,0)-4*u)/h**2
u = u + dt * kappa * lap_u
# u has spread — the Gaussian has widened by sqrt(2*kappa*t)
